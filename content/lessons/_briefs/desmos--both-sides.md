# desmos--both-sides

**Shows:** to solve an equation in x, graph each side as its own y; the x-value of each crossing is a solution, and zooming out can reveal a hidden crossing.

**Storyboard:**
1. The equation \(x^2 - 4 = 3x\) appears. An arrow splits it into two rows: "left side: \(y = x^2 - 4\)" (blue) and "right side: \(y = 3x\)" (orange).
2. A graph window opens with a tight view: the blue parabola and the orange line are drawn, and one crossing dot at \((4, 12)\) is on screen. The other crossing is off-screen (hinted by the orange line leaving the edge).
3. The window zooms out smoothly. A second dot at \((-1, -3)\) appears.
4. Each dot drops a dashed vertical line to the x-axis, ending on "x = -1" and "x = 4". The y-values (-3 and 12) are greyed out with "not the answer".
5. A last strip: "Equivalent? The graph that sits exactly on top." Two parabolas appear, one slides on top of the other and the pair turns green.

**Labels & formulas:** \(x^2 - 4 = 3x\), \(y = x^2 - 4\), \(y = 3x\), "solution: x = -1", "solution: x = 4", "zoom out before you count", "on top = equivalent".

**Stress:** the dashed drop-lines from the crossings to the x-axis (accent colour) so the eye lands on x, not y. The zoom-out moment must be slow enough to see the second crossing arrive. Dots at least 8 units wide for a phone.
