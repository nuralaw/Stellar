Research notes for the Reading and Writing domain Information and Ideas (central ideas, textual evidence, quantitative evidence, inferences), November 2026. Official material came first: the College Board Assessment Framework, the Skills Insight document and two official practice-test answer-explanation PDFs, all read in full text. Prep sites were read directly. Three limits shaped the sweep. Reddit could not be reached at all (reddit.com and old.reddit.com refused every fetch, the .json route was blocked, and the search tool excludes reddit.com), so Reddit advice appears here only where a prep site reported it second-hand. YouTube transcripts could not be loaded, so every video below is described only from its title, channel, view count, description and chapter list, and one AI summary page. The shared web-search budget ran out partway through (a few searches became possible again later), so many later sources were reached by following links from pages already found. Khan Academy's course pages could not be read either: they sit behind a JavaScript check, and its blog did not resolve.

## Central Ideas and Details

### Best sources

- [Assessment Framework for the Digital SAT Suite (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/assessment-framework-for-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — the official definition (find the central idea and the key details that support it), 25–150 word texts, 12–14 Information and Ideas questions per test, the order of domains in each module (Craft and Structure, then Information and Ideas), questions ordered by skill and then difficulty, and the note that every wrong answer is built from a common student error.
- [Skills Insight for the SAT Suite (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/skills-insight-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — shows the difficulty ladder: main idea of middle-school texts at the low bands, early-college texts at the top; details that are stated versus only implied.
- [SAT Practice Test 10 answer explanations (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/sat-practice-test-10-answers-digital.pdf) — official wording for rejecting choices; a feelings question where "excited" plus "scared" rules out "overjoyed" (strength matters); a main idea that needs both halves of the text.
- [SAT Practice Test 9 answer explanations (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/sat-practice-test-9-answers-digital.pdf) — main idea distractors built from background details, contradictions and unsupported influence; a literary detail question where a distractor reverses who wants what.
- [Mastering Central Ideas and Details (Test Ninjas)](https://www.test-ninjas.com/sat-central-ideas-and-details) — the usual shape of a short text (setup, illustration, pivot) and the "wrong focus" trap: true and on topic but answering a different question.
- [SAT Main Idea Strategy (PrepMaven)](https://prepmaven.com/blog/test-prep/sat-main-idea-strategy/) — writing a short headline before the choices; the "also true" trap; reading the whole text even under time pressure.
- [Central Ideas and Details lesson (Varsity Tutors)](https://www.varsitytutors.com/practice/subjects/sat-reading-and-writing/lessons/central-ideas-and-details) — stated versus implied central ideas, the "text it to a friend" test, and a narrow-to-broad scope line.
- [Tips for SAT Literary Fiction Passages (UWorld)](https://collegeprep.uworld.com/blog/tips-tricks-to-tackle-literary-fiction-passages-on-sat/) — the best literature-specific advice found: read the line above the text, track what each character wants, translate figurative language, break long old-style sentences into parts, and watch the trap that keeps only the first emotion of a scene that changes.
- [A Tutor's Guide to Main Idea and Detail Questions (The Test Advantage)](https://thetestadvantage.com/blog/master-digital-sat-reading-a-tutors-guide-to-main-idea-detail-questions) — "topic + core message" and the incomplete-answer trap (one of two linked ideas missing).
- [The Reddit/YouTube Digital SAT Reading Trick, Explained (The Test Advantage)](https://thetestadvantage.com/blog/the-reddit-youtube-digital-sat-reading-trick-explained-by-tta-pros) — the only second-hand report of Reddit advice found: read the last sentence first. Useful for orientation on main idea questions, much less so for evidence questions.
- [SAT Reading and Writing Tips (Princeton Review)](https://www.princetonreview.com/sat-study/sat-reading-writing-tips) — predict, then prove each choice with a phrase from the text; the echo-wording trap.
- [How to Find the Main Ideas (Magoosh)](https://magoosh.com/sat/how-to-find-the-main-ideas-in-sat-reading-passages/) — a clear list of wrong-answer types (too broad, too narrow, extreme, assumption, half right, one word off).
- [Khan Academy Central Ideas and Details Questions, Advanced (3-4-5 Tutoring, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E8uWjDlKOgw) — a 35-minute walkthrough of 11 hard questions from Khan Academy's official course (known from its description only).
- [Solving Every New SAT Reading Question (SAT 1600 with Farrock, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0SdSkmzmW1M) and its [AI summary](https://youtubesummary.com/summary/0SdSkmzmW1M) — an October 2026 walkthrough; the summary says it restates each study in plain words (question, method, result) and rejects choices that add conditions the text doesn't have.
- [The Last SAT Reading Guide You'll Ever Need (Tutored By Design, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D-Wo9mG1qUQ) — a January 2026 overview of the whole Information and Ideas domain (description only).

### What the best teachers agree on

- Read the question first, so you know whether to read for the whole text or for one detail.
- Predict the answer in your own words before reading the choices. Almost every source gives a version of "topic + what the author says about it".
- The main idea must cover the whole text. Pivot words (but, however, yet) mark the author's real point, and in "people thought X, but Y" texts the answer is about Y.
- The same wrong-answer families appear everywhere: too narrow, too broad, half right, unsupported, twisted wording, contradiction, extreme wording.
- For a detail question you should be able to point to the line. The right answer is a paraphrase, not a copy of the text's words.
- Literature questions reward close attention to feelings and how strong they are, to shifts within a scene, and to translating images into plain meaning.

### Disagreements and choices made

- **Question first or text first?** Magoosh says either works; most sources say question first. Digital SAT texts are short and the question decides how to read, so the lesson teaches question first.
- **"Read the last sentence first" (the Reddit/YouTube trick, also Magoosh's first/last sentence tip).** Each text is one short paragraph, so skipping is risky and saves little time. The lesson teaches one full read and then uses the first and last sentences as a check on the prediction.
- **How many questions?** Prep sites range from 2–4 to 8–10 per test. The College Board gives only the domain total (12–14), and the order seen in official tests suggests about 3 or 4. The lesson says "usually about 3 or 4" and explains where the number comes from.
- **"When unsure, pick the least assertive answer" (Magoosh).** It works often, but official keys are sometimes firm. The lesson teaches matching the text's strength instead, with a table of hedge words.
- **Poetry.** No source had a solid poetry method (Test Ninjas' poetry page is only an ad for a paid guide). The lesson's poetry rules (read in sentences, follow the punctuation, the speaker is not the poet, look for the shift) come from general close-reading practice and from how official answer explanations treat poems.

## Command of Evidence: Textual

### Best sources

- [Assessment Framework for the Digital SAT Suite (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/assessment-framework-for-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — the key official facts: findings are hypothetical ("if true"), so students never judge whether they really happened; the right evidence connects to the claim through reasoning and never just restates it; literary quotations can be judged without knowing the work; some questions ask for a technique such as repetition; personal stories carry little weight in science; a character's words are not the author's view.
- [Skills Insight for the SAT Suite (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/skills-insight-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — what makes the hard ones hard: the link between quotation and claim is indirect or figurative, and claims and evidence become complex or subtle.
- [SAT Practice Test 10 answer explanations (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/sat-practice-test-10-answers-digital.pdf) and [SAT Practice Test 9 answer explanations (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/sat-practice-test-9-answers-digital.pdf) — official patterns: distractors with "no bearing on the claim", distractors that would weaken when the job is to support, a weakener showing more of the supposed cause going with a worse result, and support for one side of a scholarly debate.
- [SAT Command of Evidence: Textual (Test Ninjas)](https://test-ninjas.com/sat-command-of-evidence-textual) — the most complete method found: isolate the claim's scope, note the direction, predict the shape of the proof, split compound claims, watch qualifiers, and ask whether a skeptic would change their mind.
- [The Hardest Digital SAT Reading Questions (PrepMaven)](https://prepmaven.com/blog/test-prep/hardest-sat-reading-questions/) — treat the text as an argument; separate the claim from the evidence; traps that ignore a qualifier, switch to a different group, or describe a result when the question asks for a cause.
- [SAT Reading and Writing Tips (Princeton Review)](https://www.princetonreview.com/sat-study/sat-reading-writing-tips) — test each choice by asking whether it would make the claim clearly true; cut choices that mention the topic without proving the point.
- [Weighing in on the digital SAT (The Critical Reader)](https://thecriticalreader.com/weighing-in-on-the-digital-sat-6-key-points/) — Erica Meltzer's view that support and undermine questions are logic questions, like those on the LSAT and GMAT, and that the literary claims are usually simple descriptions of the text.
- [Overview of Digital SAT Reading and Writing question types (3-4-5 Tutoring)](https://345tutoring.wordpress.com/2023/06/25/an-overview-of-digital-sat-reading-writing-question-types/) — names the strengthen and weaken formats that keep coming back: competing theories, timelines, building on earlier evidence, and focusing on the variables in a science claim.
- [1600.io strategy lectures](https://1600.io/p/strategy) — the lecture list confirms how one major course splits the type (a "which quotation" strategy, a "most directly support" strategy and a separate "most directly undermine" strategy); the videos themselves are paywalled and were not watched.
- [Command of Evidence Questions on the Digital SAT (Ivy League Mentors, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=POcYofMngBw) — about 45,000 views; the description promises a four-step routine applied to questions of rising difficulty (steps not verified without a transcript).
- [Which Quotation Most Effectively Illustrates the Claim? (Test Innovators, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZOgAvZCMRZ4) — a video just on literary quotation questions; the description notes they are not the most common type but among the hardest, because of difficult literature and poetry.
- [The 3 Main Ways to Strengthen an Argument (3-4-5 Tutoring, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EZsy-CdsIR4) and [Strengthen/Weaken: Competing Theories (3-4-5 Tutoring, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KC0k1W1asCQ) — short lessons on the support and weaken sub-types, including two rival hypotheses (descriptions only).

### What the best teachers agree on

- Find the exact claim first and keep its scope: who, what relationship, what limits.
- Decide the job (support, weaken or illustrate) before reading the choices. Picking the opposite job is the classic mistake.
- Predict what the evidence should look like if the claim is true (or false).
- Accept every finding as true; judge only whether it matters.
- Most wrong choices are on topic but irrelevant to the claim, or cover only one part of a two-part claim.
- For quotations: split the claim into parts, translate each quotation, and keep the one that shows every part about the right person.

### Disagreements and choices made

- **What to call the type.** Many tutors borrow "strengthen/weaken" from the LSAT. The lesson uses the College Board's own words (support, weaken, illustrate) so students recognize the question stems.
- **How simple are the literary claims?** Meltzer calls them simple descriptions; the College Board's Skills Insight says the hardest ones link claim and quotation indirectly or through figurative language. Both are true at different difficulty levels, so the lesson has one direct example and one indirect example (care hidden behind harsh words).
- **"Would it make the claim obviously true?" (Princeton Review) or "would it change a skeptic's mind?" (Test Ninjas).** A finding rarely makes a claim obviously true, so the lesson uses the softer doubter test.
- **Is a choice that repeats the background ever right?** The framework says the right evidence never just restates the claim. The lesson turns this into a rule: the right finding adds new information.
- **Rival hypotheses.** Few prep sites cover "support A over B" questions, but 3-4-5 Tutoring and the official explanations show they appear. The lesson's hardest example uses this format and teaches predicting what each hypothesis expects.

## Command of Evidence: Quantitative

### Best sources

- [Assessment Framework for the Digital SAT Suite (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/assessment-framework-for-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — graphics are limited to tables, bar graphs and line graphs; no calculations are needed and no calculator is allowed in this section; difficulty runs from finding one data point, to spotting patterns, to combining data with the text.
- [Skills Insight for the SAT Suite (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/skills-insight-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — the ladder from "locate simple, clearly signaled data" to "interpret and combine data to support or challenge a claim".
- [SAT Practice Test 10 answer explanations (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/sat-practice-test-10-answers-digital.pdf) — official rejections for data that is accurate but does not relate to the conclusion, for wrong values, for comparing inside one group when the claim compares two, and for picking years that hide the difference the claim describes.
- [SAT Practice Test 9 answer explanations (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/sat-practice-test-9-answers-digital.pdf) — a choice that must meet two conditions at once, a two-part hypothesis where covering one part fails, and a graph question that also needs reasoning from the text.
- [SAT Command of Evidence: Quantitative (Test Ninjas)](https://test-ninjas.com/sat-command-of-evidence-quantitative) — the clearest written method (claim, then locate, then compare), a table of claim words (steadily, peaked, gap widened) and the trap list: true but irrelevant, half the claim, wrong group, count versus percent, overreach, opposite job.
- [Overview of Digital SAT Reading and Writing question types (3-4-5 Tutoring)](https://345tutoring.wordpress.com/2023/06/25/an-overview-of-digital-sat-reading-writing-question-types/) — the "relevance first, accuracy second" shortcut used in the lesson's two-pass method.
- [Weighing in on the digital SAT (The Critical Reader)](https://thecriticalreader.com/weighing-in-on-the-digital-sat-6-key-points/) — Meltzer found she could answer almost every released graph question from the wording of the text and question, so these are reading questions in disguise.
- [3 Hardest Digital SAT English Question Types (Strategic Test Prep, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6crBdJjyD9Q) — about 29,000 views; its chapter list gives a graph routine: read the question, skip most of the paragraph, find the claim or hypothesis, eliminate.
- [Digital SAT: Command of Quantitative Evidence, Graphs (SAT Mastermind, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J7Ka3-JwGxY) — about 16,000 views (July 2025); its chapters split the type into plain data-reading questions and claim questions and flag a "common mistake that wastes everyone's time" (content not verified).
- [5 Quantitative Supporting Claims Questions in 5 Minutes (SCORE, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ERdTp8p4EMA) — the description sums up the approach: treat these like ordinary support-the-claim questions and use the chart only to check.
- [Tables on Digital SAT Reading and Writing (TestPrepSHSAT, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OPeO5jurdwg) — the description notes that tables and bar charts hold the same kind of information, and that often one or more columns are not needed at all.
- [Command of Evidence: Quantitative (NUM8ERS)](https://num8ers.com/command-of-evidence-quantitative/) — a two-sentence proof test ("the graphic shows…" / "this supports the claim because…"), the trap of picking the highest final value when the claim is about the biggest change, and a suggestion to log every miss by cause (label, unit, scale, comparison).
- [SAT reading questions with charts are easy, actually (SAT Prep Askerra, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nWmRA5_PnK0) — a May 2026 video (about 7,600 views) that argues the opposite order: its five chapters are read the chart, read the passage, look for missing information, match text to chart, pick the matching answer.
- [Infographics in 4 1/2 Steps (Ivy League Mentors, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CaqO88lutZY) — about 10,000 views; the description says students find these questions hard mostly because they approach them the wrong way (the steps themselves were not verified without a transcript).
- [Graph/Chart Passages: SAT Reading Lesson (Settele Tutoring, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QaMozY1lDVk) — an August 2025 lesson (about 6,000 views) on hard graph passages; the description says it doubles as practice for the channel's "Dumb Summaries". The name suggests the same idea as the lessons' "translate into plain words" step, but the method itself was not verified.

### What the best teachers agree on

- Start from the claim, not the graphic. Know what you are looking for before you read numbers.
- The right answer must be both accurate and relevant. "True but irrelevant" is the most common trap.
- Break the claim into groups, measure, time and relationship, and check that the choice matches each one.
- Read titles, labels, units and legends before trusting any number.
- No math is needed beyond comparing values.

### Disagreements and choices made

- **Graphic first or claim first?** Some tutors start with the graphic: the SAT Prep Askerra video reads the chart before the passage, and NUM8ERS orients to the graphic's labels first. Most, including Meltzer, Test Ninjas, 3-4-5 Tutoring and Strategic Test Prep, start from the claim. The lesson teaches claim first, because the claim tells you which numbers matter, but makes reading the title, labels and units the first thing you do once you turn to the graphic. Both camps agree that labels and units must be read before any number is trusted.
- **"Skip most of the paragraph" (Strategic Test Prep).** It saves time on simple questions, but the official explanations include hard questions where the text's reasoning matters (combining a graph with an explanation in the text). The lesson reads the claim first but tells students to read the whole short text when the claim depends on it, as in its hardest example.
- **Relevance or accuracy first?** 3-4-5 Tutoring says relevance first; others check numbers first. The lesson uses two passes, relevance then accuracy, because the relevance pass usually removes two choices without reading the graphic.
- **Weaken questions.** They are rare. The lesson includes one, built around the count-versus-share trap that official explanations and Test Ninjas both stress.

## Inferences

### Best sources

- [Assessment Framework for the Digital SAT Suite (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/assessment-framework-for-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — the clearest description anywhere of how these questions are built: a tight argument cut off just before its conclusion, written in plain academic prose (not literary), needing only general high-school knowledge, with a key that is a strong, real-world conclusion students can pick by matching rather than by elimination alone.
- [Skills Insight for the SAT Suite (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/skills-insight-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — inferences move from simple ones on middle-school texts to highly complex ones on early-college texts.
- [SAT Practice Test 9 answer explanations (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/sat-practice-test-9-answers-digital.pdf) and [SAT Practice Test 10 answer explanations (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/sat-practice-test-10-answers-digital.pdf) — official reasoning: a counterexample showing a trait shouldn't be assumed to serve one purpose, distractors that rely on information the text never gives, and conclusions that follow from "exclusively targeting" logic.
- [SAT Inferences (Test Ninjas)](https://test-ninjas.com/sat-inferences) — premises, the smallest bridge, a source check, and three named failure types (too strong, too new, too broad), plus a list of common passage structures.
- [Inference Questions on the SAT (UWorld)](https://collegeprep.uworld.com/blog/inference-questions-on-sat-reading) — "most reasonable beats most creative" and the true-in-real-life trap.
- [Inference and Main Idea Strategies (SATSphere)](https://www.satsphere.com/blog/digital-sat-reading-inference-and-main-idea-strategies) — the one-step bridge, "no line, no claim", checking each adjective against the text, and the point that science writers hedge, so choices that drop the hedge are suspect.
- [Inference Questions: 6 Strategies (PrepScholar)](https://blog.prepscholar.com/inference-questions-on-sat-reading-6-strategies) — the digital test uses only two wordings for inferences; answer in your own words, then plug the choice back in.
- [Inferences lesson (Varsity Tutors)](https://www.varsitytutors.com/practice/subjects/sat-reading-and-writing/lessons/inferences) — reversed cause and effect, scope shifts from one study to everyone, and the note that harder modules use choices that are partly supported.
- [The Hardest Digital SAT Reading Questions (PrepMaven)](https://prepmaven.com/blog/test-prep/hardest-sat-reading-questions/) — list every relevant piece of evidence and choose the ending that is consistent with all of them.
- [Master Digital SAT Inference Questions (Edison OS)](https://edisonos.com/blog/sat-inferences-questions) — right answers tend to use moderate, hedged words; wrong answers often reverse cause and effect or lean on outside knowledge.
- [Mastering Inference Questions (Walker Prep)](https://walkerprep.substack.com/p/mastering-inference-questions-on) — only the free introduction was readable; it names three kinds of allowed inference (necessary inferences, inferences that bridge adjacent sentences, and the only reasonable inference) and argues for very literal reading.
- [SAT Inference Question Strategies (Strategic Test Prep, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PWgMcZLDpkE) — about 53,000 views (May 2025), the most-viewed inference video found; chapters cover leaving these questions for last, when to annotate, and four hard examples.
- [Completing the Text Tips to Score 1500+ (Strategic Test Prep, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=laRCkRy4_0g) and [3 Hardest Digital SAT English Question Types (Strategic Test Prep, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6crBdJjyD9Q) — chapter lists give the routine: paraphrase and annotate each sentence, note the structure, then eliminate.
- [Khan Academy "Logically Completing the Text" Questions, Advanced (3-4-5 Tutoring, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KrLXitqy0q8) — a 40-minute walkthrough of 13 hard questions from Khan Academy's official course (description only).
- [The 7 Hardest Reading Questions on the Digital SAT (Ivy League Mentors, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IhWiMWq3VhE) and [Hard Module 2 SAT Reading Passages Explained (Strategic Test Prep, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LdPnfDT0b_U) — popular walkthroughs (about 24,000 and 11,000 views) of the hardest official questions, many of them inference and evidence questions; useful for seeing how hard versions look (descriptions and chapter lists only).
- [How to Get an 800 on SAT Reading and Writing (UWorld)](https://collegeprep.uworld.com/blog/how-to-get-800-on-digital-sat-reading-and-writing/) — top scorers predict what the answer must do before reading the choices, refuse partly supported choices, and keep an error log that names the cause of each miss.

### What the best teachers agree on

- The answer must follow from the text. Outside knowledge and "could be true" ideas don't count.
- Predict a conclusion before reading the choices, then eliminate with named trap types.
- The three biggest traps are too strong, new information and too broad.
- The words just before the blank tell you what the ending must do.
- Match the hedging of the text: one study or a pattern supports "may" or "suggests", not "proves" or "always".

### Disagreements and choices made

- **Predict or eliminate?** Some tutors lead with process of elimination. The College Board says the key is strong enough to choose by matching. The lesson predicts first and uses elimination as the check.
- **"The correct answer is usually hedged" (Edison OS, Princeton Review).** That's often true, but some keys are firm when the logic is firm (a date that rules something out). The lesson teaches matching strength to the logic and includes a firm-answer example.
- **Save these for last (Strategic Test Prep).** They already come last in the Information and Ideas group, and the grammar questions after them are quicker. The lesson adds a "flag it and come back" reminder rather than telling everyone to skip ahead.
- **Annotate every sentence or read once?** Strategic Test Prep annotates each sentence; others say read once. The lesson asks students to give each sentence a job in their head (background, claim, finding, contrast), which is a light version of annotating.
- **Which patterns to teach.** Test Ninjas, 3-4-5 Tutoring and the official explanations point to the same handful: ruling out a cause, counterexamples, timelines, chains, a hidden third factor, puzzles and the limits of a study. The lesson teaches these as a table and builds its examples from five of them.
