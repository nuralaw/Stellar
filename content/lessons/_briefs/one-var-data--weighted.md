# one-var-data--weighted

**Shows:** the mean of two group means is not the mean of everyone: the bigger group pulls the combined mean closer to its own mean.

**Storyboard:**
1. A number line from 60 to 95. Class A (20 students) sits at 70 as a wide stack of 20 small blocks. Class B (10 students) sits at 85 as a narrower stack of 10 blocks. Labels: "A: 20 students, mean 70" and "B: 10 students, mean 85".
2. A tempting marker appears at 77.5 (the plain average of 70 and 85) with a red cross and the faded caption "treats both classes as equal".
3. A beam balances on a pivot. Class A's 20 blocks are on the left end, Class B's 10 blocks on the right end. The beam is level only when the pivot moves left of the middle.
4. The pivot settles at 75. Two brackets show the gaps: "5" from 70 to 75 and "10" from 75 to 85, with the caption "5 : 10 = 10 : 20".
5. The totals line appears under the beam: \(\frac{20 \times 70 + 10 \times 85}{30} = 75\). The 75 marker is green.

**Labels & formulas:** "20 students", "10 students", "plain average 77.5", \(\frac{20 \times 70 + 10 \times 85}{30} = 75\).

**Stress:** the balance beam is the hero (bigger stack = more weight, pivot nearer to it); the right answer 75 in green, the wrong 77.5 in red and faded. At most five labels visible at once so it reads on a phone.
