# linear-equations-two-var--parallel

**Shows:** parallel lines have the same slope and different intercepts, and in standard form their \(x\) and \(y\) coefficients are in the same ratio.

**Storyboard:**
1. Two lines on a grid, \(y = 2x + 1\) and \(y = 2x - 3\), each with an identical slope triangle (run 1, rise 2) drawn on it. The triangles are the same colour; a label says "same slope".
2. The lines are dragged apart/together: the gap changes but the tilt never does. Different crossing points \(+1\) and \(-3\) on the \(y\)-axis are marked.
3. A third line with a different tilt crosses them and is greyed out with "not parallel".
4. Standard form row: \(3x + 4y = 28\) and \(24x + ry = 24\). A multiplier badge "\(\times 8\)" turns the first into \(24x + 32y = 224\); \(r = 32\) pops up.

**Labels & formulas:** "same slope", "different \(b\)", \(m_1 = m_2\), \(3x + 4y = 28\), \(24x + ry = 24\), \(r = 32\).

**Stress:** the two matching slope triangles in the accent colour; the crossing line in neutral grey with a crossed-out "parallel". The multiplier badge is the focal point in the last frame.
