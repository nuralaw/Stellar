# systems-linear--substitute

**Shows:** one equation has a letter alone, so that expression is dropped into the other equation (in parentheses) and only one letter is left.

**Storyboard:**
1. The system appears: `\(y = 2x\)` on top, `\(3x + y = 10\)` below. The `y` in the top equation is highlighted (primary colour).
2. A small chip `\((2x)\)` lifts out of the top equation and flies into the bottom equation, landing where `y` was. The old `y` fades out.
3. The bottom line now reads `\(3x + (2x) = 10\)`, then collapses to `\(5x = 10\)` and `\(x = 2\)`.
4. The value `2` flies back into `\(y = 2x\)` and `\(y = 4\)` appears. Final frame: both lines crossing at the point `\((2, 4)\)` on a tiny graph on the right (two lines, dot, label).

**Labels & formulas:** `\(y = 2x\)`, `\(3x + y = 10\)`, `\(3x + (2x) = 10\)`, `\(5x = 10\)`, `\(x = 2\)`, `\(y = 4\)`, `\((2, 4)\)`. Word label on the flying chip: "swap in".

**Stress:** the flying chip and the new brackets in primary colour; `\(x = 2\)` and `\(y = 4\)` turn green at the end. Keep equations big (at least 16 units) so they read on a phone; the mini graph is secondary and grey.
