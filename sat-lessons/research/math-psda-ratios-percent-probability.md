Research notes for three Problem-Solving and Data Analysis skills: ratios, rates, proportions and units; percentages; probability and conditional probability. Research date: November 2026.

How the research was done, and its limits:

- **Official material was the backbone.** The Assessment Framework (version 4, September 2026) gives the exact testing points. Skills Insight gives the score band for each task. The SAT Suite Question Bank was read item by item (226 questions in these three skills) to list every sub-type, see which ones are marked hard, and collect College Board's own explanations of why each wrong choice is wrong.
- **Khan Academy** (the official Digital SAT course) shows a bot check to scripts, so its lesson pages could not be opened. The course units match College Board's skills ("Ratios, rates, and proportions", "Unit conversion", "Percentages", "Probability and relative frequency", each at foundations, medium and advanced level), as Scalar Learning's video walk-throughs of the course list. Search-result summaries said the ratio lesson solves word problems in three steps (write equivalent ratios, plug in the knowns with a variable, isolate the variable) and insists that units line up; the unit conversion lesson says units to be removed must appear once on top and once on the bottom; and the probability lesson calls missing-value questions very rare. All of this matches the lessons, but Khan is not credited as an opened source.
- **Reddit** (r/Sat, r/SATprep, r/DigitalSAT) blocks this crawler on every route (reddit.com, old.reddit.com and .json), so no Reddit threads were read. Nothing in the lessons is presented as Reddit advice.
- **YouTube transcripts** could not be downloaded (YouTube refuses scripted transcript requests and the transcript sites refused too). The video notes below come from titles, view counts, descriptions and chapter lists only.
- The shared web-search budget ran out part-way through, so later sources were opened directly by URL.

## Ratios, rates, proportions, and units

### Best sources

- [College Board: Assessment Framework for the Digital SAT Suite](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/assessment-framework-for-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — official testing points: proportional relationships, ratios and rates "in a wide variety of contexts" including scale drawings; derived units from products (kilowatt-hours) and quotients (population per square kilometer); one-step and multistep or multidimensional conversions; and the rule that when one quantity changes by a scale factor, a proportional quantity changes by the same factor. Also the domain share (15%, 5 to 7 of 44 questions) and appendix C, which explains that questions are generated from "parent models", so many test questions are near-copies of one pattern with new numbers.
- [College Board: Skills Insight for the Digital SAT Suite](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/skills-insight-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — unit rates and unit conversions sit at band 2 (370–410), ratios in equivalent forms at band 4, proportional relationships at band 5, and multistep problems with derived units at band 6 (610–670).
- [College Board: SAT Suite Question Bank](https://satsuitequestionbank.collegeboard.org/) — 93 ratio items (42 easy, 32 medium, 19 hard), the biggest skill in the domain. Easy: one-step conversions with the factor given, unit rates, rate × time, population density. Medium: two-step conversions (furlongs to yards to feet), density, pressure × area, keeping a ratio when one part changes. Hard: squared units (square miles to square yards), density of a cube, meters per second squared to miles per minute squared, and rates written with letters.
- [The Test Advantage: March 2026 SAT unit conversion question](https://thetestadvantage.com/blog/march-2026-sat-unit-conversion-question-answer-explanation) — a recent live-test question with squared time units; why the factor must be 3,600 and not 60, and why to round only at the end.
- [Test Ninjas: Ratios, Rates, and Proportions](https://test-ninjas.com/sat-ratio-rates-proportions) — name both quantities and their units before writing a proportion; part-to-part vs part-to-whole as the main trap.
- [PrepScholar: Fractions and Ratios on SAT Math](https://blog.prepscholar.com/complete-guide-to-fractions-and-ratios-sat-math) — the multiplier method (parts ax and bx, total (a + b)x) and using "the total must be a multiple of a + b" to eliminate choices.
- [Target Test Prep: Ratio and Proportion](https://sat.blog.targettestprep.com/ratio-and-proportion/) — cue phrases ("at this rate", "directly proportional"), y = kx in two steps, and the spring trap where the stretch, not the total length, is proportional.
- [Felder: When Units Go Square](https://felderbooks.com/papers/units2.html) — the 3-by-3 grid picture that shows why 1 square yard is 9 square feet.
- [Varsity Tutors: SAT Math Rates lesson](https://www.varsitytutors.com/practice/subjects/sat-math/lessons/rates) — unit rate as k in y = kx, conversion factors equal to 1, and a list of setup errors.
- [Axiom Academy: Unit Conversion and Dimensional Analysis](https://theaxiomacademy.org/learn/sat-unit-conversion-dimensional-analysis) — convert the top and bottom of a rate separately; a smaller unit means a bigger number.
- [Open Exam Prep: Ratios, Proportions, and Units](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sat-math/problem-solving-data-analysis/ratios-proportions-units) — inverse variation (worker-hours) and typing a multi-step fraction as one expression.
- [Magoosh: SAT Math Problem Solving and Data Analysis](https://magoosh.com/hs/sat/sat-math-problem-solving-and-data-analysis/) — average speed is total distance over total time; a map-scale area example that squares the factor.
- [Unique Prep: SAT Math 17, Ratios, Rates, Proportions and Units](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-1FpV_37NAc) — YouTube, 32k views, 77 minutes. Chapters: recognizing ratios, rates and proportions; scale models; translating "directly proportional"; graphing a direct proportion; conversion factors and cancellation; square and cubic units; "per" and "for each" statements; work questions. Built for the paper SAT; its description notes that Desmos is now allowed on every question.
- [Tutorllini Test Prep: Digital SAT Math Ottocento #2 Unit Conversions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pGrGcA36ENs) — YouTube, 35k views: a 28-minute lecture by an 800 scorer, then 10 worked questions.
- [James Lu SAT: All of Problem Solving and Data Analysis on the SAT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rytygT3oFO4) — YouTube, 132k views, February 2026. Its summary notes cubing the conversion number for volume (12³ for cubic feet to cubic inches); its chapter labels estimate unit conversions at about 2.4% of SAT Math questions.
- [John Jung: Proportion Word Problems, One Concept to Solve Them All](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_GqGYR8BII) — YouTube, 81k views; description only (one proportion concept for all word problems).
- [Settele Tutoring: Unit Conversion, the table method](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ffy1AXEVR1c) — YouTube, 7.7k views; description: a "table method" to sort the many units a question throws at you.
- [Tutorllini Test Prep: Ottocento #30 Ratio and Proportion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xr7bb_u7aWg) — YouTube, February 2026: a 21-minute lecture, then 12 worked questions.
- [Scalar Learning: SAT Math on Khan Academy, Ratios, Rates, and Proportions (Foundations)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HFticqOjvH4) and [Unit Conversion (Foundations)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K9fLV9mwYmY) — YouTube, about 2k and 1k views; four Khan practice questions each, and the full list of Khan's Digital SAT math units in the description.

### What the best teachers agree on

- Almost all errors are setup errors, so write a unit next to every number and make the unwanted units cancel (called dimensional analysis, the factor-label method or a "conversion train").
- For a ratio with a total, use one multiplier for every part; the most common ratio trap is reading a part-to-part ratio as part-to-whole.
- For "at this rate", find the unit rate first, then scale it.
- A rate needs two conversions, one for the top unit and one for the bottom unit.
- Square the length factor for area units and cube it for volume units; this is where most hard questions live.
- Check the size of the answer: converting to a smaller unit gives a bigger number.
- A calculator, including Desmos, does not catch setup errors.

### Disagreements and choices made

- **Proportion or unit rate first?** Test Ninjas and Target Test Prep lead with cross-multiplying a proportion; Varsity Tutors and Axiom lead with unit rates and conversion factors. The lesson leads with unit rates and conversion factors, because one idea then covers proportions, conversions and density, and it avoids the "flipped proportion" error. The proportion is shown as an equal alternative.
- **Work and inverse problems.** Test Ninjas and Unique Prep teach combined-work problems, but none of the 93 official items is one, so the lesson gives inverse proportion a single line.
- **How common part-to-whole is.** PrepScholar says part-to-whole ratios are rare, but official items with a total still require turning the ratio into part-to-whole, so the lesson trains it in Example 1.
- **Desmos.** Most text sources give no Desmos advice; The Test Advantage says Desmos helps with long chains. The lesson adds Desmos typing traps (after `/` or `^` the cursor stays in the denominator or exponent until you press the right arrow) based on how the Desmos editor works, and avoids input it could not confirm, such as typing a proportion with two fractions.
- **Old paper-SAT advice was dropped:** "no calculator" practice and the old grid-in limits.

## Percentages

### Best sources

- [College Board: Assessment Framework for the Digital SAT Suite](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/assessment-framework-for-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — testing points: discounts, interest, taxes and tips; increases and decreases "for many different quantities"; the link between percent change and growth factor (5% and 1.05); and percents of 100% or more. Its sample question (a percent of a percent) lists the three classic wrong moves: subtracting, adding, and dividing the two percents.
- [College Board: SAT Suite Question Bank](https://satsuitequestionbank.collegeboard.org/) — 84 percentage items, 34 of them hard, more hard items than any other skill in the domain. Hard patterns: "p% greater than" with p over 100, chains of "greater than" and "less than" statements, reading a multiplier expression ("kx is x decreased by what percent?"), working backward from a very large increase, the net change after a big rise and a smaller fall, a markup followed by a markdown, and very small percents (thousandths of a percent). College Board's wrong-answer notes repeat the same errors: "p% of" read as "p% greater than", the base reversed, the multiplier written as r or with a misplaced decimal point instead of 1 + r, and percents subtracted.
- [College Board: Skills Insight for the Digital SAT Suite](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/skills-insight-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — simple percent problems at band 1, multistep percent problems at band 6 (610–670).
- [Test Ninjas: Percentage Questions on the SAT](https://www.test-ninjas.com/sat-percentage) — six sub-types, the multiplier method, and traps like "150% of" vs "a 150% increase" and typing 1.02 when the question asks for 2.
- [The Test Advantage: Mastering Percent Change](https://thetestadvantage.com/blog/mastering-percent-change-digital-sat-math-practice) — a 1900% increase is a multiplier of 20, not 19; in "p% less than B", B is the base.
- [The Test Advantage: How Percentages and Ratios Are Tested](https://thetestadvantage.com/blog/how-are-percentages-and-ratios-tested-on-the-digital-sat) — in their banks more than half of the percent questions are hard, which matches the official bank (about 40%).
- [SAT Sphere: The Multiplier Method for Percent Change](https://www.satsphere.com/blog/percent-change-markups-and-discounts-digital-sat) — markup, discount and tax as one chain of factors; if x is 20% of y, then y is 500% of x.
- [Target Test Prep: SAT Percent Problems](https://sat.blog.targettestprep.com/sat-percent-problems/) — translating "is" and "of", undoing a discount by dividing, multiplying successive discounts, and rounding only at the end.
- [Magoosh: SAT Math Problem Solving and Data Analysis](https://magoosh.com/hs/sat/sat-math-problem-solving-and-data-analysis/) — assume 100 when no starting value is given.
- [Brilliant: SAT Ratios, Proportions, and Percents](https://brilliant.org/wiki/sat-ratios-proportions-percents/) — the shortcut a% of b = b% of a.
- [James Lu SAT: All of Percentages on the SAT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wThmYHwbOVo) — YouTube, 68k views, December 2025; chapters: converting between percents and decimals, then six worked problems. His longer PSDA video (132k views) summarizes the rule "multiply by 1 + r for an increase and 1 − r for a decrease".
- [Penguin SAT Prep: EVERY Percentage Rule on the SAT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LpB0DfVrduE) — YouTube, 36k views; chapters: percent of, percent change, adding percent, subtracting percent.
- [The SAT Gamified: All of SAT Percentages in 32 Minutes (2026)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hKk_20uhJ54) — YouTube, October 2026; chapters: tip and discount logic, a Desmos shortcut, moving decimals, writing an equation, percent change, two changes in a row, picking a number, percents of each other, mixtures.
- [John Jung: PERCENT Questions, Guaranteed to Show Up](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F7l_s1t4FD0) — YouTube, 126k views; description: the key skill is telling "20% of something" from "20% more or less than something".
- [Tutorllini Test Prep: Desmos Lesson #16 Percents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Ik_TbQ7ouE) and [Ottocento #1 Percents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E9sAOPfGKsM) — YouTube, 86k and 74k views; a Desmos-only lesson and a 53-minute lecture with 16 questions.
- [Settele Tutoring: Percents, use the OPEN formulas to avoid traps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8xL22fIoW40) — YouTube, 7.4k views; the description calls percentage questions "the single largest source of preventable mistakes". The formulas themselves are not in the description.
- [Scalar Learning: SAT Math on Khan Academy, Percentages (Foundations)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EuzcYMTIDjg) — YouTube, 1.3k views; four questions from Khan's foundations lesson.
- [bprp math basics: SAT percentage problem](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7MXtgpDcGdo) — YouTube, 13.5k views; works one official "increase by a very large percent" item, which shows how well known this pattern is.

### What the best teachers agree on

- Turn every percent change into a multiplier: × (1 + r) for an increase and × (1 − r) for a decrease.
- For several changes, multiply the multipliers; never add or subtract the percents.
- To find the original value, divide by the multiplier.
- The base is the original value, or the number after "of" or "than".
- "p% of" and "p% more than" are different, and the difference is biggest for percents of 100% or more.
- When no number is given, start with 100.
- Percent change and percentage-point change are different answers.

### Disagreements and choices made

- **Formula first or multiplier first?** Target Test Prep, Magoosh and The Organic Chemistry Tutor lead with percent change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100%. Test Ninjas, SAT Sphere, The Test Advantage and James Lu lead with multipliers. The lesson leads with multipliers, because the official hard items are written in multiplier form (0.35x, 3.49t, "k times") or as chains, and gives the formula as the tool for "what percent change" questions.
- **Settele's "OPEN" formulas** could not be checked (description only), so they were not used.
- **Mixtures and concentrations** (1600.io's chapter list, The SAT Gamified) did not appear among the official percentage items read, so the lesson leaves them out to stay focused.
- **Pick 100 or algebra?** Both are taught by good sources. The lesson uses algebra as the main route and "pick 100" as the check in Examples 4 to 6, because it is the fastest way to catch a wrong base.
- **Desmos.** Tutorllini has a full Desmos lesson for percents, but its content was not visible. The lesson keeps Desmos short: type the translated equation with x as the unknown, and avoid fractions in the input.

## Probability and conditional probability

### Best sources

- [College Board: Assessment Framework for the Digital SAT Suite](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/assessment-framework-for-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — testing points: one- and two-way tables, area models and other displays; relative frequency, probability and conditional probability; and, "infrequently", finding an unknown number from a probability, including using a probability to find how often an event happens.
- [College Board: Skills Insight for the Digital SAT Suite](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/skills-insight-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — probability from a frequency table, two-way table or description at band 2 (370–410); conditional probability, including from a two-way table, at band 6 (610–670).
- [College Board: SAT Suite Question Bank](https://satsuitequestionbank.collegeboard.org/) — 49 probability items (24 easy, 14 medium, 11 hard), about one per test. None involves two separate draws or independent events. Hard items are conditional probabilities whose condition covers several rows or columns (an "at least" condition, or "not in" one group) or that come as a story you must turn into counts first. Typed answers say "express your answer as a decimal or fraction, not as a percent". College Board's wrong-answer notes: dividing by the grand total, the reversed condition, using only part of a combined category, a percent where a count is asked, and the complement.
- [Test Ninjas: Probability Questions on the SAT](https://test-ninjas.com/sat-probability) — choosing the right total, the signal words, and mistakes such as reversing the condition or typing a percent that makes the answer bigger than 1.
- [PrepScholar: Complete Guide to Probability on SAT Math](https://blog.prepscholar.com/complete-guide-to-probability-on-sat-math-practice-questions) — desired outcomes over total outcomes and solving an incomplete table with a system; written for the old paper SAT (it describes 58 math questions).
- [Larry Learns: SAT Probability Questions](https://www.larrylearns.com/blog/sat-probability-questions) — four families of questions (complement, conditional, without replacement, "or") with the trap for each, and the rule "write the count before you divide".
- [LearnQ: Techniques for SAT Probability Questions](https://blogs.learnq.ai/?p=2353) — a four-step routine and P(A given B) = P(A and B) ÷ P(B). Its own two-way example has an answer-key error, a reminder to recompute any answer seen in a source.
- [SAT Math Guide: Data and Statistics](https://satmathguide.pages.dev/topics/data-statistics) — decide whether the question wants a row rate, a column rate or a total rate before computing.
- [Varsity Tutors: SAT Math Probability lesson](https://www.varsitytutors.com/practice/subjects/sat-math/lessons/probability) — P(A given B) and P(B given A) use different denominators; "or" needs the overlap subtracted; the usual mistakes in one list.
- [Just Locked In: Conditional Probability pattern](https://justlockedin-main.pages.dev/patterns/math/problem-solving-and-data-analysis/probability-and-conditional-probability/conditional-probability.html) — in story questions, multiply per-batch counts into totals before dividing; don't narrow the condition group too far.
- [Axiom Academy: SAT Probability and Conditional Probability](https://theaxiomacademy.org/learn/sat-probability-conditional-probability) — "given that" and "if" as signal words; two-way tables, tree diagrams and Venn diagrams as tools.
- [Kaplan SAT & ACT Prep: Two-way table statistics and probability questions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GM2jQ5KZ7ck) — YouTube, 4k views, 2019; description: three two-way table questions, "not the most common" on SAT Math but worth knowing.
- [MathPrep with Ahmad Bilal: Probability, Conditional Probability, AND/OR Conditions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QanwUGI49XE) — YouTube, 1.9k views, 22 minutes, Digital SAT; title only (no description), so used only as evidence that "and/or" table questions are taught as a group.
- [Settele Tutoring: Probability on the SAT, including hard conditional probability](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G39Ae3bgoDM) — YouTube, 12.9k views; description: probability is small but very predictable, "make a fraction", and in conditional questions the outcomes are limited.
- [John Jung: Probability, Simple Technique to Solve Them All](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9Mu9Miy-Kc8) — YouTube, 111k views; description: understand the context, then identify the target and the total.
- [Tutorllini Test Prep: Ottocento #20 Probability and Two-Way Tables](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=63q0bcwB1l4) — YouTube, 13k views: a 29-minute lecture, then 22 worked questions.
- [James Lu SAT: All of Problem Solving and Data Analysis on the SAT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rytygT3oFO4) — YouTube; summary: find the total first, and with "given that" the total is only the group mentioned. His chapter labels estimate probability at about 1.7% of SAT Math questions.

### What the best teachers agree on

- Every question is favorable ÷ total; the real skill is choosing the total (the denominator).
- "Given that", "of the", "among" and similar phrases shrink the group to one row or column.
- In a two-way table, inside cells are "and" counts, row and column totals are single features, and the corner is everyone.
- The complement is 1 minus the probability; "neither" and "not" questions use it.
- Read carefully: the math is easy and the points are lost on reading.

### Disagreements and choices made

- **Independent events and draws without replacement.** Larry Learns, LearnQ, Prof Curious and Test Ninjas teach them; the official bank has no such items. The lesson gives them one short paragraph marked low priority.
- **"Or" with overlap.** PrepScholar says overlapping "either/or" events are not tested; Larry Learns and Test Ninjas teach subtracting the overlap. The lesson includes one "or" example, because two-way tables make the overlap easy to see and double-counting is a natural mistake.
- **Desmos.** Test Ninjas suggests graphing x/(x + 18) against the target probability for working-backward questions. Plain algebra is faster for these, so the probability lesson has no Desmos section.
- **How often it appears.** Albert's guide says 10–15% of SAT Math questions involve chance and files them under "Heart of Algebra", a paper-SAT label. The official numbers (49 of 421 bank items in a domain worth about 15% of the test) point to about one question per test, so the lesson says that.
- **"Out of the ___, how many ___?"** This rewording is not quoted from any one source; it combines the "identify target and total" advice (John Jung, PrepScholar) with College Board's own explanations, which always name the group first.
