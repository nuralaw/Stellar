# right-triangles-trig--pythagoras

**Shows:** the squares on the two legs together have the same area as the square on the hypotenuse, so \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\), and the whole-number triples such as 3-4-5 fit it exactly.

**Storyboard:**
1. A right triangle with legs 3 (vertical) and 4 (horizontal) and hypotenuse 5, right angle marked. The sides are labelled \(a\), \(b\), \(c\); \(c\) is tagged "hypotenuse: opposite the right angle, longest".
2. A square grows outward on each side. The 3 by 3 square (9 unit cells) and the 4 by 4 square (16 unit cells) are tinted blue and orange; the 5 by 5 square on the hypotenuse is tinted green.
3. The 9 blue cells and 16 orange cells slide and fill the green square (25 cells), cell by cell. The equation \(9 + 16 = 25\) appears, then \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\).
4. The triangle is scaled up in steps: 6-8-10, 9-12-15, 15-20-25. The squares are dropped; only the side labels and a small "\(\times 2\), \(\times 3\), \(\times 5\)" badge show.
5. A row of chips shows the triples to recognise: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25, highlighted one after another with a small right triangle drawn for each.

**Labels & formulas:** \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\), \(9 + 16 = 25\), \(c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\), "find a leg: \(a^2 = c^2 - b^2\)", the triples.

**Stress:** the cells that move from the two small squares into the big one draw the eye (colour moves, shape does not change). Keep the hypotenuse label in the accent colour so it is not confused with a leg. On a phone, show frames 1-3 and the triple chips; the scaling frame can be a single line of text.
