## What this question looks like

These are short word problems about real things: recipes, speeds, prices, maps, flowing water, the mass of an object, or the number of people living in an area. You can spot them in a few seconds by their words:

- **Ratio words:** "the ratio of A to B is 3 to 5", "3 : 5", "for every 3 A there are 5 B".
- **Rate words:** "per", "each", "every", "at this rate", "at a constant rate", "miles per hour", "dollars per pound".
- **Proportion words:** "is proportional to", "at the same rate", "scale", "1 inch represents 20 miles".
- **Unit words:** two units for the same kind of thing (meters and kilometers, minutes and hours). The conversion is usually given in parentheses, like "(1 mile = 1,760 yards)".
- **Derived units:** a unit built from two other units, like grams per cubic centimeter (density), people per square mile (population density) or kilowatt-hours.

Problem-Solving and Data Analysis makes up about 5 to 7 of the 44 Math questions. In College Board's official question bank, this skill is the largest part of that group (93 of 421 questions), so expect about 1 or 2 of these on your test. Some are multiple choice. Others ask you to type the answer yourself (a student-produced response).

The sub-types you will meet:

1. A ratio with a total or a difference ("there are 42 pens in all").
2. A unit rate or proportion ("at this rate, how many in 2 hours?").
3. A unit conversion, in one step or several steps, including rates like meters per second.
4. Squared and cubed units (square feet, cubic meters).
5. Density and other derived units.
6. Scale drawings, maps and models.
7. Proportional relationships (y = kx), often with letters instead of numbers.

Easy versions take one step. Hard versions chain two or three steps or hide a squared or cubed unit. College Board makes many versions of each pattern by changing only the numbers and the story, so once you know the patterns below, new questions will look familiar.

## The method

1. **Name the target and its unit.** Write it down: "? grams" or "? km per hour". This tells you where to stop.
2. **Write every number with its unit.** Write "14 g per 5 min", not just "14" and "5". Units are your safety net.
3. **Choose the tool.** A ratio with a total or difference: call the parts 2k and 5k. "At this rate" or "proportional": find the unit rate first. Different units: multiply by conversion factors. "Per square mile" or "per cubic centimeter": use density = amount ÷ size.
4. **Set it up so the units cancel.** Every unit you don't want must appear once on top and once on the bottom.
5. **Check the leftover unit before you calculate.** If it is not your target unit, the setup is wrong. Fix it, then calculate (use Desmos for long chains).
6. **Check the size and the question.** Converting to a smaller unit gives a bigger number. Make sure you answer what was asked: the yogurt and not the juice, kilograms and not grams.

> **Key idea:** Almost every mistake on these questions is a setup mistake, not an arithmetic mistake. Spend your time on steps 1 to 5. The calculator can do the rest.

## Rules and formulas you need

### Ratios

- A ratio a : b can also be written "a to b" or a/b. Order matters: "dogs to cats" is not "cats to dogs".
- **Part-to-part vs part-to-whole.** If boys : girls = 3 : 5, the class has 3 + 5 = 8 equal parts. Boys are 3/8 of the class, not 3/5.
- **The k method.** Call the parts 3k and 5k, where k is the size of one part. The total is 8k. The difference is 5k − 3k = 2k. Solve for k, then multiply.
- The real total must be a multiple of the sum of the parts. With 3 : 5, a class of 30 is impossible, but 32 works.
- To keep a ratio the same, multiply (or divide) both parts by the same number. Adding the same number to both parts changes the ratio.

### Rates and unit rates

- A **rate** compares two different units, like miles per hour or dollars per pound.
- A **unit rate** is the amount for one unit: $6 for 4 pounds is $6 ÷ 4 = $1.50 per pound.
- distance = rate × time. In the same way, amount made = rate × time, and cost = price per unit × number of units.
- **Average speed** = total distance ÷ total time. Never just average the two speeds.
- For "at this rate" questions, find the unit rate and multiply. Or write a proportion with the same unit in the same place on both sides: (14 g)/(5 min) = (x g)/(120 min).

### Proportional relationships

- "y is proportional to x" means y = kx for some number k, the **constant of proportionality**. k = y ÷ x is the same for every pair of values.
- The graph is a straight line through (0, 0). A table shows a proportional relationship only if y ÷ x is the same in every row.
- **Scale factor rule:** if x is multiplied by some number, y is multiplied by the same number. If 3 kg cost $8, then 12 kg (3 × 4) cost $32 ($8 × 4). With letters: if n bags hold c cups of rice, then 5n bags hold 5c cups.
- Rarely, more of one thing means less of the other (more workers, less time). Then the product stays the same: 4 workers × 6 hours = 24 worker-hours = 3 workers × 8 hours.

### Unit conversion

- A **conversion factor** is a fraction equal to 1, such as (3 ft)/(1 yd) or (1 yd)/(3 ft). Multiplying by it changes the unit but not the amount.
- Choose the version that puts the unwanted unit on the opposite level (top or bottom), so it cancels.
- For a rate, convert the top unit and the bottom unit separately. Example: 12 m/s × (3,600 s)/(1 h) × (1 km)/(1,000 m) = 43.2 km/h. Seconds cancel, meters cancel, km per hour is left.
- The test gives you unusual conversions (furlongs, fathoms, miles to yards). Know these by heart: 60 s = 1 min, 60 min = 1 h (so 3,600 s = 1 h), 24 h = 1 day, 12 in = 1 ft, 3 ft = 1 yd, 10 mm = 1 cm, 100 cm = 1 m, 1,000 m = 1 km, 1,000 g = 1 kg, 1,000 mL = 1 L, 1 cm³ = 1 mL.

### Squared and cubed units

Area units use the length factor twice. Volume units use it three times.

| Length | Area | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| 1 ft = 12 in | 1 ft² = 144 in² | 1 ft³ = 1,728 in³ |
| 1 yd = 3 ft | 1 yd² = 9 ft² | 1 yd³ = 27 ft³ |
| 1 m = 100 cm | 1 m² = 10,000 cm² | 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³ |

- Squared time works the same way. 1 min = 60 s, so 1 min² = 3,600 s². An acceleration (how fast a speed grows) of 2 meters per second squared is 2 × 3,600 = 7,200 meters per minute squared.
- The safest move: convert the lengths first, then compute the area or volume.

### Density and other derived units

- **density = mass ÷ volume**, for example in grams per cubic centimeter (g/cm³). Rearranged: mass = density × volume, and volume = mass ÷ density.
- **population density = number of people ÷ area**. Rearranged: area = people ÷ population density.
- For a cube with edge s, volume = s³. To go back from volume to edge, take the cube root (∛).
- Some units come from multiplying: kilowatt-hours = kilowatts × hours. A 1.5-kilowatt heater running for 4 hours uses 1.5 × 4 = 6 kilowatt-hours. In the same way, force (pounds) = pressure (pounds per square inch) × area (square inches).

### Scale drawings, maps and models

- Write the scale as a rate: "2 cm on the map represent 5 km" means 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5 km per cm.
- If lengths are multiplied by k, areas are multiplied by k² and volumes by k³.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Ratio with a total

A smoothie recipe mixes yogurt and fruit juice in a ratio of 2 to 5 by volume. A café makes 4.2 liters of this smoothie, using only yogurt and juice. How many liters of yogurt does the café use?

- (A) 0.6
- (B) 1.2
- (C) 1.68
- (D) 3.0

**Solution.** Call the parts 2k (yogurt) and 5k (juice). Together they make 7k, so 7k = 4.2 and k = 0.6. Yogurt = 2k = 1.2 liters. Check: juice = 5k = 3.0 liters, and 1.2 + 3.0 = 4.2. Choice (A) is k, the size of one part, so it stops one step early. Choice (C) is 2/5 of 4.2: it treats the part-to-part ratio 2 : 5 as if yogurt were 2/5 of the whole, but yogurt is 2/7 of the whole. Choice (D) is the juice, not the yogurt.

**Answer:** (B)

### Example 2: Rate and time in different units

A 3D printer uses 14 grams of plastic every 5 minutes. At this rate, how many grams of plastic does the printer use in 2 hours?

- (A) 5.6
- (B) 42.9
- (C) 336
- (D) 1,680

**Solution.** Target: grams. The rate is in minutes but the time is in hours, so change 2 hours to 2 × 60 = 120 minutes. Unit rate: 14 ÷ 5 = 2.8 grams per minute. Amount: 2.8 × 120 = 336 grams. Choice (A) multiplies 2.8 by 2, forgetting to change hours to minutes. Choice (B) uses the rate upside down (5/14 minute per gram × 120). Choice (D) treats 14 grams as the amount for one minute instead of five.

**Answer:** (C)

### Example 3: Converting a rate

Water flows from a hose at a constant rate of 3.6 gallons per minute. What is this rate in quarts per second? (1 gallon = 4 quarts)

*Student-produced response: enter your answer.*

**Solution.** Target: quarts per second. Change the top unit (gallons → quarts) and the bottom unit (minutes → seconds) separately:

3.6 gallons/minute × (4 quarts)/(1 gallon) × (1 minute)/(60 seconds) = (3.6 × 4)/60 = 14.4/60 = 0.24 quarts per second.

Gallons cancel and minutes cancel, leaving quarts per second. Size check: 14.4 quarts in a whole minute is a little less than a quarter of a quart each second, so 0.24 makes sense. If you got 14.4, you converted only the top unit. You may type .24 or the fraction 6/25.

**Answer:** .24 or 6/25

### Example 4: Density with cubed units

Seawater has a density of 1.03 grams per cubic centimeter. What is the mass, in kilograms, of 2.5 cubic meters of seawater? (1 meter = 100 centimeters and 1 kilogram = 1,000 grams)

- (A) 0.2575
- (B) 25.75
- (C) 2,575
- (D) 2,575,000

**Solution.** The density uses cubic centimeters, so first change cubic meters to cubic centimeters. A cubed unit uses the factor three times: 1 m³ = 100 × 100 × 100 = 1,000,000 cm³. So 2.5 m³ = 2,500,000 cm³.

mass = density × volume = 1.03 × 2,500,000 = 2,575,000 grams. In kilograms: 2,575,000 ÷ 1,000 = 2,575 kg.

Choice (A) uses 100 only once (as if 1 m³ were 100 cm³). Choice (B) uses it twice (the factor for square units). Choice (D) is the mass in grams: the last step to kilograms is missing. Sense check: one cubic meter of water is a big tank, and about a ton (1,000 kg) is right for it.

**Answer:** (C)

### Example 5: Scale map and area

On a map, 2 centimeters represent 5 kilometers. A rectangular nature reserve appears on the map as a rectangle 3 centimeters by 4.8 centimeters. What is the actual area of the reserve, in square kilometers?

- (A) 36
- (B) 72
- (C) 90
- (D) 360

**Solution.** Scale as a rate: 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5 km per cm. Convert each length first: 3 × 2.5 = 7.5 km and 4.8 × 2.5 = 12 km. Area = 7.5 × 12 = 90 km². (Same result: map area 14.4 cm² × 2.5² = 90.) Choice (A) multiplies the map area by 2.5 only once, as if area scaled like length. Choices (B) and (D) misread the scale as 1 cm for 5 km; (B) also uses the factor only once, and (D) squares it.

**Answer:** (C)

### Example 6: A rate with letters

A machine wraps 4w gifts in h hours, where w and h are positive constants. At this rate, which expression represents the number of gifts the machine wraps in 30 minutes?

- (A) h/(8w)
- (B) 2w/h
- (C) 2wh
- (D) 120w/h

**Solution.** Treat the letters like numbers. Unit rate: 4w gifts ÷ h hours = 4w/h gifts per hour. The time must be in hours too: 30 minutes = 1/2 hour. Gifts = (4w/h) × (1/2) = 2w/h. Quick check with numbers: if w = 3 and h = 2, the machine wraps 12 gifts in 2 hours, so 3 gifts in half an hour, and 2w/h = 6/2 = 3. Choice (A) uses the rate upside down (hours per gift). Choice (C) multiplies by h instead of dividing. Choice (D) multiplies by 30, as if the time were 30 hours.

**Answer:** (B)

## Traps to avoid

- **Part-to-part used as part-to-whole.** With a 2 : 5 ratio, the first part is 2/7 of the whole, not 2/5. Add the parts to get the whole.
- **Stopping one step early.** The choices include k (one part), the other part, and the total. Reread what the question asks for.
- **A rate or conversion factor upside down.** If the unwanted unit does not cancel, your fraction is flipped. The flipped answer is usually a choice.
- **Converting only half of a rate.** Meters per second to kilometers per hour needs two conversions, one for the top unit and one for the bottom unit.
- **Using 60 when you need 3,600.** Seconds to hours is 60 × 60. A "per second squared" unit also needs 60².
- **Forgetting to square or cube.** 1 yd² = 9 ft², not 3 ft². 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³, not 100 cm³. When in doubt, convert lengths first.
- **Using a length scale on an area.** On a map with 2.5 km per cm, 1 cm² stands for 2.5² = 6.25 km².
- **Adding to keep a ratio.** If length : width = 5 : 3 and the width grows by 6, the length must grow by 6 × 5/3 = 10, not by 6.
- **Averaging speeds.** Average speed is total distance ÷ total time.
- **The wrong unit in the answer.** Grams vs kilograms and minutes vs hours both appear as choices. Check the unit in the question once more before you answer.

## Desmos shortcut

Desmos cannot set up the problem for you, but once the setup is right it removes arithmetic slips:

- Type a whole conversion chain on one line, with the division last, like `3.6*4/60` or `12*3600/1000`. You never round in the middle.
- Typing trap: after `/` or `^`, everything you type stays in the denominator or the exponent until you press the right arrow key. `14/5*120` becomes 14 ÷ (5 × 120), and `100^3/1000` puts 3/1000 in the exponent. Type `14*120/5`, press the right arrow before you continue, or type plain numbers like `1000000`. Always look at the expression Desmos shows.
- For a proportion, cross-multiply on paper first and type one line. For 2/7 = x/4.2, type `4.2*2/7` to get 1.2.
- For a cube, the edge is the cube root of the volume: type `cbrt(270)`.

> **Tip:** On one-step questions, mental math is faster. Save Desmos for chains of conversions and messy decimals.

## Quick checklist

- Did I write the target unit before starting?
- Does every number carry its unit?
- Is the same quantity in the same position on both sides of my proportion?
- Did every unwanted unit cancel, including the one on the bottom of a rate?
- For area or volume, did I square or cube the conversion factor?
- Did I answer the exact thing asked, in the exact unit asked?
- Does the size of my answer make sense?
