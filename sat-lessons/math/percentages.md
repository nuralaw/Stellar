## What this question looks like

Percent questions are easy to spot: the % sign or the word "percent" is in the question. The story is often money (discounts, tax, tips, interest), populations, test scores or a table of counts. Typical stems:

- "What is 15% of 80?", "18 is what percent of 72?", "54 is 30% of what number?"
- "The price was increased by 25%. What is the new price?"
- "After a 20% discount, the price is $36. What was the original price?"
- "The value rose 30% and then fell 30%. What was the net percent change?"
- "The number a is 30% greater than b, and c is 20% less than a. c is what percent of b?"
- "Which percent decrease turns a quantity n into 0.82n?"

Problem-Solving and Data Analysis makes up about 5 to 7 of the 44 Math questions, and percentages make up about a fifth of it (84 of 421 questions in College Board's official question bank). Expect about 1 or 2 on your test. Don't relax because they look simple: in the official bank, about 4 in 10 percentage questions are marked hard, and no other skill in this group has as many hard questions. They get hard when they use percents of 100% or more, chain two or three changes, or make you work backward.

The sub-types:

1. Percent of a number, "what percent", and "of what number".
2. Percent increase and decrease (new value or percent change).
3. Finding the original value before a change.
4. Several changes in a row (successive changes and net change).
5. Percent of a percent, and chains like "a is 60% greater than b".
6. Expressions with letters (1.25m, 0.6x, "k times as many").
7. Percents read from a table.

## The method

1. **Find the base.** The base is the 100%: the number after "of" or "than", or the starting value before a change.
2. **Translate into an equation.** "is" means =, "of" means ×, "what number" is a variable, and p% means p/100.
3. **Turn each change into a multiplier.** Increase by r%: × (1 + r/100). Decrease by r%: × (1 − r/100).
4. **Chain the multipliers.** Several changes: multiply them all. Going backward: divide by the multiplier.
5. **Answer the exact question.** New value? Amount of change? Original value? Percent? To read a percent change from a multiplier, subtract 1: a multiplier of 1.24 means a 24% increase.
6. **Check size and form.** An increase must give a bigger number. On a typed answer, enter only the number: no % sign, no $ sign.

> **Key idea:** A percent change is a multiplication, never an addition. "Increase by 8%" means "multiply by 1.08." Once every change is a multiplier, even the hardest chains are one line of arithmetic.

## Rules and formulas you need

### Percent basics

- Percent means "out of 100": 35% = 35/100 = 0.35. To change a percent to a decimal, move the decimal point two places left: 7.5% = 0.075, 0.5% = 0.005, 150% = 1.5.
- The three basic questions all come from part = (percent as a decimal) × whole:

| Question | Equation | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| What is 30% of 80? | x = 0.30 × 80 | 24 |
| 24 is what percent of 80? | 24 = (p/100) × 80 | p = 30 |
| 24 is 30% of what number? | 24 = 0.30 × x | x = 80 |

> **Tip:** a% of b equals b% of a. So 8% of 50 is the same as 50% of 8, which is 4.

### Multipliers

| Words | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| increase by 6% | × 1.06 |
| decrease by 6% | × 0.94 |
| 6% of | × 0.06 |
| increase by 150% (150% greater than) | × 2.5 |
| 150% of | × 1.5 |
| decrease by 80% (80% less than) | × 0.2 |

- "p% greater than b" means b × (1 + p/100). "p% less than b" means b × (1 − p/100).
- **Read a multiplier back:** the percent change is (multiplier − 1) × 100%. 1.18 × 250 is 18% greater than 250. 2.6t is a 160% increase from t. 0.82n is an 18% decrease from n (because 0.82 − 1 = −0.18).

### Percent change

- percent change = (new − original) ÷ original × 100%. Always divide by the **original** (starting) value.
- "a is what percent greater than b?" = (a − b) ÷ b × 100%. "a is what percent less than b?" = (b − a) ÷ b × 100%. The number after "than" is the base.

### Finding the original value

- original × multiplier = new, so **original = new ÷ multiplier**.
- If a price after a 20% discount is $36, the original is 36 ÷ 0.80 = $45. Taking 20% of $36 and adding it back gives $43.20, which is wrong, because the 20% was a percent of the original price.

### Several changes in a row

- Multiply the multipliers. Never add or subtract the percents.
- Up 20%, then down 20%: 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, a 4% decrease overall, not 0%.
- Net percent change = (product of the multipliers − 1) × 100%.
- The same change repeated n times is (1 + r)^n. That is exponential growth, which the nonlinear functions lesson covers.

### Percent of a percent and chains

- "25% of 60% of the group" = 0.25 × 0.60 = 0.15, so 15% of the group.
- For chains, write every statement as an equation in terms of one letter, then multiply.
- Reversing a comparison changes the percent. If A = 1.25B, then B = A ÷ 1.25 = 0.8A. So B is 80% of A, or 20% less than A, not 25% less.

### Money words

- Discount: × (1 − r). Sales tax or tip: × (1 + r). Tax is usually added after the discount.
- A markup is a percent of the store's cost. A discount is a percent of the marked price.
- Simple interest: interest = principal × rate × time.

### Percent vs percentage points

- If a rate goes from 40% to 46%, it rose by 6 **percentage points** (46 − 40). The percent increase is 6 ÷ 40 = 15%. Read which one the question asks for.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Of what number

At a high school, 216 students ride a bus to school. This number is 45% of all the students at the school. How many students are at the school?

*Student-produced response: enter your answer.*

**Solution.** The base (the 100%) is "all the students", which is unknown. Call it x. Translate: 216 is 45% of x, so 216 = 0.45x, and x = 216 ÷ 0.45 = 480. Check: 45% of 480 is 216, and the whole school must be bigger than one part of it. If you got 97.2, you took 45% of 216, but 216 is the part, not the whole. If you got 4.8, you divided by 45 instead of 0.45.

**Answer:** 480

### Example 2: Percent change from a table

The table shows the number of visitors to a science museum in three months.

| Month | Visitors |
|---|---|
| February | 1,850 |
| March | 2,220 |
| April | 1,776 |

By what percent did the number of visitors decrease from March to April?

- (A) 20%
- (B) 25%
- (C) 80%
- (D) 125%

**Solution.** The original (starting) value is March, so March is the base. Change: 2,220 − 1,776 = 444. Percent change: 444 ÷ 2,220 = 0.20, so a 20% decrease. Choice (B) divides by the new value: 444 ÷ 1,776 = 0.25. Choice (C) says April is 80% of March, which is true, but that is "percent of", not "percent decrease". Choice (D) is March as a percent of April. Notice: visitors rose 20% from February to March and then fell 20%, yet April (1,776) is below February (1,850). Up 20% and down 20% is 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, a 4% loss overall.

**Answer:** (A)

### Example 3: The value before a change

The population of a town grew by 12% from 2020 to 2025. In 2025, the population was 70,000. What was the population in 2020?

- (A) 8,400
- (B) 61,600
- (C) 62,500
- (D) 78,400

**Solution.** Growth by 12% means 2020 population × 1.12 = 70,000. Going backward, divide: 70,000 ÷ 1.12 = 62,500. Check: 62,500 × 1.12 = 70,000. Choice (B) takes 12% off 70,000, but the 12% was a percent of the 2020 population, not of 70,000. Choice (A) is just 12% of 70,000. Choice (D) increases 70,000 by another 12%.

**Answer:** (C)

### Example 4: Percent of a percent

In a club, 60% of the members are seniors. Of the seniors, 25% play chess. What percent of all the club members are seniors who do **not** play chess?

- (A) 15%
- (B) 35%
- (C) 45%
- (D) 75%

**Solution.** The seniors who do not play chess are 100% − 25% = 75% of the seniors. The seniors are 60% of the club. So the group is 75% of 60% of the club: 0.75 × 0.60 = 0.45 = 45%. With numbers: in a club of 100 members, 60 are seniors, and 75% of 60 is 45. Choice (A) is the seniors who do play chess (0.25 × 0.60). Choice (B) subtracts the percents (60 − 25), which mixes two different bases. Choice (D) is a percent of the seniors only, not of the whole club.

**Answer:** (C)

### Example 5: Net change with a percent over 100%

The value of a rare coin increased by 140% from 2020 to 2022 and then decreased by 35% from 2022 to 2024. What was the net percent change in the value of the coin from 2020 to 2024?

- (A) A 9% decrease
- (B) A 56% increase
- (C) A 105% increase
- (D) A 156% increase

**Solution.** Turn each change into a multiplier. Increase by 140%: × (1 + 1.40) = × 2.40. Decrease by 35%: × 0.65. Net: 2.40 × 0.65 = 1.56. Subtract 1: 0.56, so a 56% increase. With numbers: a $100 coin becomes $240, then 0.65 × $240 = $156, which is $56 more than at the start. Choice (A) uses 1.40 as the multiplier, which is "140% of", not "increased by 140%". Choice (C) subtracts the percents (140 − 35). Choice (D) reads the multiplier 1.56 as the percent change, forgetting to subtract 1.

**Answer:** (B)

### Example 6: A chain of comparisons

In a school survey, the number of students who chose soccer was 40% greater than the number who chose basketball. The number who chose tennis was 25% less than the number who chose soccer. The number of students who chose tennis is p% of the number who chose basketball. What is the value of p?

*Student-produced response: enter your answer.*

**Solution.** Write everything in terms of basketball, b. Soccer is 40% greater than b: soccer = 1.40b. Tennis is 25% less than soccer: tennis = 0.75 × 1.40b = 1.05b. So tennis is 105% of basketball, and p = 105. The fastest check is to pick 100 for the base: 100 basketball, so 140 soccer, so 0.75 × 140 = 105 tennis. If you got 115, you added the percents (100 + 40 − 25), but the 25% is a percent of soccer, not of basketball. Type 105, not 1.05 and not 105%.

**Answer:** 105

## Traps to avoid

- **"p% of" vs "p% greater than".** 150% of 40 is 60, but 150% greater than 40 is 40 × 2.5 = 100. With percents of 100% or more, expect both numbers among the choices.
- **The wrong base.** Percent change divides by the original value, and "greater than b" or "less than b" uses b as the base. Dividing by the new value gives a wrong answer that is usually one of the choices.
- **Adding or subtracting percents.** Successive changes multiply: +140% then −35% is not +105%. Percents of different bases can't be added.
- **Undoing a change with the same percent.** A 20% decrease is not undone by a 20% increase. To go backward, divide by the multiplier.
- **Multiplier vs percent.** A 6% increase is × 1.06, not × 0.06 or × 1.6. A multiplier of 1.56 means +56%, not +156%.
- **Answering the wrong quantity.** "Sold", "left" and "original" can all be choices. If 30% of a store's books were sold and 252 are left, the original number is 252 ÷ 0.7 = 360, and 108 were sold.
- **Percentage points vs percent.** 40% to 46% is 6 percentage points, but a 15% increase.
- **Wrong answer form.** If the question says "p%" and asks for p, type 105, not 1.05. Never type the % sign or the $ sign.

## Desmos shortcut

Percent questions are short, so Desmos mostly saves you from slips:

- Type the translated equation with x as the unknown, like `0.45x=216`. Desmos draws the vertical line x = 480. Zoom out to find it (it is far to the right of the normal view), click the line, then click where it crosses the x-axis to read the value.
- For "p% greater" questions, write the percent with x and avoid fractions: for "126 is p% greater than 45", type `45(1+0.01x)=126`. The line is at x = 180.
- Multiply a whole chain of multipliers in one line, like `2.4*0.65`, then subtract 1 in your head.
- Use x, not p, for the unknown. Desmos graphs equations in x and y; for another letter like p it offers a slider instead of solving.

> **Tip:** The "pick 100" trick needs no calculator. When no starting number is given, start with 100 and apply each change.

## Quick checklist

- What is the base (the 100%) for each percent in the question?
- Did I turn every change into a multiplier, with 1 + r or 1 − r?
- For several changes, did I multiply, not add?
- Going backward, did I divide by the multiplier?
- Am I answering the asked quantity: new value, change, original, or percent?
- Is my typed answer just the number, with no % or $ sign?
