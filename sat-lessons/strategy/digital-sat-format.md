This lesson explains how the test is built, so nothing on test day surprises you. Every fact here comes from College Board's own pages as they stood in November 2026: the 2026-27 SAT Weekend Student Guide, the Fall 2026 test directions and the 2026 edition of the Assessment Framework. If College Board changes something later, its website wins.

## The test at a glance

The SAT has two sections: Reading and Writing (R&W) first, then Math. Each section is split into two parts called *modules*. Each module has its own timer.

| Part | Time | Questions | Scored questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading and Writing, module 1 | 32 minutes | 27 | 25 (+2 unscored) |
| Reading and Writing, module 2 | 32 minutes | 27 | 25 (+2 unscored) |
| Break | 10 minutes | none | none |
| Math, module 1 | 35 minutes | 22 | 20 (+2 unscored) |
| Math, module 2 | 35 minutes | 22 | 20 (+2 unscored) |
| Total | 134 minutes of testing (2 hours 14 minutes), plus the break | 98 | 90 |

- On average you have about 71 seconds per R&W question (College Board's figure is 1.19 minutes) and about 95 seconds per Math question (1.59 minutes).
- **Unscored questions** are *pretest* questions: College Board is trying them out for future tests. Each module has two. They look exactly like the other questions, so you can't spot them. Treat every question as if it counts.
- There is no essay on the weekend SAT. (Some U.S. school-day SATs add an essay.)
- Students approved for accommodations, such as extra time, get different timing.

## Bluebook, the testing app

- You take the SAT in Bluebook, College Board's app, on a laptop or tablet: your own Windows or Mac laptop, an iPad, a Windows tablet or a school-managed Chromebook. You can't test on a phone. If you don't have a device, you can ask to borrow one from College Board at least 30 days before the test.
- Install Bluebook before test day. Between 1 and 5 days before the test, complete *exam setup* in the app. This creates your admission ticket.
- Bluebook has a short test preview and several free full-length practice tests. After a practice test, My Practice shows your score, the correct answers and explanations.
- If the Wi-Fi drops during the test, keep working: your answers are saved and you don't lose time. If the app crashes, reopen it, choose Resume Testing and raise your hand so the proctor can let you back in; the timer pauses until you're back.
- Bluebook times each student separately, so people in your room start their breaks and finish at different moments.

## Modules and adaptive routing

The SAT is *adaptive*: it adjusts to how you are doing. But it doesn't change after every question. It adjusts only once per section, between module 1 and module 2.

1. Module 1 has a broad mix of easy, medium and hard questions.
2. Based on your module 1 results, Bluebook gives you a harder or an easier module 2. Both versions still mix easy, medium and hard questions; what changes is the average difficulty.
3. Your section score comes from all the scored questions in both modules.

What this means for you:

- The dividing line is set near the middle: about half of all test takers get the harder module 2.
- The harder module 2 gives you access to the highest scores. The easier module 2 limits how high your section score can go. College Board does not publish the exact limits, so treat any number you see online as a guess.
- Many middle scores can be earned on either path.
- Missing questions on purpose to get an easier module 2 doesn't work. College Board's own simulations show that this strategy leads to a lower score.
- Bluebook doesn't tell you which module 2 you got. Don't waste test time trying to work it out.

How you can move around:

- Inside a module, you can move freely: skip ahead, go back and change answers.
- When the module timer reaches zero, Bluebook saves your answers and moves you on automatically. You can never go back to an earlier module.
- With standard time, you can't leave a module early, even if you have finished. (Bluebook practice tests do let you move on early, so don't build that habit.)

## The order of the questions

### Reading and Writing

Every R&W question has its own short passage (25 to 150 words; sometimes a pair of passages, a table or a graph) and one multiple-choice question with four choices. The questions are grouped by type, and the groups always come in this order:

| Order | Group (domain) | Question types | What you'll see | Scored per test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Craft and Structure | Words in Context, Text Structure and Purpose, Cross-Text Connections | "the most logical and precise word or phrase", the purpose of a sentence or text, Text 1 and Text 2 | 13–15 |
| 2 | Information and Ideas | Central Ideas and Details, Command of Evidence (from text or from data), Inferences | the main idea, "which finding would most directly support…", tables and graphs, "most logically completes the text" | 12–14 |
| 3 | Standard English Conventions | Boundaries; Form, Structure, and Sense | "conforms to the conventions of Standard English": punctuation, verbs, pronouns | 11–15 |
| 4 | Expression of Ideas | Transitions, Rhetorical Synthesis | "the most logical transition", then bulleted student notes | 8–12 |

- Inside each group, questions of the same type sit together and go from easier to harder. So difficulty "resets" when a new type starts: the hardest vocabulary question can be followed by an easy question of the next type.
- The grammar group (Standard English Conventions) is different: its questions go from easier to harder whatever grammar skill they test.
- Unscored questions sit among questions of the same type.
- You may not use a calculator in this section; a handheld calculator must stay under your desk.

### Math

- Four content areas, mixed together. Scored questions per test: Algebra 13–15, Advanced Math 13–15, Problem-Solving and Data Analysis 5–7, Geometry and Trigonometry 5–7.
- Across each module, questions go from easiest to hardest. Unscored questions can appear anywhere in the module.
- About 30% of the questions are word problems set in science, social studies or real life.
- About 75% are multiple choice with four choices. The rest are *student-produced response* (SPR) questions: you type your own answer into a box.
- You may use a calculator on every math question.
- Unless a question says otherwise, figures are drawn to scale.

## Typing math answers (student-produced response)

These are the official rules from College Board's 2026 test directions:

1. If you find more than one correct answer, enter only one.
2. You can enter up to 5 characters for a positive answer and up to 6 characters for a negative answer. The minus sign counts as a character, and College Board's examples show that the decimal point and the fraction bar (/) count too.
3. If your answer is a fraction that doesn't fit, enter its decimal.
4. If your answer is a decimal that doesn't fit, cut it off or round it at the fourth digit, so that it fills the space. A shorter decimal such as `0.66` for 2/3 is marked wrong.
5. Enter a mixed number as an improper fraction or a decimal: 3½ becomes `7/2` or `3.5`.
6. Don't type symbols such as a percent sign, a comma or a dollar sign.

Bluebook shows an answer preview next to the box: the way it reads your entry. Check it every time.

Here is how the rules work on some sample answers (our own examples):

| Your answer | Accepted entries | Marked wrong |
|---|---|---|
| 4¼ | `17/4`, `4.25` | `4 1/4` (a space), `41/4` (read as 41 over 4) |
| 5/9 = 0.5555… | `5/9`, `.5555`, `.5556`, `0.555`, `0.556` | `0.55`, `.56`, `0.56` (too short) |
| −2/7 = −0.285714… | `-2/7`, `-.2857`, `-0.285`, `-0.286` | `-.28`, `-0.29` (too short) |
| twelve thousand five hundred | `12500` | `12,500` (no commas) |
| 30, when the question asks for the value of p in "p%" | `30` | `30%` (no symbols) |

## The built-in tools

| Tool | What it does | How to use it well |
|---|---|---|
| Timer | Counts down the time left in the module. You can hide it until 5 minutes remain; then it stays visible and you get an alert. | Hide it if it stresses you, but check it at your pacing checkpoints. |
| Mark for Review | Puts a flag on a question so you can find it again in the same module. | Always choose an answer before you flag. |
| Question menu | Shows which questions you answered, skipped or flagged, and jumps to any question in the module. | Open it near the end to catch blanks. |
| Option Eliminator (the answer eliminator) | Crosses out answer choices you've ruled out. You can undo it. | Use it on hard questions so you don't reread dead choices. |
| Highlights & Notes (the annotation tool) | Highlights passage text and adds notes. R&W questions only. | Mark the key words of the question or the goal. |
| Calculator | A built-in Desmos calculator for the whole Math section. You can switch between graphing and scientific at any time. | You may bring your own approved calculator instead. |
| Reference sheet | Common math formulas, available in the Math section. | Learn what's on it before test day. |
| Line reader, zoom, color contrast | Help you focus on one line, enlarge text or change colors. | Try them in the test preview. |

More things to know:

- After the last question of a module you reach a review page called Check Your Work. Like the question menu, it lets you jump back to any question in the module while time remains.
- The reference sheet includes the area and circumference of a circle, the areas of a rectangle and a triangle, the Pythagorean theorem, the two special right triangles (30-60-90 and 45-45-90), the volumes of a box, cylinder, sphere, cone and pyramid, and three facts: a circle has 360 degrees of arc, a circle has 2π radians of arc, and the angles of a triangle add up to 180 degrees.
- Your own calculator must not have CAS (computer algebra system) features, which solve or rearrange equations with letters. Models with "CAS" in the name are not allowed.
- The test center gives you scratch paper; bring pencils or pens. A watch without an alarm is allowed.
- There is one 10-minute break, between the two sections. Snacks are for the break only. If you take an extra (unscheduled) break, you lose testing time.

## Scoring

- Each section is scored from 200 to 800, in steps of 10. Your total score is the sum: 400 to 1600.
- **There is no penalty for a wrong answer.** A wrong answer and a blank both earn nothing, so always answer. College Board itself says it is better to guess than to leave a question blank, especially after you rule out one or two choices.
- Your score is not simply the number of right answers. College Board uses *item response theory* (IRT): your score depends on which questions you got right, including how hard they were, and on whether your pattern of answers looks like guessing. College Board says this is not a guessing penalty. One result: two students with the same number of right answers can get different scores.
- Unscored pretest questions never affect your score.
- College Board's college-readiness benchmarks are 480 for Reading and Writing and 530 for Math.

### Your score report

- Most weekend scores are released 2 to 4 weeks after the test. For example, scores for the November 7, 2026 test come out on November 20, 2026.
- Online you see your total and section scores, percentiles (how you compare with other students) and how you did in each content domain.
- If the test went badly, you can cancel a weekend score until 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time one week after the test date. A canceled score can't be brought back.

## Sending scores and superscoring

- Every registration includes 4 free score sends. You choose the colleges when you register, any time before the test, or up to 9 days after it.
- **Score Choice** lets you pick which test dates to send. You send a whole test date, never a single section. Score Choice does not apply to the 4 free sends, and some colleges require all of your scores. If you don't use Score Choice, College Board sends your scores from your most recent six test dates.
- **Superscoring** means a college combines your best R&W score and your best Math score, even if they come from different test dates. College Board doesn't do this; each college decides. Check each college's admissions website.

## Check yourself

### Example 1: Entering 7/12

You solve a math question and get 7/12, which is 0.58333… Which of these entries will NOT receive credit?

- (A) `7/12`
- (B) `.5833`
- (C) `0.583`
- (D) `0.58`

**Solution.** The fraction 7/12 fits in 5 characters, so (A) is accepted. A decimal must fill the space, cut off or rounded at the fourth digit: `.5833` uses 5 characters and `0.583` uses 5 characters, so (B) and (C) are accepted. `0.58` stops too early, so it gets no credit, just like `0.66` for 2/3 in College Board's own examples.

**Answer:** (D)

### Example 2: A negative answer

Your answer to a math question is −5/6, which is −0.83333… Which entry will receive credit?

- (A) `-0.83`
- (B) `-0.84`
- (C) `-.8333`
- (D) `-.83`

**Solution.** A negative answer may use 6 characters, and the minus sign counts. `-.8333` uses all 6 (minus, point, four digits), so (C) is accepted. (A) and (D) are too short, like `-0.33` and `-.33` for −1/3 in College Board's examples. (B) is too short and also rounds wrongly.

**Answer:** (C)

## Quick checklist

- Bluebook is installed, exam setup is done 1 to 5 days before the test, and my admission ticket is ready.
- I know the timing: 32 + 32 minutes of R&W, a 10-minute break, then 35 + 35 minutes of Math.
- I know the R&W order: vocabulary and reading first, grammar next, transitions and notes last.
- I can type math answers correctly: 5 characters (6 if negative), decimals that fill the space, no symbols.
- I have tried every tool in a full Bluebook practice test.
- I will answer every question, because wrong answers cost nothing.
