# nonlinear-functions--shifts

**Shows:** adding to \(f\) outside moves the graph up or down, replacing \(x\) by \(x + h\) inside moves it left (the "backward" move), and a minus in front flips it.

**Storyboard:**
1. The parabola \(f(x) = (x - 5)^2 + 2\) on a grid with its vertex dot at \((5, 2)\) in the accent colour.
2. A copy slides left 3: label \(f(x + 3)\), vertex dot moves to \((2, 2)\). A horizontal arrow "left 3". A small note shows why: "\(x + 3 = 5\) when \(x = 2\)".
3. From there a copy slides down 4: label \(f(x + 3) - 4\), vertex at \((2, -2)\). A vertical arrow "down 4".
4. A quick flip: \(-f(x)\) mirrors the original over the \(x\)-axis (maximum instead of minimum), then fades.
5. Summary strip: "inside = left/right, backward" and "outside = up/down".

**Labels & formulas:** \(f(x) = (x - 5)^2 + 2\), \(f(x + 3)\), \(f(x + 3) - 4\), \(-f(x)\), "vertex \((5, 2)\)", "\((2, -2)\)".

**Stress:** the original graph stays neutral, the moving copy is in the accent colour, and the vertex dot follows the copy. One move per beat. Arrows are short and thick; the "why" note appears under the left shift only.
