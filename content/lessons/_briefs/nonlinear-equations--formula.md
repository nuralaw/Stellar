# nonlinear-equations--formula

**Shows:** how \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) travel from the equation into the quadratic formula, and where the sign slips happen.

**Storyboard:**
1. Equation \(2x^2 - 5x - 3 = 0\). The three coefficients are boxed and labelled \(a = 2\), \(b = -5\), \(c = -3\) (signs included, in the accent colour).
2. The formula \(x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\) appears with each letter dimmed. One by one the boxes fly in and replace the letters: \(-(-5)\) turns into \(5\), \((-5)^2\), \(4 \cdot 2 \cdot (-3)\).
3. The discriminant is computed inside the root: \(25 + 24 = 49\), then \(\sqrt{49} = 7\).
4. The \(\pm\) splits the fraction into two: \(\dfrac{5 + 7}{4} = 3\) and \(\dfrac{5 - 7}{4} = -\dfrac12\).
5. A trap strip at the bottom: "\(-b\) is the opposite of \(b\)" and "divide the whole top by \(2a\)".

**Labels & formulas:** \(2x^2 - 5x - 3 = 0\), \(x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\), \(x = 3\) and \(x = -\dfrac12\), "read the signs".

**Stress:** the substituted numbers are the accent; the formula frame stays neutral. Negative numbers always show their brackets so the sign work is visible. Phones: keep the fraction at least 18 px tall.
