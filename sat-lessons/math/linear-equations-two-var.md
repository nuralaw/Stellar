## What this question looks like

The equation has two letters, usually x and y, and both appear only to the first power: y = 3x − 5, 4x + 7y = 140 or y − 2 = 5(x + 1). A *solution* is a pair of numbers (x, y) that makes the equation true. All the solutions together form a straight line in the xy-plane.

On screen you may see an equation, a graph of a line, a table of points, or a story where two amounts share one total: money, time or points.

Common question stems:

- "What is the slope of the line?"
- "The graph crosses the y-axis at the point (0, b). What is the value of b?"
- "Which equation defines a line that is perpendicular to line k and passes through (4, 1)?"
- "Which point (x, y) lies on the line?"
- "What is the best interpretation of 7 in this context?"
- "If 12 banners are bought, how many packs of balloons can be bought?"

College Board does not publish a count for each skill. Algebra gives 13 to 15 of the 40 scored math questions, shared by five linear skills, so expect around three of these per test (our estimate). Slope and intercept questions are often student-produced response (SPR) questions, where you type your own answer.

| Sub-type | What you do |
|---|---|
| Slope and intercepts | Read them from any form, from a graph or from two points |
| Write the line | Use two points, a point and a slope, or a parallel or perpendicular line |
| Parallel and perpendicular | Same slope, or the flipped slope with the opposite sign |
| Point on the line | Put the point into the equation |
| Graph and equation | Match the signs of the slope and intercept to a picture |
| Context: Ax + By = C | Explain each number, or find one amount from the other |

## The method

1. **Decide what you need:** a slope, an intercept, a full equation, a point, or a meaning.
2. **Use the form that shows it fastest** (see the table below). Slope-intercept form shows the slope and the y-intercept. Standard form gives the intercepts quickly. Point-slope form builds a line through a known point.
3. **Find the slope.** From two points: rise ÷ run. From Ax + By = C: −A/B.
4. **Find intercepts.** Set x = 0 to get the y-intercept. Set y = 0 to get the x-intercept.
5. **Build a line.** Slope first, then a point: y − y_1 = m(x − x_1). Simplify only as far as the answer choices need.
6. **Parallel or perpendicular?** Parallel lines have the same slope. For a perpendicular line, flip the slope and change its sign.
7. **In a story, name each letter with units.** A coefficient is the amount for one item, a term like 4x is the total for that kind of item, and the constant is the grand total.
8. **Check.** Put a known point into your final equation.

## Rules and formulas you need

None of these are on the test's reference sheet, so learn them by heart.

### Three forms of a line

| Form | Looks like | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Slope-intercept | y = mx + b | reading the slope m and the y-intercept b |
| Point-slope | y − y_1 = m(x − x_1) | writing a line through a known point (x_1, y_1) |
| Standard | Ax + By = C | finding intercepts, and totals in word problems |

From Ax + By = C (with B ≠ 0): slope = −A/B, y-intercept = C/B, and x-intercept = C/A (when A ≠ 0).

> **Tip:** In standard form, the number in front of x is *not* the slope. Use −A/B, or solve for y.

### Converting between forms

- Standard to slope-intercept: move the x-term to the right side, then divide everything by the number in front of y. 4x + 2y = 10 becomes 2y = −4x + 10, then y = −2x + 5.
- Point-slope to slope-intercept: distribute m, then add y_1 to both sides. y − 3 = 2(x + 1) becomes y = 2x + 2 + 3, which is y = 2x + 5.
- Slope-intercept to standard: clear the fraction, then put x and y on the same side. y = (2/3)x − 4 becomes 3y = 2x − 12. Subtract 3y and add 12 on both sides: 12 = 2x − 3y, so 2x − 3y = 12.
- Two equations describe the same line when one is a multiple of the other: 2x − 3y = 12 and 4x − 6y = 24 are the same line.

### Slope

- m = (y_2 − y_1)/(x_2 − x_1). Subtract in the same order on top and bottom.
- Positive slope: the line rises from left to right. Negative slope: it falls. Slope 0: a horizontal line, like y = 3. Undefined slope: a vertical line, like x = 3.
- The bigger the slope's absolute value (its size without the sign), the steeper the line.

### Intercepts: the cover-up method

- An x-intercept is a point (a, 0). A y-intercept is a point (0, b).
- In 3x − 8y = 24, cover the y-term: 3x = 24, so x = 8 and the x-intercept is (8, 0). Cover the x-term: −8y = 24, so y = −3 and the y-intercept is (0, −3).
- The two intercepts are two points, so they also give the slope: (−3 − 0)/(0 − 8) = 3/8, the same as −A/B = −3/(−8).

### Parallel and perpendicular

- Parallel lines have equal slopes and different y-intercepts. Equal slopes and equal intercepts mean the same line.
- Perpendicular slopes multiply to −1: 2/3 and −3/2, 4 and −1/4, −5 and 1/5.
- A horizontal line and a vertical line are perpendicular.

### Points and solutions

- A point lies on the line exactly when its coordinates make the equation true. Put its x-value in for x and its y-value in for y.
- In an ordered pair (x, y), x always comes first.

### Equations in context: Ax + By = C

A café sells coffees for $3 and sandwiches for $5. The equation 3x + 5y = 60 describes x coffees and y sandwiches that bring in exactly $60.

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 3 | the price of one coffee |
| 3x | the money from all the coffees |
| 60 | the total sales |
| x-intercept (20, 0) | 20 coffees and no sandwiches |
| y-intercept (0, 12) | 12 sandwiches and no coffees |
| slope −3/5 | for every 5 more coffees, 3 fewer sandwiches keep the total at $60 |

To write such an equation yourself, use (price of one item) × (number of items) + (price of the other item) × (number of those items) = total. To find one amount from the other, substitute. If y = 6, then 3x + 30 = 60, so x = 10.

### Moving a line

- Up k units: y = mx + b + k. Down k units: y = mx + b − k.
- Right h units: replace x with (x − h).
- Moving a line never changes its slope.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Slope hidden in standard form

In the xy-plane, line p has the equation 5x + 2y = 30. What is the slope of line p?

- (A) −5/2
- (B) −2/5
- (C) 5/2
- (D) 15

**Solution.** Solve for y. Subtract 5x from both sides: 2y = −5x + 30. Divide by 2: y = −(5/2)x + 15. The slope is −5/2. The shortcut −A/B gives the same value, −5/2.

Choice (C) forgets that 5x changes sign when it moves to the other side. Choice (B) turns the fraction upside down. Choice (D) is the y-intercept, not the slope.

**Answer:** (A)

### Example 2: From two points to the x-intercept

A line in the xy-plane passes through the points (−3, 7) and (5, −9). The line crosses the x-axis at the point (r, 0). What is the value of r?

*Student-produced response: enter your answer.*

**Solution.** Slope: (−9 − 7)/(5 − (−3)) = −16/8 = −2. Use point-slope form with (−3, 7): y − 7 = −2(x − (−3)), which is y − 7 = −2(x + 3). Distribute: y − 7 = −2x − 6, so y = −2x + 1. Check with the other point: −2(5) + 1 = −9.

On the x-axis, y = 0. So 0 = −2x + 1, and x = 1/2.

A common slip is writing (x − 3) instead of (x + 3). That gives y = −2x + 13 and the wrong value 6.5. Also make sure you set y = 0, not x = 0. Setting x = 0 gives the y-intercept, 1.

**Answer:** 1/2 or .5 or 0.5

### Example 3: Perpendicular line through a point

Line k is perpendicular to the line 2x − 3y = 12 and passes through the point (4, 1). Which equation defines line k?

- (A) y = −(3/2)x + 7
- (B) y = −(2/3)x + 11/3
- (C) y = (2/3)x − 5/3
- (D) y = (3/2)x − 5

**Solution.** The slope of the given line is −A/B = −2/(−3) = 2/3. (Or solve for y: −3y = −2x + 12, so y = (2/3)x − 4.) A perpendicular slope is the flipped fraction with the opposite sign: −3/2. Through (4, 1): y − 1 = −(3/2)(x − 4), so y = −(3/2)x + 6 + 1 = −(3/2)x + 7.

All four lines pass through (4, 1), so checking the point can't help here. Only the slope decides. Choice (C) has the same slope as the given line, so it is parallel, not perpendicular. Choice (B) only changes the sign. Choice (D) only flips the fraction.

**Answer:** (A)

### Example 4: What does the intercept mean?

Rami has $140 to spend on supplies for a school event. Each pack of balloons costs $4, and each banner costs $7. The equation 4x + 7y = 140 represents the numbers of packs of balloons, x, and banners, y, that Rami can buy if he spends exactly $140. What is the best interpretation of the y-intercept of the graph of this equation in the xy-plane?

- (A) If Rami buys no banners, he can buy 20 packs of balloons.
- (B) If Rami buys no packs of balloons, he can buy 20 banners.
- (C) If Rami buys no packs of balloons, he can buy 35 banners.
- (D) Each banner costs $20.

**Solution.** The y-intercept is the point where x = 0, which means no packs of balloons. Then 7y = 140, so y = 20. The point (0, 20) means: with no packs of balloons, Rami can buy 20 banners.

Read the other parts the same way. 4 is the price of one pack, and 7 is the price of one banner. 4x is the money spent on balloons, 7y is the money spent on banners, and 140 is the total. The x-intercept, (35, 0), means 35 packs of balloons and no banners.

Choice (A) swaps the roles of x and y. Choice (C) divides 140 by the price of a pack instead of the price of a banner. Choice (D) treats a number of banners as a price. Same equation, different question: if Rami buys 14 packs of balloons, then 56 + 7y = 140, so 7y = 84 and he can buy 12 banners.

**Answer:** (B)

### Example 5: Read the signs from a graph

In the xy-plane, the graph of a line slants down from left to right and crosses the y-axis above the origin. Which of the following could be an equation of this line?

- (A) 3x + 2y = 6
- (B) 3x − 2y = 6
- (C) −3x + 2y = 6
- (D) 3x + 2y = −6

**Solution.** "Slants down from left to right" means a negative slope. "Crosses the y-axis above the origin" means a positive y-intercept. For each choice, find the slope −A/B and the y-intercept C/B:

- (A): slope −3/2, y-intercept 3. Both match.
- (B): slope 3/2, y-intercept −3.
- (C): slope 3/2, y-intercept 3.
- (D): slope −3/2, y-intercept −3.

**Answer:** (A)

### Example 6: Unknown coefficient and a perpendicular line

In the xy-plane, line q has the equation ax + 6y = 18, where a is a constant. Line q is perpendicular to the line that passes through the points (−1, 4) and (3, 7). What is the value of a?

*Student-produced response: enter your answer.*

**Solution.** Slope of the line through the two points: (7 − 4)/(3 − (−1)) = 3/4. Line q is perpendicular to it, so the slope of line q is −4/3. The slope of ax + 6y = 18 is −A/B = −a/6. Set them equal: −a/6 = −4/3. Multiply both sides by −6: a = 8.

Check: 8x + 6y = 18 gives y = −(4/3)x + 3, and (3/4) × (−4/3) = −1. Mistakes to avoid: dropping the minus sign in −a/6 gives a = −8, and using the parallel slope 3/4 gives a = −4.5.

**Answer:** 8

## Traps to avoid

- **Reading the slope straight from standard form.** In 5x + 2y = 30 the slope is −5/2, not 5 or 2.
- **Flipping −A/B.** The slope is −A/B (x-coefficient on top), not −B/A.
- **Subtracting in different orders.** (y_2 − y_1)/(x_1 − x_2) gives the wrong sign. Keep the same point first on top and bottom.
- **Mixing up the intercepts.** The x-intercept has y = 0; the y-intercept has x = 0. Questions often put both values in the choices.
- **Doing only half of a perpendicular slope.** You must flip the fraction and change the sign. Checking that the product is −1 catches both mistakes.
- **Sign slips with negative coordinates.** In point-slope form with the point (−3, 7), you get (x + 3), not (x − 3).
- **Trusting a point check alone.** When every choice passes through the given point, only the slope decides (Example 3).
- **Swapping x and y in a story.** Write down what each letter counts before you read the choices.
- **Confusing a price with a total.** In 4x + 7y = 140, 4 is the price of one item, 4x is the money spent on that item, and 140 is the whole budget.
- **Horizontal versus vertical.** y = 3 is horizontal, with slope 0. x = 3 is vertical, and its slope is undefined.

## Desmos shortcut

**See the line and its intercepts.** Type the equation in any form, exactly as given, for example `5x+2y=30`. Click the line: gray points appear where it crosses the axes, here (0, 15) and (6, 0). Zoom out to see (0, 15), which is above the normal view. Two points give the slope: (15 − 0)/(0 − 6) = −2.5.

**Line through two points.** Make a table with the points: −3 and 5 in the x_1 column, 7 and −9 in the y_1 column. Then type `y1~mx1+b` (Desmos turns each 1 into a small subscript by itself; with an underscore, the ~ gets stuck in the subscript and you get an error). Desmos shows m = −2 and b = 1. Graph `y=mx+b` and click the point on the x-axis: (0.5, 0).

**Check a point or a choice.** Type a point like `(4,1)` to see whether it sits on your line, or graph each answer choice and compare it with the line described in the question.

**Careful with pictures.** Desmos never prints the slope of a line. Also, if the two axes use different scales, lines can look perpendicular when they aren't. For slopes, parallel lines and perpendicular lines, trust −A/B and the "product is −1" test.

For a find-the-constant question like Example 6, the algebra (−a/6 = −4/3) takes seconds, so skip the slider.

## Quick checklist

- Do I need a slope, an intercept, an equation, a point or a meaning?
- If the line is in standard form, did I use −A/B or solve for y?
- Did I subtract in the same order on top and bottom of the slope formula?
- For an intercept, did I set the correct variable to 0?
- For perpendicular lines, did I flip and change the sign, so the product is −1?
- In a story, do I know what x and y count, and what each number means?
- Did I check my final equation with a known point?
