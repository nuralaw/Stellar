# boundaries--clause-test

**Shows:** the first move of every boundary question: cut the text at the blank and test each side alone for subject + verb.

**Storyboard:**
1. A sentence on one line with a blank slot in the middle: "The harbor froze in January [ ] the ferries stopped running." The blank pulses.
2. A scissor cut drops at the blank. The line splits into LEFT and RIGHT cards, which slide apart.
3. On the LEFT card, "harbor" lights blue (subject) and "froze" lights orange (verb); a green tick and the label "full sentence" appear.
4. Same on the RIGHT card ("ferries" blue, "stopped" orange, green tick).
5. Both ticks pulse: a verdict bar reads "full + full = period, semicolon or comma + FANBOYS". Then a counter-example flashes: "After the ferries stopped" with a red cross and the label "fragment" (the word "After" leaves it hanging).

**Labels & formulas:** "subject", "verb", "full sentence", "fragment", "Left?", "Right?", verdict "full + full", "fragment: hook it on, no period, no semicolon". No math formulas.

**Stress:** subject (blue) and verb (orange) chips are the heroes; the tick/cross colours carry the verdict. Keep the cut line thick. Readable on a phone: at most two cards and one verdict bar on screen, nothing smaller than the kit's caption size.
