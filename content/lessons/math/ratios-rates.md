# Rates and scales
Write the units. Multiply so they cancel.

## Units cancel like numbers

Write every number **with its unit**. A **rate** compares two units, like liters per minute. Multiply so the unwanted unit cancels.

```example
A pump fills 15 liters in 3 minutes. At this rate, how many liters does it fill in 8 minutes?
1. 15 L / 3 min :: Write the rate as a fraction.
2. 15 L / 3 min × 8 min :: Multiply by the time. Minutes cancel.
3. 15 × 8 ÷ 3 = 40 :: Only liters are left.
4. 40 liters
```

```more
A **rate** is a fraction with two different units, such as 5 L/min ("5 liters per minute"). When you multiply a rate by an amount of time, the minutes on the bottom cancel with the minutes you multiply by. What is left is the unit you want.
```

## Set up a rate

```quiz
A printer prints 18 pages in 4 minutes. At this rate, how many pages does it print in 10 minutes?
answer: 45
- 180 :: 18 pages take 4 minutes, not 1. Divide by 4.
- 7.2 :: You multiplied by 4 and divided by 10. Swap them.
> 18 pages / 4 min × 10 min = 45 pages.
~ zoom -1 14 -5 60
~ type y=\frac{18}{4}x :: Pages = rate × minutes. x is minutes.
~ type x=10 :: Mark 10 minutes.
~ point (10, 45) :: Click the crossing: 45 pages.
```

## Read a scale

```quiz
On a map, 2 centimeters stand for 5 kilometers. Two towns are 7 centimeters apart on the map. How many kilometers apart are the towns in real life?
answer: 17.5
- 2.8 :: You flipped the scale. 1 cm stands for 2.5 km.
- 35 :: You used 5 km for each cm. 2 cm = 5 km.
> The scale is 5 km / 2 cm. So 7 cm × 5 km / 2 cm = 17.5 km.
~ type \frac{5}{2}\cdot 7 :: Kilometers per cm times 7 cm: 17.5.
```

# Unit conversions
A conversion factor is a fraction worth 1.

## Chain the fractions

A **conversion factor** is a fraction equal to 1, like (1 m)/(100 cm). Start unit on top? Put it on the bottom: it cancels.

```example
A rope is 4.5 kilometers long. How many centimeters is that? (1 km = 1,000 m, 1 m = 100 cm)
1. 4.5 km × (1,000 m)/(1 km) = 4,500 m :: Kilometers cancel.
2. 4,500 m × (100 cm)/(1 m) :: Meters cancel.
3. 450,000 cm
```

```more
(1 m)/(100 cm) equals 1 because 1 m and 100 cm are the same length. Multiplying by 1 does not change the length, only the unit. Some questions use US units, like feet and gallons. The question always gives the facts, such as 1 foot = 12 inches. Chain them the same way.
```

## Convert with fractions

```quiz
A hose is 1,080 inches long. How long is it in yards? (1 foot = 12 inches, 1 yard = 3 feet)
answer: 30
- 90 :: That is feet. Convert feet to yards too.
- 270 :: You multiplied by 3. To cancel feet, divide by 3.
> 1,080 in × (1 ft)/(12 in) × (1 yd)/(3 ft) = 30 yd.
~ type \frac{1080}{12\cdot 3} :: Divide by 12 inches per foot, then 3 feet per yard: 30.
```

# Convert a rate
Two units, so two factors.

## Top and bottom

A speed has two units, so use two factors. 1 hour = 3,600 seconds.

```visual
ratios-rates--cancel
```

```example
A car drives at 90 kilometers per hour. What is its speed in meters per second? (1 kilometer = 1,000 meters)
1. 90 km/h × (1,000 m)/(1 km) = 90,000 m/h :: Kilometers cancel.
2. 90,000 m/h × (1 h)/(3,600 s) :: Hours cancel.
3. 25 m/s
```

```more
Convert one unit at a time. First the top: kilometers to meters. Then the bottom: hours to seconds. Put each old unit opposite where it is now: km is on top, so km goes on the bottom of its factor. h is on the bottom, so h goes on top of its factor. Check at the end that only m/s is left.
```

## Convert both units

```quiz
A drone flies at 6 meters per second. What is its speed, in kilometers per hour? (1 kilometer = 1,000 meters)
answer: 21.6
- 0.36 :: You used 60 seconds per hour. It is 3,600.
- 21600 :: That is meters per hour. Divide by 1,000.
> 6 m/s × (3,600 s)/(1 h) × (1 km)/(1,000 m) = 21.6 km/h.
~ type 6\cdot \frac{3600}{1000} :: Seconds to hours (× 3,600), meters to km (÷ 1,000): 21.6.
```

# Density
A rate made from two other units.

## Mass per volume

**Density** = mass ÷ volume, a rate like g/cm³. A **derived unit** is built from two units.

```example
A metal has a density of 8 grams per cm³. A block of this metal has a volume of 45 cm³. What is its mass, in grams?
1. Density: 8 g/cm³ :: 8 grams in every 1 cm³.
2. 8 g/cm³ × 45 cm³ :: Cubic centimeters cancel.
3. 360 grams
```

```more
A **derived unit** is a unit made by combining others: km/h is distance per time, and g/cm³ is mass per volume. Population density works the same way: people per square mile. To find the total, multiply the density by the amount. To find the density, divide the total by the amount.
```

## Find the density

```quiz
A rock has a mass of 120 grams and a volume of 40 cm³. What is its density?
* 3 g/cm³
- 0.33 g/cm³ :: You divided volume by mass. Density is mass ÷ volume.
- 4,800 g/cm³ :: You multiplied. Density is mass ÷ volume.
> Density = 120 g ÷ 40 cm³ = 3 g/cm³.
```

## Find the mass

```quiz
A piece of wood has a density of 0.6 grams per cm³ and a volume of 250 cm³. What is the mass of the wood, in grams?
answer: 150
- 1250/3 :: You divided. Mass = density × volume.
- 250 :: That is the volume. Multiply it by the density.
> Mass = density × volume = 0.6 g/cm³ × 250 cm³ = 150 g.
~ type 0.6\cdot 250 :: Density times volume: 150.
```

# Ratios and totals
3 to 5 means parts 3k and 5k.

## Parts of k

A ratio of a to b means parts **ak** and **bk**. The total is (a + b)k.

```example
Paint mixes blue and white in a ratio of 2 to 7. A batch is 36 liters. How many liters are white?
1. 2k + 7k = 36 :: Blue is 2k, white is 7k.
2. k = 4 :: 9k = 36.
3. White = 7k = 28 liters
```

```more
A **ratio** of 2 to 7 means for every 2 parts blue there are 7 parts white. One part is a mystery size, k. So blue = 2k and white = 7k. The total is 9 parts. Find k from the total, then multiply by the parts you want.
```

## Ratio with a total

```quiz
A jar holds only red and blue beads, in a ratio of 3 red to 5 blue. There are 64 beads in all. How many are red?
- 40 :: That is blue, 5k. Red is 3k.
* 24
- 38.4 :: Red is 3/8 of the total, not 3/5.
> 8k = 64, so k = 8. Red = 3k = 24.
~ zoom -5 70 -5 70
~ type y=\frac{5}{3}x :: x is red, y is blue: 5 blue per 3 red.
~ type x+y=64 :: Red plus blue is 64.
~ point (24, 40) :: Click the crossing: red x = 24.
```

# Trap: square and cube units
Area: square the factor. Volume: cube it.

## Square or cube the factor

1 yd = 3 ft, but 1 yd² = 3 × 3 = **9** ft², and 1 yd³ = 3 × 3 × 3 = **27** ft³.

```visual
ratios-rates--square
```

```example
A floor has an area of 30 square yards. What is its area in square feet? (1 yard = 3 feet)
1. 1 yd² = 3 × 3 = 9 ft² :: Square the length factor.
2. 30 × 9 = 270 :: Feet are smaller: the number grows.
3. 270 square feet
```

```more
Area counts squares, so a change of unit happens in two directions: length and width. That is why the factor is squared. Volume counts cubes, so it happens in three directions, and the factor is cubed. Never use 3 once for an area or a volume.
```

## Area units

```quiz
A tile has an area of 288 square inches. What is its area in square feet? (1 foot = 12 inches)
- 24 :: You divided by 12 once. Square it: 12 × 12 = 144.
* 2
- 3456 :: You multiplied. Feet are bigger, so divide by 144.
> 1 ft² = 144 in². A bigger unit gives a smaller number: 288 ÷ 144 = 2.
```

## Volume units

```quiz
A rectangular storage tank has a volume of 5 cubic yards. What is the volume of the tank, in cubic feet? (1 yard = 3 feet)
answer: 135
- 15 :: That is 5 × 3. Cube the factor: 3 × 3 × 3.
- 45 :: That is 5 × 9. For volume, 1 yd³ = 27 ft³.
> 1 yd³ = 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 ft³. So 5 yd³ = 5 × 27 = 135 ft³.
~ type 5\cdot 3^{3} :: Five cubic yards times 27: 135.
```
