# ratios-rates--density

**Shows:** a combined density is total people over total area, not the average of two densities.

**Storyboard:**
1. Two boxes side by side. Box X: "18,000 people", "60 mi\(^2\)", density 300 per mi\(^2\). Box Y: "30,000 people", "40 mi\(^2\)", density 750 per mi\(^2\). Each box is filled with dots (one dot = 1,000 people): box X is wide with 18 dots, box Y is narrow with 30 dots, so Y looks more crowded.
2. A tempting wrong move appears and fades with a red cross: \(\frac{300 + 750}{2} = 525\). Caption "averages ignore size".
3. The two boxes slide together into one big box: people add to 48,000 and area adds to 100 mi\(^2\) (a counting-up animation).
4. The division appears: \(\frac{48000}{100} = 480\). The result is marked on a number line under the box, between 300 and 750 but closer to 300.
5. The marker for the wrong 525 is shown faded on the same line, to the right of 480.

**Labels & formulas:** "total people", "total area", \(\frac{48000}{100} = 480\), the crossed-out \(\frac{300 + 750}{2} = 525\).

**Stress:** the merge of the two boxes is the main motion; the correct 480 in green, the averaged 525 in red and faded. The number line must stay readable on a phone with only the three numbers 300, 480 and 750 labelled (525 only as a faded tick).
