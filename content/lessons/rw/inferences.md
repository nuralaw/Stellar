## An argument with no ending

The text builds a short argument (background, a finding, often a twist) and stops at a blank. The question is usually **"Which choice most logically completes the text?"** You're writing the argument's last line.

> **Key idea:** The answer is **one small step** past the facts. It doesn't repeat a fact, and it doesn't leap beyond them.

```quiz
Gardeners in one town believed their tomatoes split open because of heavy rain. But this summer was the driest on record, and the tomatoes split just as often as usual. This suggests that ______
Which choice most logically completes the text?
- this summer was the driest on record. :: That repeats a fact. The blank needs the step the facts lead to.
* rain may not be the main cause of the splitting.
- tomatoes grow best in dry summers. :: Nothing says how well the plants grew. That's a new claim.
- rain never affects tomato plants. :: *Never* is far too strong. One dry summer can't prove a rule about all rain and all plants.
> Belief: rain causes the splitting. Fact: the driest summer on record, same splitting. So rain probably isn't the cause. The answer stays careful (*may not*) because the evidence is one summer.
```

## Read the lead-in first

The **lead-in** is the few words just before the blank. It tells you what kind of ending to look for.

| Lead-in | The blank gives… |
|---|---|
| suggests that, so, therefore | the conclusion the facts point to |
| because, since | the reason that links the facts |
| however, but, yet | a turn against what came just before |
| unless, only if | the condition |

Watch for flips: after "it is unlikely that ______", the blank holds an idea the facts argue against.

```quiz
A text ends: "The council warns that the survey may overstate support for the new park, since ______"
What must the blank give?
- A conclusion: most residents want the park :: That's the claim being doubted. *Since* asks for the reason behind the warning.
- A turn: the park is popular after all :: There's no *however* here. *Since* calls for a reason, not a turn.
* A reason the survey's results could look too positive
> *Since* means the blank must explain the warning, for example "only people already at the park site were asked." Know what kind of ending you're hunting for before you read the choices.
```

## List the facts, then predict

1. Give each sentence a job: *belief*, *finding*, *twist*.
2. Find the **turn**, often after *however*, *but* or *yet*. The conclusion lives there.
3. Predict the ending in a few words, starting with "So…". Use every key fact and add nothing.
4. Only then read the choices and match your prediction.

```quiz
Many people believe homing pigeons find their way by following roads. Yet pigeons released over open sea, far from any road, still flew straight home. These findings suggest that ______
Which prediction fits the blank best?
- So pigeons released over the sea flew home. :: That repeats a fact. A prediction says what the facts add up to.
- So pigeons prefer flying over the sea. :: Nothing shows a preference. The sea was just a test with no roads.
* So pigeons can find their way home without roads.
- So roads are no use to pigeons. :: Too strong. Pigeons may still use roads when they're there; they just don't need them.
> Belief: roads guide pigeons. Twist: no roads, still home. So roads can't be their only guide. That's the smallest step that uses both facts.
```

## Know the common patterns

The same few arguments keep coming back.

| The text shows | So the ending says |
|---|---|
| a cause, then evidence against it | something else explains it |
| a rule, then a case that breaks it | the rule doesn't always hold |
| an object with something newer in it | it can't be older than that |
| A leads to B, B leads to C | A affects C |
| one group or place studied | it may not apply elsewhere |

```quiz
Biologists have long treated pale skin in lizards as an adaptation to hot, sunny deserts. However, biologist Ines Moreau found several pale lizard species living in cool, shaded caves, where their color helps them blend in with light rock. Moreau's findings suggest that ______
Which choice most logically completes the text?
- desert lizards are not really adapted to hot, sunny places. :: Too far. Moreau studied cave lizards, so her work says nothing about the desert ones.
- the cave lizards descended from desert lizards. :: Ancestry is never mentioned. It might be true, but nothing in the text forces it.
* pale skin in a lizard is not necessarily a sign that it is adapted to heat.
- blending in matters more to most lizards than staying cool. :: *Most lizards* goes far past a few cave species, and the text never ranks the two needs.
> Pattern: a rule, then a case that breaks it. Rule: pale skin means adapted to heat. Twist: pale lizards in cool caves, pale for a different reason.
> Prediction: *so pale skin doesn't always mean heat.* The right choice says exactly that, and *not necessarily* is the right strength: Moreau showed the rule has exceptions, not that it's never true.
```

## Match the strength

Check every strong word in a choice against the evidence.

| Evidence | Fits words like |
|---|---|
| one study, a pattern | may, likely, suggests, some |
| a fact that rules something out | cannot, must, was not |

Firm words aren't always wrong, and careful words aren't always right. The choice must be exactly as strong as the facts.

```quiz
In a study of 200 office workers, those who took a short walk after lunch reported feeling more alert in the afternoon than those who stayed at their desks. These results suggest that ______
Which choice most logically completes the text?
- walking is the only way to stay alert at work. :: *Only* is far too strong. The study compared walking with sitting, nothing else.
- every worker who walks after lunch will feel more alert. :: *Every* turns a tendency in 200 people into a guarantee for everyone.
- the walkers were already more alert before lunch. :: Possible, but the text never says so. A choice can't add facts.
* a short walk after lunch may help some people feel more alert.
> One study of 200 people supports a careful claim: *may*, *some*. Words like *only* and *every* need far more proof than one comparison can give.
```

## Nothing new, nothing wider

Two more tests for every choice:

- **Nothing new.** If a choice needs a fact the text never gave, cut it.
- **Same scope.** If the text is about one town, species or period, the answer is too. A rule about everything is too broad.

```quiz
Fishers in Port Avel say the bay's crab catch has fallen since a factory opened upstream. Tests show the factory's waste water has warmed the bay by 3°C, and lab studies show that the bay's crab species breeds poorly in water that much warmer. This evidence suggests that ______
Which choice most logically completes the text?
* the factory's warm waste water may be one reason the crab catch has fallen.
- factories harm sea life in bays around the world. :: Too broad. The text covers one factory and one bay.
- the factory's waste also contains chemicals that poison crabs. :: Chemicals are never mentioned. That's new information.
- the fishers have been catching too many crabs. :: Overfishing is never mentioned, and this choice ignores the evidence about heat.
> It's a chain: factory → warmer water → crabs breed poorly → fewer crabs. The answer joins the two ends and stays in Port Avel. *May be one reason* fits, since the text doesn't rule out other causes.
```

## When a firm answer is right

Some facts leave no wiggle room, like a date that rules something out. When the logic is airtight, the firm choice is the right one. It still claims only what the facts force, nothing more.

```quiz
Some historians believed that a hand-drawn map in a town archive dates from 1650. However, the map is drawn entirely in an ink containing a blue pigment that chemists first produced in 1704. If that date is right, it can be concluded that ______
Which choice most logically completes the text?
- the map was made in 1704. :: Too exact. The ink shows only that the map is from 1704 or later. It could be from 1750.
- the pigment was invented earlier than chemists think. :: That denies the condition. The text tells you to accept the 1704 date.
- the map was copied from an older one drawn in 1650. :: It could be true, but nothing in the text says so. It adds a story.
* the map was not made in 1650.
> *It can be concluded* asks for what **must** be true. Ink first made in 1704 can't be on a map drawn in 1650, so a firm answer is right here because the logic is firm. It still adds nothing extra, like an exact date.
```

## The trap: it could be true

> **Trap:** The most tempting wrong answer is a sensible story the text never tells. It *could* be true, and might even be true in real life. But the right answer **must** follow from the text.

Test each choice: which sentences prove it? If you can't point to any, cut it.

```quiz
A city put bright new lights in one park. Over the next year, evening visits to that park doubled, while evening visits to a similar, unlit park nearby stayed the same. Officials concluded that ______
Which choice most logically completes the text?
- people avoided the unlit park because of crime there. :: A natural story, but crime and safety are never mentioned. No sentence proves it.
* the new lights likely led more people to visit the park in the evening.
- the lit park also drew more visitors during the day. :: Only evening visits were counted. Daytime is never measured.
- every park in the city should get bright lights. :: That's a plan, not a conclusion, and it stretches one park to all of them.
> The unlit park is the comparison: its visits didn't change, so a citywide trend can't explain the jump. That points to the lights. The crime story feels right, but it adds facts the text never gives.
```

## Recap

- Read the lead-in: does the blank need a conclusion, a reason, a turn or a condition?
- Give each sentence a job and find the turn.
- Predict a "So…" ending before reading the choices.
- Cut choices that repeat a fact, add a new one or widen the scope.
- Match the strength: careful for one study, firm only when the logic is firm.
- Pick the choice you can prove by pointing at the text.
