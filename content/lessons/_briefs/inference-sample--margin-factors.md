# inference-sample--margin-factors

**Shows:** a larger random sample narrows the margin of error, and more spread in the data widens it.

**Storyboard:**
1. A number line with the true mean marked. A cloud of 10 estimates (small dots) from samples of 25 people, spread wide. A bracket under them: "wide margin".
2. The sample size changes to 100: the same number of dots now sits in a tighter cluster. The bracket shrinks. Label "bigger sample, smaller margin".
3. New row: the individual data points (grey dots) of one sample are tightly packed, with a short bracket for the margin.
4. The data points spread out (larger standard deviation, same sample size). The bracket for the margin grows. Label "more spread, larger margin".
5. Last frame: two small cards "bigger random sample: margin down" and "more spread: margin up".

**Labels & formulas:** "sample of 25", "sample of 100", "standard deviation", "margin of error".

**Stress:** the bracket (margin) in a strong colour that visibly shortens and lengthens; the dots calm. Keep it readable at 320 px wide.
