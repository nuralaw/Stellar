# boundaries--dependent

**Shows:** a dependent clause (because, although, which, that) cannot stand alone; it hooks onto a main clause with a comma, with no mark, or with a comma tail, and never with a period or semicolon.

**Storyboard:**
1. A main-clause block "the bus took the coast road" (green, solid). A dependent block "Because the pass was closed" (orange, with a loose hook) floats in from the left; it is labelled "dependent: cannot stand alone".
2. The hook connects to the main block with a comma between them: "opener, main".
3. The dependent block jumps to the right side: "the bus took the coast road because the pass was closed" with no comma, labelled "essential tail: no mark".
4. A "which" tail attaches with a comma: ", which surprised everyone", labelled "extra tail: comma".
5. A period and a semicolon try to sit between the blocks and bounce off with red crosses: "leaves a fragment".

**Labels & formulas:** "main clause", "dependent clause", "because / although / which / that", "opener, main", "main + essential: no comma", "main, which ...". No math formulas.

**Stress:** green solid block for the main clause, orange hooked block for the dependent one; the hook visibly snaps on. Red crosses for period/semicolon. Phone: stack blocks vertically; the joining word (because, which) is bold and a bit larger.
