# two-var-data--line-graph

**Shows:** the average rate of change between two points of a line graph is the slope of the segment that joins them, and the steepest segment has the greatest rate.

**Storyboard:**
1. A line graph on a grid: x is "year" (2010, 2012, 2014, 2016), y is "population (thousands)". Four dots at (2010, 40), (2012, 46), (2014, 48), (2016, 60) are joined in order, drawn one segment at a time.
2. The 2014 to 2016 segment thickens in the accent colour: it is the steepest. Beside it a small rise-and-run triangle shows "rise 12, run 2".
3. A wide bracket spans 2012 to 2016 with a straight dashed chord between those two dots (the curve of the graph is faded beneath it). The triangle under the chord shows "rise 14, run 4".
4. The formula \(\frac{60 - 46}{2016 - 2012} = 3.5\) types in beside the chord and the label "3.5 thousand per year" lands on the dashed chord.
5. A closing caption "steep segment = big rate, high point = big value" with the highest dot (2016) and the steepest segment marked in two different colours.

**Labels & formulas:** axis names "year" and "population (thousands)", \(\frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\), \(\frac{60 - 46}{2016 - 2012} = 3.5\), "3.5 thousand per year".

**Stress:** the straight dashed chord is the key mark (it is a slope even though the graph bends); the steepest solid segment in one accent colour, the highest dot in another. At most four labels at a time so it reads on a phone.
