# nonlinear-functions--axis

**Shows:** a parabola is a mirror image about its axis, so the vertex is at \(x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}\), halfway between any two points with the same height.

**Storyboard:**
1. Parabola \(f(x) = -2x^2 + 8x + 3\) on a grid, opening down. The vertical axis line is drawn dashed through its top.
2. A horizontal dashed line cuts the parabola at two points. Their \(x\)-values (0 and 4) glow; a midpoint marker lands at \(x = 2\) on the axis: "\(\dfrac{0 + 4}{2} = 2\)".
3. The formula \(x = -\dfrac{b}{2a} = -\dfrac{8}{2(-2)} = 2\) appears next to the axis and agrees with the midpoint.
4. The vertex dot at \((2, 11)\) lights up with two labelled readouts: "where: \(x = 2\)" (on the axis) and "how high: \(f(2) = 11\)" (on the \(y\)-axis side).
5. A second horizontal line at the top touches once: "touches once only at the vertex".

**Labels & formulas:** \(f(x) = -2x^2 + 8x + 3\), \(x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}\), "axis of symmetry", "same height, mirror points", "where" and "how high".

**Stress:** the axis and the midpoint marker are in the accent colour; the two mirror points are the same colour as each other. "Where" and "how high" use different colours so the two jobs are not confused. Keep the labels at least 12 px.
