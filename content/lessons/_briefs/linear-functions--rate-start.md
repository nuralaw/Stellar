# linear-functions--rate-start

**Shows:** in a story, \(b\) is the amount at time 0 and \(m\) is the change per ONE unit; a rate given per several units must be divided down first.

**Storyboard:**
1. A battery bar at 100% labelled "start \(b = 100\)", time axis "minutes".
2. After 6 minutes the bar is at 64%. A bracket shows "\(-36\) in 6 min".
3. The bracket splits into six equal steps of \(-6\), each labelled "per 1 minute". Formula: \(m = \frac{-36}{6} = -6\).
4. The rule assembles: \(B(t) = 100 - 6t\). Beneath it, a faded "per 6 minutes = \(-36\)" with an arrow to "per 1 minute = \(-6\)".

**Labels & formulas:** "start (\(t = 0\))", "rate per 1 minute", \(m = \frac{-36}{6} = -6\), \(B(t) = 100 - 6t\).

**Stress:** the start value and the per-1 rate in two different accent tones; the total drop \(-36\) is neutral (it is NOT the rate). Make the six small steps clearly equal and readable on a phone.
