# probability--counts

**Shows:** a probability times the group size gives a count, and rows must be turned into items before you divide.

**Storyboard:**
1. A bag of 60 dots with a bar showing "blue: 0.35". The bar is split into 100 small ticks, 35 coloured.
2. An arrow from the bar to the bag: \(0.35 \cdot 60 = 21\). 21 dots light up.
3. New scene: 4 buckets of roses and 6 buckets of tulips, each bucket labelled with its flowers (5 and 12).
4. The buckets "pour out" into two piles: roses 20, tulips 72. The labels read \(4 \cdot 5 = 20\) and \(6 \cdot 12 = 72\).
5. The fraction \(\frac{20}{92}\) builds over the piles, with a crossed-out \(\frac{4}{10}\) (buckets) next to it: "pick a flower, not a bucket".

**Labels & formulas:** \(\text{count} = P \cdot \text{total}\); \(0.35 \cdot 60 = 21\); \(\frac{20}{92} = \frac{5}{23}\).

**Stress:** the multiplication arrow and the piles; the crossed-out bucket fraction in a muted red. Large type for the three key numbers.
