# equivalent-expressions--match

**Shows:** if two expressions are equal for every \(x\), each power of \(x\) must have the same coefficient on both sides, so you can read the unknown constants straight off.

**Storyboard:**
1. Equation \((3x + 4)(x + k) = 3x^2 + bx + 20\) with the words "true for all \(x\)" in the accent.
2. The left side expands in two rows: \(3x^2 + 3kx + 4x + 4k\); like terms are grouped into three columns: \(x^2\), \(x\), number.
3. Three columns are drawn under both sides. The number column lights first: \(4k\) on the left and \(20\) on the right with an equals sign: \(k = 5\).
4. The \(x\) column: \((3k + 4)x\) beside \(bx\); \(k = 5\) drops in and gives \(b = 19\).
5. Final frame: \((3x + 4)(x + 5) = 3x^2 + 19x + 20\); each of the three coefficients has a green tick.

**Labels & formulas:** \((3x + 4)(x + k) = 3x^2 + bx + 20\), "\(x^2\)", "\(x\)", "number", \(4k = 20\), \(b = 3k + 4\), \(b = 19\).

**Stress:** the three columns each have their own colour and the matching terms on the two sides share that colour. The column holding only one unknown goes first. Keep the formulas at least 14 px and the three columns apart so a phone screen reads them in order.
