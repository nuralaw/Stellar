## Two sections, four modules

The SAT has two sections: Reading and Writing (R&W) first, then Math. Each section is split into two **modules**, blocks of questions with their own timer.

| Part | Questions | Time |
|---|---|---|
| R&W module 1 | 27 | 32 min |
| R&W module 2 | 27 | 32 min |
| Break | none | 10 min |
| Math module 1 | 22 | 35 min |
| Math module 2 | 22 | 35 min |

You take it in College Board's **Bluebook** app on a laptop or tablet, not a phone: 98 questions in 2 hours 14 minutes, plus the break.

```quiz
Your friend plans to give every R&W question about 90 seconds. What happens in a 32-minute module?
- She finishes with a few minutes to spare :: 27 × 90 seconds is 40.5 minutes, far more than the 32 she has.
* She runs out of time after about 21 questions :: Right. 32 minutes is 1,920 seconds, and 1,920 ÷ 90 ≈ 21.
- She runs out after about 10 questions :: That would mean over 3 minutes per question. At 90 seconds she gets twice as far.
> The R&W average is about 71 seconds per question (32 minutes ÷ 27). Math gives more: about 95 seconds (35 minutes ÷ 22). The pacing lesson shows how to split that time.
```

## Each module has its own clock

- **Inside a module** you can move freely: skip ahead, go back, change answers.
- **At 0:00**, Bluebook saves your answers and moves you on by itself.
- **A finished module is locked.** You can never go back to it.
- **Time doesn't carry over.** Minutes left in one module are lost, and with standard time you can't move on early.

```quiz
The R&W module 1 timer hits 0:00 while you're reading question 25. Questions 25 to 27 have no answer yet. What happens to them?
- You get a short grace period to finish :: There's no extra time. At 0:00 the module ends.
- You can answer them after module 2 :: A finished module is locked for good.
* They stay blank and earn nothing :: Right. That's why every question needs at least a guess before the clock runs out.
> Bluebook keeps what you entered and opens module 2. Anything still blank stays blank forever.
```

## Module 1 decides module 2

The test is **adaptive**, but it adapts only once per section. Module 1 is a broad mix of easy, medium and hard questions. Based on how you do, Bluebook gives you a **harder** or an **easier** module 2. Both still mix difficulties.

- Roughly half of test takers get the harder one.
- Only the harder one opens the top scores; the easier one caps your section score. College Board doesn't publish the cap, so numbers you see online are guesses.
- Bluebook never tells you which module 2 you got.

```quiz
A classmate's plan: "I'll miss a few on purpose in module 1, so module 2 is easier and I get more right." Good idea?
- Yes, more right answers means a higher score :: The score also depends on which questions you got right, and the easier path caps how high you can go.
* No, it lowers the score :: Right. College Board's own simulations found that this trick leads to lower scores.
- Only in Math, where module 2 is harder :: Routing works the same way in both sections, and the trick fails in both.
> Give module 1 your most careful work. A careless miss there costs the point and can also close off the harder module 2.
> If module 2 feels hard, that's often a good sign: you get the harder module 2 only when module 1 went well.
```

## Some questions don't count

Each module hides **2 unscored questions**. College Board is trying them out for future tests. That leaves 25 scored questions in each R&W module and 20 in each Math module.

They look exactly like the others, and nothing tells you which ones they are. So treat every question as if it counts.

```quiz
Question 9 looks odd, unlike anything you've practiced. You suspect it's an unscored tryout question. What do you do?
- Leave it blank, since it probably doesn't count :: You can't know that. If it is scored, a blank is a sure zero.
- Spend extra time on it, since it's unusual :: Unusual doesn't mean worth more. It gets the same time limit as any other question.
* Treat it like any other question :: Right. Give it your normal time, and guess, flag and move on if you get stuck.
> Only 2 questions in a module are unscored, so an odd-looking question is most likely a scored one. Trying to spot the tryouts only wastes time.
```

## Reading and Writing: a fixed order

Each R&W question has a short passage (sometimes two texts, a table or a graph) and four choices. The question types always come in this order:

| # | Group | You'll see |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Craft and Structure | word choice, purpose, Text 1 and 2 |
| 2 | Information and Ideas | main idea, evidence, graphs |
| 3 | Standard English Conventions | grammar, punctuation |
| 4 | Expression of Ideas | transitions, student notes |

Questions of one type sit together and go from easier to harder. Then the next type **starts easier again**. The grammar group runs easier to harder as a whole.

```quiz
You're on question 6, a very hard vocabulary question. You see that question 7 starts a new type: the purpose of a text. What should you expect?
* Question 7 is probably easier, so don't let question 6 eat its time :: Right. Difficulty resets when a new question type begins.
- Question 7 is even harder, because the module gets harder as it goes :: That's how Math works. In R&W, each new type starts easier again.
- Both are about the same, because R&W questions are all one level :: Within each type, questions get harder as you go.
> In R&W, the hardest question of one type often sits right before the easiest question of the next. Stuck on the last question of a type? Guess, flag it and collect the easier points.
```

## Math: easier to harder

- Algebra, Advanced Math, Problem-Solving and Data Analysis, and Geometry and Trigonometry come **mixed together**.
- The whole module runs from **easier to harder**, so the last questions are the toughest.
- About 75% are multiple choice with four choices. The rest are **student-produced response** (SPR): you type your own answer.
- A calculator is allowed on every Math question: the built-in Desmos or your own approved one. A **reference sheet** of formulas is always one click away.
- Figures are **drawn to scale** unless the question says otherwise.

```quiz
A geometry question shows a triangle with no "not drawn to scale" note. Angle B clearly looks smaller than a right angle. You're stuck. Can the picture help you guess?
* Yes: rule out choices of 90° or more for angle B :: Right. Unless the question says otherwise, figures are drawn to scale, so the picture is evidence.
- No: SAT figures are never reliable :: That's only true when the question says "not drawn to scale".
- Only on the easy questions at the start :: The scale rule holds for every figure, early or late, unless the question says otherwise.
> Use a figure to estimate and to cross out impossible choices, not to replace the math. If a question does say "not drawn to scale", don't trust the picture.
```

## Typing your own answer

On SPR questions you type the answer into a box. The rules:

- Up to **5 characters** for a positive answer, **6** for a negative one. The minus sign, the decimal point and the fraction bar all count.
- No mixed numbers: 3½ becomes `7/2` or `3.5`.
- No symbols: no %, $ or commas.
- If more than one answer works, enter just one.

Bluebook shows a **preview** of how it reads your entry. Check it every time.

```quiz
You get 4¼. Which entry earns credit?
- `4 1/4` :: Mixed numbers aren't allowed. Change 4¼ to an improper fraction or a decimal.
- `41/4` :: Bluebook reads this as 41 over 4, which is 10.25.
* `17/4` :: Right. 4¼ = 16/4 + 1/4 = 17/4. The decimal `4.25` works too.
- `$4.25` :: Symbols like $ aren't allowed, even for money. Type just the number.
> To change a mixed number into a fraction: whole number × bottom + top, all over the bottom. Then glance at the preview to see that Bluebook read it the way you meant.
```

## Long decimals fill the box

When a fraction fits, typing the fraction is the safest choice. If it doesn't fit, or you use a decimal that goes on and on, **fill every space**: cut it off or round it at the fourth digit. A shorter decimal is marked wrong.

| Answer | Accepted | Marked wrong |
|---|---|---|
| 5/9 | `5/9`, `.5555`, `.5556`, `0.556` | `0.55`, `0.56` |
| −2/7 | `-2/7`, `-.2857`, `-0.285` | `-.28`, `-0.29` |

```quiz
Your answer is −7/9, which is −0.7777… You decide to type a decimal. Which entry earns credit?
- `-0.78` :: Too short. A long negative decimal must fill all 6 characters.
- `-.78` :: Too short. Only 4 characters, so it isn't accurate enough.
* `-.7778` :: Right. Minus, point and four digits make 6 characters, rounded at the fourth digit.
- `-0.7778` :: That's 7 characters. A negative answer gets at most 6, so it won't fit.
> `-0.778` and `-.7777` would also be accepted. Easiest of all: the fraction `-7/9` fits in 4 characters.
```

## Bluebook's tools

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| Timer | Counts down; you can hide it until 5 minutes remain |
| Mark for Review | Flags a question so you can find it later |
| Question menu | Shows answered, blank and flagged questions; jumps to any |
| Option Eliminator | Crosses out choices you've ruled out |
| Highlights & Notes | Marks passage text (R&W only) |
| Calculator | Desmos, graphing or scientific (Math only) |
| Reference sheet | Common Math formulas |

After the last question you reach a **Check Your Work** page that works like the question menu. Try every tool in a practice test first.

```quiz
Two minutes left. You want to know whether any question in the module is still blank. What's the fastest way?
- Click Back through every question :: That can take a minute or more. The menu shows every question at once.
* Open the question menu :: Right. It marks each question as answered, blank or flagged, and jumps straight to any of them.
- Flag every question you're unsure of :: Flagging marks questions for later. It doesn't show you the blanks.
> The Check Your Work page at the end of a module gives the same overview. Use either one in your final minutes.
```

## Scoring: no penalty for guessing

- Each section is scored from **200 to 800**. Your total is **400 to 1600**.
- A wrong answer and a blank both earn **zero**. Nothing is taken away for a wrong answer.
- Your score isn't a plain count of right answers. College Board uses *item response theory*: it looks at **which** questions you got right, including how hard they were. Two students with the same number right can get different scores.

```quiz
A friend says: "If you're not sure, leave it blank. Wrong answers lower your score." What's true?
- She's right: wrong answers subtract points :: That rule belongs to an old version of the SAT. Today a wrong answer costs nothing.
- She's right, but only for hard questions :: No question has a penalty for a wrong answer, easy or hard.
* She's wrong: always enter an answer :: Right. A blank is always zero, and a guess might be right.
> College Board itself says it's better to guess than to leave a question blank, especially after you rule out one or two choices.
```

## Get ready in Bluebook

- **Weeks before:** install Bluebook on your test device and take a full practice test in it. Afterward, My Practice shows your score, the correct answers and explanations.
- **1 to 5 days before:** complete **exam setup** in Bluebook. This creates your admission ticket.
- **Test day:** bring the charged device, your admission ticket, a photo ID, and pencils or pens. Scratch paper is handed out. Your phone stays put away, even during the break.

```quiz
Your SAT is on Saturday, and today is Wednesday. You installed Bluebook last month. What still needs doing?
- Nothing: installing Bluebook is enough :: Installing isn't the same as exam setup, and exam setup is what creates your admission ticket.
* Exam setup in Bluebook :: Right. It's done 1 to 5 days before the test and creates your admission ticket.
- Exam setup at the test center on Saturday :: Exam setup happens before test day. It creates the admission ticket you bring with you.
> Do exam setup early in the 1-to-5-day window, so there's time to fix any problem before Saturday.
```

## Recap

- R&W: two modules of 27 questions in 32 minutes. Math: two modules of 22 in 35.
- Each module has its own clock and locks for good at 0:00.
- Module 1 decides whether module 2 is harder or easier, so give it your most careful work.
- R&W goes type by type, each easier to harder; Math gets harder across the module.
- Typed answers: 5 characters (6 with a minus), no mixed numbers, no symbols, long decimals fill the box.
- No penalty for wrong answers, so never leave a blank.
