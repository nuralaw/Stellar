# inferences--not-stronger

**Shows:** a conclusion can never be stronger than the evidence: compare each choice's strength with the facts, not with a hedge word.

**Storyboard:**
1. A bar labelled "evidence" shows one small trial (a short bar with "20 volunteers, one week").
2. Three choice cards each get a bar of their own on the same scale: "may help some runners" (short, fits under the evidence bar), "helps every runner" (very tall, pokes above), "has no effect" (negative, points opposite).
3. The tall bar bounces off a ceiling line at the evidence height and is stamped "too strong"; the negative bar is stamped "against the facts".
4. The short bar sits exactly under the line and turns green: "matches the evidence".
5. A second pair: evidence "the text states the fact outright" -> a firm statement with no hedge also fits (green). Caption: "Match the facts, not the hedge word."

**Labels & formulas:** "evidence", "too strong", "against the facts", "matches", "may / likely / is: match to the facts". No formulas.

**Stress:** the ceiling line at the evidence height, and bars hitting it. Use distinct colours for too strong (red), against (orange), match (green). Readable on a phone.
