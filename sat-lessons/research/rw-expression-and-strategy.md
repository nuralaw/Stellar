Research notes for four lessons: Rhetorical Synthesis, Transitions, How the Digital SAT Works, and Pacing and Guessing. Research was done in November 2026.

How the research was done, and its limits:

- **Official sources first.** The College Board facts come from pages and PDFs opened and read in full: the Assessment Framework (2026 edition, created September 2026), the 2026-27 SAT Weekend Student Guide, the Fall 2026 Bluebook test directions, and the live College Board help pages.
- **Reddit could not be reached.** The fetch tool and the search tool both refuse reddit.com, and the old.reddit.com JSON address returned a login wall. For student voices we used College Confidential threads and Test Ninjas' "Exam Feedback" pages, which sum up what students reported after each 2026 test date.
- **YouTube transcripts could not be reached.** The caption files need a player token. Videos are credited only for what their titles, descriptions and chapter lists say.
- **Khan Academy's course pages** load only with JavaScript and could not be read. We used College Board's page about the Khan Academy course instead.
- **The shared web-search budget ran out** partway through. After that, sources were found through site maps, links inside pages, and the related-video lists on YouTube pages.
- **Blind check of the examples.** A separate agent solved all 16 worked examples without seeing the answers. Every item had one clear best answer except one pacing scenario, which was rewritten. It also caught a factual slip (wild crows shape twig hooks; they don't bend them), titles that hinted at answers, and wording a non-native reader could misread. All of these were fixed.

## Rhetorical Synthesis

### Best sources

- [Assessment Framework for the Digital SAT Suite (College Board, PDF)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/assessment-framework-for-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — The official definition: blend two or more facts from bullet notes into one sentence that meets a stated goal, such as supporting a generalization or emphasizing a contrast. The right sentence must include only relevant information and must achieve the goal; all choices are grammatical. The sample's wrong-answer explanations show three failure types: a wrong relationship word (*however* for a similarity goal), a sentence that is too general, and facts listed with no relationship. Expression of Ideas is 8 to 12 scored questions per test.
- [The SAT Reading and Writing Section (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/whats-on-the-test/reading-writing) — Questions are grouped by type and ordered from easiest to hardest.
- [Mastering Rhetorical Synthesis Questions (Test Ninjas)](https://www.test-ninjas.com/sat-rhetorical-synthesis) — A table of goal types (similarity, difference, summary, example, sequence or cause) and the main traps: all choices true, right topic but wrong goal, half-right, outside information. Advises skipping the notes on the first pass.
- [SAT Rhetorical Synthesis Strategy (PrepMaven)](https://prepmaven.com/blog/test-prep/sat-rhetorical-synthesis-strategy/) — A four-step method: goal verb, find the choice that fully meets it, drop partial choices, use the bullets only to confirm or break ties. Clear audience rule (familiar audience: no definitions; introducing: define).
- [How to Answer Student Notes Questions on the Digital SAT (The Critical Reader)](https://thecriticalreader.com/answer-student-notes-questions-digital-sat/) — Erica Meltzer's "ignore the notes and match the key words" approach; most questions take seconds.
- [Rhetorical Synthesis: Goal Verbs and Note Selection (Open Exam Prep)](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sat-reading-writing/expression-of-ideas/rhetorical-synthesis) — A four-filter check (on goal, complete, supported, accurate) and accuracy traps such as hidden quantifiers (all, most, only).
- [Expression of Ideas: Rhetorical Synthesis (Achievable)](https://app.achievable.me/study/sat/learn/expression-ideas-rhetorical-synthesis) — Less common goal types, such as "describe a debate" (two viewpoints) and "emphasize scale".
- [March 2026 Digital SAT Synthesis Questions (The Test Advantage)](https://thetestadvantage.com/blog/march-2026-digital-sat-synthesis-questions-on-digital-exams) — "Perfect match" rule: include every fact the goal asks for and nothing else; omission and extra-information traps.
- [Exam Feedback, May 2, 2026 (Test Ninjas)](https://test-ninjas.com/exam-feedback-2026-05-02) — Students reported that all choices seemed to use the notes and that the closest match to the goal decided the answer.
- [SAT English Hacks: Rhetorical Synthesis (Penguin SAT Prep, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G1QkmrTR1i0) — The most-watched video found for this skill (about 29,000 views); chapters show a 3-step strategy and practice.
- [Rhetorical Synthesis Questions on the Digital SAT (Ivy League Mentors, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lo7LFbac_9o) — Description: three simple steps; calls these "research note" questions and among the easiest on the test.
- [November SAT is CHANGING: New Rhetorical Synthesis Questions (SAT 1600 with Farrock, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r1CGTjCb_sQ) — September 2026. Description: newer, trickier formats, and skipping the notes no longer always works.
- [Accuracy Matters (3-4-5 Tutoring, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WvUsb00iyl4) — Description: most notes questions can be answered without the bullets, but some choices sound right for the goal and are inaccurate.
- [NEW Strategy for Student Notes Section (Jelly SAT Prep, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t9igiZEMhjA) — November 2026. Description: goals with several requirements, familiar and unfamiliar audiences, choices that match the goal but contain wrong information.
- [6 Key Tips for "Student Goal" Questions (Anna's Universe, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=96ae8HlE878) — Description: these are the last questions of each module; a worked "unfamiliar audience" example (the right answer must both describe the thing and present the research).
- [Just Count the Bullets! (SexyJ, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zc-lcnNuyZE) and [SKIP the Notes! (SexyJ, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c-Y5Kr2ezzk) — The strongest "skip the notes" position; for generalization goals, says the choice that uses the most bullets is usually right.
- [Tips for Digital SAT prep (College Confidential)](https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/tips-for-digital-sat-prep/3657544) — A tutor's thread: notes questions are answered fastest by focusing on the key words of the question.

### What the best teachers agree on

- Read the goal first. The goal is the whole task; the notes are raw material.
- All four choices are usually true and grammatical, so "is it true?" removes nothing. "Does it do the job?" is the only useful test.
- The main trap is the right topic with the wrong job (for example, the finding instead of the method). Next comes the half-done goal.
- A comparison needs both items and the right relationship word (*both, like* versus *whereas, unlike*).
- Audience clues decide some questions: an unfamiliar audience needs to be told what the thing is.
- A generalization is broad; an example is one specific case. Choices that confuse the two are classic traps.
- These questions sit at the end of every module and are among the quickest points.

### Disagreements and choices made

- **Skip the notes, or check them?** Meltzer, PrepMaven, Test Ninjas, SexyJ and UKhan's channel say to skip them. 3-4-5 Tutoring, Farrock (2026), Jelly SAT Prep (2026) and Open Exam Prep warn about choices that meet the goal but misstate the notes. Choice: goal first, skip the notes on the first pass, then always do a 10-second accuracy check on the surviving choice and read the notes to break ties. Reason: the newest reports (2025–2026) describe exactly this kind of trap, and the check is cheap.
- **How many per module?** Meltzer estimated 4 to 5 notes questions and about 5 transitions per module; other guides (seen only in search-result summaries, not opened) say 2 to 4 per module or 4 to 5 per test. The official specification is 8 to 12 scored Expression of Ideas questions per test for both skills together (about 4 to 6 per module, plus any unscored items). The lesson gives only the official figure.
- **Count the bullets, or more is not better?** SexyJ says the choice using the most bullets usually wins generalization questions; The Test Advantage and Achievable warn that extra facts are a trap. Choice: we teach "more is not better", because College Board says the right answer includes only relevant information.
- **Emphasis by sentence structure** (putting the key detail in the main clause) appears in some tutoring advice, but we found too little support to teach it as a rule, so it was left out.
- **Example content.** All examples are original. We used well-established real facts (axolotl and zebrafish regeneration, tardigrades, Mendel's pea plants, animal tool use, radiocarbon and tree-ring dating) and avoided precise numbers we could not verify.

## Transitions

### Best sources

- [Assessment Framework for the Digital SAT Suite (College Board, PDF)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/assessment-framework-for-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — Skill definition, and a sample whose blank sits inside a sentence after an opening phrase yet still links back to the previous sentence. Each wrong choice is explained by the relationship it "illogically signals" (stronger version, consequence, or just more information).
- [The SAT Reading and Writing Section (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/whats-on-the-test/reading-writing) — Grouping and order of question types.
- [How to Work Through Transition Questions on the Digital SAT (The Critical Reader)](https://thecriticalreader.com/how-to-work-through-transition-questions-on-the-digital-sat/) — Read the whole short passage, decide the relationship before looking, and learn transitions by category rather than fine nuance. Notes that *still* at the start of a sentence means "despite this" and is a favorite right answer.
- [The Trickiest SAT/ACT Transition Questions (The Critical Reader)](https://thecriticalreader.com/the-trickiest-satact-transition-questions/) — Paper-test era, but the punctuation point still holds: a transition set off by commas in the middle of a sentence links to the previous sentence.
- [SAT Transitions (Test Ninjas)](https://test-ninjas.com/sat-transitions) — A Name, Match, Check routine; a decision order for hard items (contrast and concession first, plain addition last); look-alike pairs; traps such as the nearest-sentence trap, a contrast inside the sentence, and the "moreover default".
- [SAT Transition Questions: Words and Phrases (UWorld)](https://collegeprep.uworld.com/blog/sat-transition-questions-words-and-phrases/) — Phrase-length transitions; the "sounds smooth" trap.
- [Transition Questions on SAT Writing (PrepScholar)](https://blog.prepscholar.com/transition-questions-on-sat-writing) — The "and, but, so" test and the synonym rule; partly written for the older test format.
- [Transition Words on the SAT and Digital SAT (3-4-5 Tutoring)](https://345tutoring.wordpress.com/2023/04/22/transition-words-on-the-sat-and-digital-sat/) — A categorized master list with counts of how often each word appears as a choice; the most frequent include *however*, *for example*, *nevertheless*, *similarly*, *likewise* and *therefore*.
- [James Lu's transition statistics (Google Sheets)](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1XKfW5CENAchCbwtLUC9dWR3ry_mWMme-LmDA7bvHWac) — For each category: its share of all answer choices and how often a choice in it is correct. Contrast ("subverting expectations") is the most common category (about 27% of choices, correct about 23% of the time); restatement is correct only about 2% of the time; confirmation (*in fact, indeed*) about 57%.
- [Exam Feedback, May 2, 2026](https://test-ninjas.com/exam-feedback-2026-05-02) and [March 14, 2026 (Test Ninjas)](https://test-ninjas.com/exam-feedback-2026-03-14) — Students reported transition choices that were whole phrases of five to ten words, and wordy module 2 transition questions that needed close reading.
- [Never Miss Another SAT Transition Question (James Lu SAT, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BhDFdrPA368) — About 131,000 views (April 2026). His description summarizes the method: misses come from misreading or not knowing the categories; eight categories; predict the category first; two choices in the same category are almost never right.
- [All SAT Transition Questions Explained (Jelly SAT Prep, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2U3VFDk_IE) — November 2026; chapters walk through each category from easiest to hardest, plus an elimination trick credited to James Lu.
- [Transitions Questions on the Digital SAT (Ivy League Mentors, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kz_P_b3H0oA) and [8 Hard SAT Transitions Questions Explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VaTPlXQ5xzs) — Transitions join two sentences or two parts of one sentence; hard items depend on context clues.
- [Transition-Word Questions (3-4-5 Tutoring, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=92GaKBgEG7E) and ["Therefore" vs "Indeed" vs "Granted"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2jtxuKRc7zY) — A "big six" set of categories that covers most right answers; telling cause, emphasis and concession apart.
- [Transition Word Questions Explained by a 1600 Scorer (Anna's Universe, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=crjUGORmFnw) — Category-based review with a shared transition spreadsheet.

### What the best teachers agree on

- Predict the relationship before reading the choices.
- Learn transitions by family; fine differences inside a family (*nevertheless* versus *nonetheless*) are almost never tested.
- Two choices with the same meaning are both wrong.
- Decide by logic, never by what "sounds smooth".
- Restatement words are rarely right; *in fact* and *indeed* are often right when they appear.
- Concession is the family students know least: *granted* and *admittedly* admit a point, *still* and *nevertheless* hold the main claim despite it.

### Disagreements and choices made

- **How much to read.** Meltzer says read the whole passage; James Lu, UWorld and Test Ninjas focus on the sentence before the blank and the sentence with it. Choice: read the whole short text once, then decide using the two ideas around the blank, and teach the "wrong sentence" trap with an example where the sentence before already has its own transition. Reading everything costs about ten seconds.
- **How many categories.** Meltzer uses three (continue, cause, contrast), James Lu eight, Test Ninjas ten. Choice: a table of eleven rows (concession split in two), because concession, emphasis, example and restatement are where the trap pairs live; Meltzer's three-way idea survives as the quick "and, but, so" prediction.
- **The twins rule.** James Lu treats same-category pairs as a strong signal; Meltzer says they are rarer on the digital SAT than on the ACT. Choice: teach it as a cross-check (step 6), not as the first move.
- **Frequency statistics.** James Lu's and 3-4-5 Tutoring's counts are useful but come from limited samples and can mislead if used as a strategy. Mentioned only as a tie-breaker.
- **Phrase-length transitions.** These are new in 2026 student reports and are not yet covered by older guides. We added a section and a hard example that checks the details of each phrase.

## How the Digital SAT Works

### Best sources

- [How the SAT Is Structured (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/whats-on-the-test/structure) — 64 and 70 minutes, 54 and 44 questions, 98 in total, 134 minutes, a 10-minute break, and module 1 as a broad mix of difficulties.
- [How Are Scores Calculated? (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/scores/understanding-scores/how-scores-are-calculated) — 20 to 25 scored questions plus 2 pretest questions per module, free movement inside a module, IRT scoring, "better to guess than leave a question blank", the 400–1600 scale and the 480 and 530 benchmarks.
- [Assessment Framework for the Digital SAT Suite (College Board, PDF)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/assessment-framework-for-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — The module question sequence (Table 9) and ordering rules, 1.19 and 1.59 minutes per question, domain counts, the universal tools, the routing cut point near the median (about half routed up), the simulation showing that gaming module 1 lowers scores, the guessing sidebar ("not the same as a guessing penalty"), the multiple-choice and SPR split, and the fact that the Essay is not offered on weekend tests.
- [SAT School Day Bluebook Test Directions, Fall 2026 (College Board, PDF)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/english-sat-test-directions-bb.pdf) — 27 and 22 questions per module, no return to a finished module, the full SPR entry rules with examples, figures drawn to scale, and unscheduled breaks costing time.
- [2026-27 SAT Weekend Student Guide (College Board, PDF)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/sat-student-guide.pdf) — Tools, devices, connection and crash handling, the scoring table, score sends, Score Choice (with the "most recent six administrations" default) and cancellation.
- [Bluebook Testing Tools (College Board)](https://bluebook.collegeboard.org/students/tools) — The official tool list and the timer rule (hide until 5 minutes remain).
- [Can I finish the test early? (Bluebook Help Center)](https://bluebook.collegeboard.org/help-center/can-i-finish-test-early) and [the extended-time FAQ (College Board Accommodations)](https://accommodations.collegeboard.org/help-center/how-will-new-extended-time-policy-allowing-student-move-when-ready-work-sat-and-ap) — Standard-time students can't leave a module early; extended-time students can after the standard time; the end-of-module screen is called Check Your Work.
- [SAT Suite Calculator Policy (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/in-school-assessments/calculator-policy) — CAS calculators banned, Desmos graphing or scientific, no calculator during R&W.
- [SAT Suite Score Release Dates (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/scores/score-release-dates) — 2026-27 dates and "most weekend scores are released 2–4 weeks after test day".
- [What to Expect on Test Day (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/what-to-bring-do/what-to-expect) and [What to Bring (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/what-to-bring-do/what-to-bring) — Sample schedule, breaks, scratch paper, and that a watch without an alarm is allowed.
- [Full-Length Digital Practice Tests on Bluebook (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice/practice-tests/bluebook) — Practice tests let you move on early (the real test doesn't); scores and explanations appear in My Practice.
- [What's an SAT Superscore? (College Board blog)](https://blog.collegeboard.org/what-is-an-sat-superscore) and [Score Choice (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/scores/sending-sat-scores/additional/score-choice) — Superscoring is each college's policy; Score Choice works by test date and not for the free sends.
- [Digital SAT Structure (Princeton Review)](https://www.princetonreview.com/college-advice/digital-sat-structure) — Screen layout, the fixed order of R&W question types, and a "floor and cap" view of the two module 2 paths.
- [The Complete Guide to the Digital Adaptive SAT (Applerouth)](https://www.applerouth.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-the-digital-adaptive-sat) — Their own estimate of score ranges for each path; tells students not to worry about which path they are on.
- [Digital SAT guide (Compass)](https://www.compassprep.com/digital-sat/) and [SAT Adaptive Testing (PrepScholar)](https://blog.prepscholar.com/sat-adaptive-testing) — Stage-adaptive design explained for families; both are partly dated.
- [EVERYTHING You Need to Know about the Digital SAT (LearnSATMath, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=06Y6OtRj0n8) — About 317,000 views; its description has no details, so it was not used for any fact.

### What the sources agree on

- Two sections, two modules each, separately timed; R&W first, then a 10-minute break, then Math.
- The test adapts once per section, after module 1; there is no going back to a finished module.
- No penalty for wrong answers; IRT scoring means the score is not a simple count of right answers.
- Bluebook tools: timer, mark for review, option eliminator, highlights and notes (R&W only), Desmos, reference sheet.

### Disagreements and choices made

- **Score limits on each path.** Princeton Review describes a cap on the easier path and a floor on the harder one; Applerouth estimates the easier path tops out near 620–630 per section, Test Ninjas says about 600, and a College Confidential parent quotes a routing threshold of about 600. College Board publishes no number; the framework only says that many middle scores are possible on either path and that gaming module 1 sharply limits the highest possible score. Choice: the lesson says a limit exists and gives no number.
- **Timer.** Compass says the timer stays hidden until the last 5 minutes; College Board says it is shown and can be hidden until 5 minutes remain. We used College Board's wording.
- **SPR length.** Princeton Review says "up to 5 digits"; College Board says 5 characters (6 for negatives) and its examples count the decimal point. We used College Board's rule and built our own examples in the same pattern. We avoided cases the official examples don't cover (such as a three-digit decimal with no leading zero).
- **Order inside the R&W groups.** College Board gives the domain order and the ordering rules, not the exact order of skills inside each domain. That the notes questions come last is reported by many prep sources and matches every practice test discussed; the lesson states the official domain order and puts notes last.

## Pacing and Guessing

### Best sources

- [How Are Scores Calculated? (College Board)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/scores/understanding-scores/how-scores-are-calculated) and the guessing sidebar in the [Assessment Framework (College Board, PDF)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/assessment-framework-for-digital-sat-suite.pdf) — Guess rather than leave a blank, after eliminating one or two choices; random or same-letter guessing is "unlikely to benefit" scores; the pattern check is not a guessing penalty.
- [Can I finish the test early? (Bluebook Help Center)](https://bluebook.collegeboard.org/help-center/can-i-finish-test-early) — No early exit with standard time, so spare minutes are only useful for checking.
- [Time Management Strategies for the Digital SAT (Acely)](https://acely.com/blog/time-management-strategies-for-digital-sat) — The 90-second placeholder rule, the 30-second "no mark on the scratch paper" rule for Math, a 60-second variant, and a letter-of-the-day fill when time runs out.
- [Helpful Test-Taking Strategies from Students (Acely)](https://acely.com/blog/post-sat-strategies-reflection-and-helpful-test-taking-strategies-from-students) — Students found Bluebook practice easier than the real test, bookmarked tough questions, and warned against shortest-answer and same-letter habits.
- [Should You Guess on the SAT? (Test Ninjas)](https://test-ninjas.com/should-you-guess-on-the-sat) — The 25%, 33% and 50% odds after eliminating zero, one or two choices.
- [Should You Guess on the SAT? (PrepScholar)](https://blog.prepscholar.com/should-you-guess-on-the-sat-guessing-strategies) — No penalty, so answer everything; mostly written for the 2016–2023 test.
- [Digital SAT Structure (Princeton Review)](https://www.princetonreview.com/college-advice/digital-sat-structure) — Reading questions come first, then writing questions (roughly 13–17 and 10–14 per module); "personal order of difficulty".
- [Don't Lose Points Trying to Finish on Time (The Critical Reader)](https://thecriticalreader.com/dont-lose-points-trying-to-finish-on-time/) — 2012 and written for the old test with a guessing penalty, but the principle carries over: slowing down on questions you can get right can be worth more than reaching every question.
- [Exam Feedback: August 22](https://test-ninjas.com/exam-feedback-2026-08-22), [September 12](https://test-ninjas.com/exam-feedback-2026-09-12) and [March 14, 2026 (Test Ninjas)](https://test-ninjas.com/exam-feedback-2026-03-14) — Many students ran out of time in R&W module 2 and guessed on the last questions; Math module 2 ended with a cluster of slow problems; many finished Math module 1 with 10–15 minutes to spare; a fraction answer didn't fit the SPR box and had to be converted on the spot.
- [Is the practice SAT test (Bluebook) accurate? (College Confidential)](https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/is-the-practice-sat-test-bluebook-accurate/3668724) — Tutors and parents: later practice tests are harder and closer to the real test; practice scores were mostly predictive, sometimes higher than the real score.
- [Give me 13 minutes, I'll teach you to get a 1500 SAT score (Patrick Choi, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=67DN6sz66Ps) — About 159,000 views (October 2026). Chapters: why finishing every question isn't the goal, and the "now, later or last" order.
- [Reading Pacing: Common SAT Weakness (Settele Tutoring, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F70oTd8GEtM) — About 17,000 views; description: getting through the hard R&W module when time is short.
- [How to Manage Time on the Digital SAT (Ivy League Mentors, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gMGQHSUFRCU) — Ten time strategies; the description doesn't list them, so none are quoted.

### What the best teachers agree on

- Never leave a blank; eliminate before guessing.
- Work in two passes: answer, guess and flag the slow ones, then come back.
- Put an answer in before flagging, in case time runs out.
- In Math, bank time on early questions, because difficulty climbs across the module.
- Learn the quick question types well; that is where time is won.
- Practice with full-length, timed Bluebook tests under real conditions.

### Disagreements and choices made

- **When to skip.** Acely gives 90 seconds in one place and 60 seconds in another; for Math it uses "no mark on the scratch paper after 30 seconds". Choice: 90 seconds in R&W, 30 seconds without a plan in Math, and 2 to 3 minutes for late, hard math questions when you do have a plan.
- **Finish everything, or slow down?** Patrick Choi and Meltzer say finishing isn't the goal; most guides say answer every question. Both are kept: accuracy first, but every question gets at least a guess.
- **Order inside an R&W module.** Most guides go in order; Acely suggests starting with the types you answer fastest. Choice: go in order with the "reset rule" by default; offer "writing block first" only to students who often run out of time, and only after testing it on a practice test. The 2026 reports of time running out in R&W module 2, where the last questions are quick notes questions, support offering it.
- **Same-letter guessing.** Acely recommends it for the final seconds; Acely's student article and College Board both say it isn't a real strategy. Choice: a last-seconds tool only.
- **Does guessing hurt?** Test Ninjas suggests heavy guessing near the routing cutoff could hurt; College Board says its pattern check is not a guessing penalty and that guessing beats a blank. Choice: the lesson never says guessing can hurt.
- **Checkpoints.** No official source gives checkpoints. Ours are a plan built from the official timing, the easy-to-hard order and the reading and writing blocks; the lesson presents them as a plan, not as College Board facts.
