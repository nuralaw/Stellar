# probability--or-overlap

**Shows:** for "A or B", adding both groups counts the overlap twice, so the overlap is subtracted once.

**Storyboard:**
1. Two overlapping circles inside a box of 40 students: Art (22) and Music (15). The overlap is labelled 6.
2. The Art circle fills: "22". The Music circle fills: "15". The sum "22 + 15 = 37" appears.
3. The overlap flashes twice, once per circle, with the note "counted twice".
4. The overlap is subtracted: "37 - 6 = 31". The union (both circles, overlap once) fills with one colour.
5. The fraction \(\frac{31}{40}\) builds. A short last frame shows the "neither" region (9 students) with \(1 - \frac{31}{40} = \frac{9}{40}\).

**Labels & formulas:** \(P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \text{ and } B)\); \(22 + 15 - 6 = 31\); "Art", "Music", "both".

**Stress:** the overlap in a contrasting colour that flashes at the "counted twice" moment; the union filled in one calm colour. Keep the circles large enough for 2-digit labels on a phone.
