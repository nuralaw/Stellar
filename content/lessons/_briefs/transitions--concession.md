# transitions--concession

**Shows:** a concession admits a point against the writer, then turns back: the admission sentence leans one way, the next sentence swings back.

**Storyboard:**
1. A balance with the writer's claim on the left: "Best café in town." (heavy, primary).
2. A small weight lands on the right: "Admittedly, it is small." The scale dips a little. Chip "Admittedly,".
3. A curved arrow turns back to the left: "Still, its coffee wins." The scale tilts back to the left past level. Chip "Still,".
4. Hold: the claim wins; the two chips are marked "admit" and "turn back".

**Labels & formulas:** "Best café in town.", "Admittedly, it is small.", "Still, its coffee wins.", tags "admit", "turn back".

**Stress:** the small weight is a visible but modest cost; the turn-back arrow is primary. Keep the scale simple and centred.
