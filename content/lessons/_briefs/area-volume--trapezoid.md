# area-volume--trapezoid

**Shows:** a parallelogram is a rectangle in disguise (\(A = bh\), the slanted side does not matter), and a trapezoid is half of a parallelogram built from two copies (\(A = \tfrac{1}{2}(b_1 + b_2)h\)).

**Storyboard:**
1. A parallelogram appears: base \(b = 14\) along the bottom, slanted side 9 on the left, a dashed vertical height \(h = 6\) from the top edge down to the base with a small right-angle square. The slanted side is outlined in grey and tagged "not the height".
2. A right triangle at the left end (cut by the dashed height) lifts off and slides to the right end. The parallelogram becomes a 14 by 6 rectangle. The label \(A = bh\) fades in under it.
3. Reset to a trapezoid: bases \(b_1 = 7\) (top) and \(b_2 = 13\) (bottom), height \(h = 5\) with the dashed line and right-angle square.
4. A copy of the trapezoid turns upside down (rotates 180 degrees) and slides in beside the first. Together they form a parallelogram with base \(b_1 + b_2\) and the same height. The bottom edge is labelled \(b_1 + b_2 = 20\).
5. Half of the parallelogram is highlighted (the original trapezoid) and the formula appears: \(A = \tfrac{1}{2}(b_1 + b_2)\,h = \tfrac{1}{2}(20)(5) = 50\). The two bases get the accent colour.

**Labels & formulas:** \(A = bh\), \(A = \tfrac{1}{2}(b_1 + b_2)\,h\), "height: straight across, at a right angle", "not on the sheet: memorise", values 14, 9, 6, 7, 13, 5, 50.

**Stress:** the dashed height line and the right-angle mark draw the eye first; the slanted side is dimmed. The moving triangle and the flipped copy use the accent colour; everything else stays muted. On a phone, show the two halves one after the other (frames 1-2, then 3-5), with labels at least 12 px.
