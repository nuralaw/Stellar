# desmos--slider

**Shows:** an unknown constant becomes a slider; dragging it until the graph matches the words ("touches once") reveals the constant.

**Storyboard:**
1. The problem line: "\(y = x^2 - 6x + k\) touches the x-axis exactly once." The letter k pulses.
2. A graph with the parabola at k = 0 crossing the x-axis twice; a slider bar "k" appears under it with the handle at 0.
3. The handle slides right in steps; the parabola rises. At k = 5 and k = 7 it still crosses twice (two dots flash and disappear).
4. At k = 9 the vertex lands on the axis; one dot at \((3, 0)\) in the accent colour and the label "k = 9: one touch". Beyond 9, a short flash shows the parabola floating above the axis ("no crossing").
5. A closing tag: "one constant, simple answer: slider. Several constants or odd fractions: algebra."

**Labels & formulas:** \(y = x^2 - 6x + k\), "k = 0: two crossings", "k = 9: one touch", "k > 9: none", "slider".

**Stress:** the handle position and the parabola move together; the number of crossing dots (2, 1, 0) is the thing to read, drawn big. The final tag in muted colour. Slider handle at least 16 units wide for a phone.
