# rhetorical-synthesis--compare

**Shows:** a comparison goal needs both things in one sentence, tied together on the same feature, with the right signal word.

**Storyboard:**
1. Two cards side by side: "Pine Hill library" and "Cedar Park library", each with a small row of feature chips: "opened 1921 / 40,000 books" and "opened 1987 / 40,000 books".
2. Goal tag "similarity": the matching chips ("40,000 books") glow and a bracket links them; signal chip "both".
3. Goal tag swaps to "difference": the mismatching chips ("1921" vs "1987") glow and a bracket links them; signal chip "while".
4. A third sentence card that names only Pine Hill's chips tries to land and bounces off (red "one side only"). Hold.

**Labels & formulas:** "Pine Hill", "Cedar Park", chips "opened 1921", "opened 1987", "40,000 books"; tags "similarity", "difference", signals "both", "while", "one side only".

**Stress:** the linked pair of chips is the focus (primary); everything else pale. Red only on the one-side-only card.
