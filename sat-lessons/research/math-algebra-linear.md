## Linear equations in one variable

### Best sources

- [College Board: Assessment Framework for the Digital SAT Suite](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/assessment-framework-for-digital-sat-suite.pdf): the authority. Appendix B lists the SAT testing points for this skill: create and use equations in context, identify the equation for a context, interpret a constant, variable, factor, term or solution, solve "making strategic use of algebraic structure", and decide when there is no solution, one solution or infinitely many. Appendix D has the SPR entry rules.
- [College Board: SAT Math algebra question types](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/whats-on-the-test/math/types/algebra): confirms the five Algebra types and the domain description.
- [Khan Academy: Solving linear equations and linear inequalities (advanced lesson)](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/v2-sat-math/x0fcc98a58ba3bea7:algebra-harder/x0fcc98a58ba3bea7:solving-linear-equations-and-inequalities-harder/a/v2-sat-lesson-solving-linear-equations-and-inequalities): College Board's practice partner. Good on when to clear fractions (only x has a fraction: leave it to the last step; fractions everywhere: clear them first), on finding an expression straight from the given equation, and on solution counts with a constant. The [foundations version](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/v2-sat-math/x0fcc98a58ba3bea7:algebra-easier/x0fcc98a58ba3bea7:solving-linear-equations-and-inequalities-easier/a/v2-sat-lesson-solving-linear-equations-and-inequalities) has the same rules with easier examples.
- [Test Ninjas: SAT Linear Equations in One Variable](https://www.test-ninjas.com/sat-linear-equations-in-one-variable): the clearest list of sub-types (direct solves, fractions and decimals, word problems, parameter questions, solution-type questions), a simplify-organize-solve-check routine and a solid trap list.
- [JustLockedIn: pattern pages for this skill](https://justlockedin-main.pages.dev/patterns/math/algebra/linear-equations-in-one-variable/): five patterns. The [Analyze Solutions](https://justlockedin-main.pages.dev/patterns/math/algebra/linear-equations-in-one-variable/analyze-solutions.html) page covers the x = 0 trap and constants hidden inside a coefficient. The [Evaluate Expression](https://justlockedin-main.pages.dev/patterns/math/algebra/linear-equations-in-one-variable/evaluate-expression.html) page covers scaling, shared pieces and block substitution, plus factoring a repeated (x − 3) instead of expanding it.
- [Magoosh: Solving Linear Equations](https://sat.magoosh.com/lessons/7546-solving-linear-equations): a short video lesson page. Good on variables on both sides, the reciprocal for a fraction coefficient, the LCM to clear fractions, and solving straight for an expression.
- [Open Exam Prep: Linear equations in one variable](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sat-math/algebra-linear/linear-equations-one-variable): the minus-sign trap, a proportional shortcut for expression questions, and the Desmos check of graphing y = left side and y = right side.
- [PrepScholar: Plugging in answers](https://blog.prepscholar.com/plugging-in-answers-a-critical-sat-math-act-math-strategy) and [Plugging in numbers](https://blog.prepscholar.com/plugging-in-numbers-sat-act-math-strategy): the backsolving method (start with a middle choice, use the order of the choices). The articles come from the paper-test years, but the method still works for multiple choice.
- [YouTube, James Lu SAT: The Last SAT Desmos Guide You'll Need](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e-O4nwVHQ-Y) (about 1.08 million views): the creator's own description lists his methods. Single-variable equations graph as vertical lines. For number of solutions, graph the left and right sides as separate lines. For constants, set x_1 to a list such as [1...10] and use ~ regression. The chapter titles also give his own (unverified) share of SAT Math for each method.
- [YouTube, Tutorllini Test Prep: Desmos Lesson #6 How Many Solutions?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-fS29WVBxlw) and [Lesson #7 with Constants](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wgIuacO3Xdw) (about 129,000 views each): popular short Desmos lessons. The chapters show constants handled in Desmos and checked against the multiple-choice options.
- [YouTube, Penguin SAT Prep: Easily Solve SAT Constants Questions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LJooEH4d434) (about 120,000 views): the chapters pair Desmos with plugging in values and choosing your own numbers for infinite-solution and no-solution questions.
- [YouTube, LearnSATMath: The only SAT Math Desmos Guide you'll ever need](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jCT0jTVzKy0) (about 1 million views): has chapters on single-variable equations and on the limits of Desmos. A third-party AI summary ([youtubesummary.com](https://youtubesummary.com/summary/jCT0jTVzKy0)) says the video graphs both sides separately and warns that questions about how many solutions exist still need the algebra rules.
- [The Test Advantage: a tutor fact-checks a viral Reddit SAT guide](https://thetestadvantage.com/blog/pro-tutor-fact-checks-viral-1500-sat-in-2-weeks-reddit-guide): the only Reddit view we could reach. It calls the claim that Desmos replaces about 90% of the math an overstatement and quotes a commenter who points out that the section still tests real math. Its advice: learn the concepts first, then use Desmos to carry out the steps.

### What the best teachers agree on

- Read the last line first. Many questions ask for an expression (2x + 3, 6x + 9) or a constant, and the value of x is planted in the choices.
- Clear fractions by multiplying every term, whole numbers included, by the LCD. Distributing a negative is the most common single error.
- For solution counts, simplify both sides to ax + b and compare. If the x-terms cancel, a false leftover means no solution and a true leftover means infinitely many. A linear equation never has exactly two solutions, and x = 0 is a real solution.
- For constants: no solution means equal coefficients and different constants; infinitely many means both are equal. Simplify first, because combined terms change the coefficient.
- For word problems, define the variable with units. The rate multiplies the variable, the fixed amount stands alone, "less than" reverses the order, and a negative or fractional count signals a setup error.
- Desmos helps as a check and for messy numbers: type the equation (a vertical line appears) or graph both sides and read the crossing point.

### Disagreements and choices made

- **Desmos first or algebra first?** The Desmos-heavy channels (James Lu, LearnSATMath, Ping Pong Physics' "800 with only Desmos") push Desmos for almost everything. Khan, Test Ninjas, The Test Advantage and the College Board calculator page say short equations are faster by hand. Effortless Math's May 2026 report (anecdotal, based on student reports) describes Module 2 questions that are harder to do in Desmos. Choice: algebra is the main method. Desmos is taught as a check and a backup, including the list trick for two constants, because several sources document it the same way.
- **Plugging in answer choices.** PrepScholar calls it a top strategy. Test Ninjas says it does not replace algebra and fails on SPR questions, and PrepScholar's own algebra article admits it is slower for simple linear equations. Choice: we left it out of the method and kept substitution as the check step.
- **Fractions.** Khan leaves a lone fraction coefficient to the last step. Most other sources always clear denominators. Choice: clear the LCD when several terms have fractions, and multiply by the flipped fraction when only x has one.
- **Counting solutions in Desmos.** James Lu teaches graphing both sides. The LearnSATMath summary warns that lines can look parallel or identical when they are not. Choice: compare coefficients as the main method, and use Desmos only as a visual check, with that warning in the lesson.

## Linear functions

### Best sources

- [College Board: Assessment Framework for the Digital SAT Suite](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/assessment-framework-for-digital-sat-suite.pdf): defines a linear function by its input and output and lists the tasks: build a function for a context, interpret an input-output pair, constant, factor or term ("including situations where seeing structure provides an advantage"), interpret a graph, connect tables, graphs and rules, find an output from an input or an input from an output, and write the rule from two pairs or from one pair and a rate. One framework sample combines two linear functions with a multiplier. Its wrong choices come from dropping the multiplier, or from multiplying the input instead of the output.
- [Khan Academy: Understanding linear relationships (lesson)](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/v2-sat-math/x0fcc98a58ba3bea7:algebra-easier/x0fcc98a58ba3bea7:linear-equation-word-problems-easier/a/v2-sat-lesson-understanding-linear-relationships): one-time versus repeated amounts, clue words for slope ("per", "each", "rate") and for the intercept ("initial", "starting"), and building a function from a table.
- [Khan Academy: Graphs of linear equations and functions (lesson)](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/v2-sat-math/x0fcc98a58ba3bea7:algebra-easier/x0fcc98a58ba3bea7:graphs-of-linear-equations-and-functions-easier/a/v2-sat-lesson-graphs-of-linear-equations-and-functions): the slope formula and reading slope and intercepts from graphs and equations.
- [Test Ninjas: SAT Linear Functions](https://www.test-ninjas.com/sat-linear-functions): the widest list of sub-types (evaluating, input from output, graphs, context, building from data, threshold models, unknown constants, combined functions, shifts) and sharp traps: f(x + 2) versus f(x) + 2, f(0) versus f(x) = 0, and dropping units such as thousands of dollars.
- [JustLockedIn: Linear functions patterns](https://justlockedin-main.pages.dev/patterns/math/algebra/linear-functions/): five patterns. [Data to Equation](https://justlockedin-main.pages.dev/patterns/math/algebra/linear-functions/data-to-equation.html) lists the traps (an upside-down slope, using a table value at x ≠ 0 as b, using average cost instead of the rate). [Graphical Analysis](https://justlockedin-main.pages.dev/patterns/math/algebra/linear-functions/graphical-analysis.html) covers shifted graphs. [Abstract Functions](https://justlockedin-main.pages.dev/patterns/math/algebra/linear-functions/abstract-functions.html) covers constants, combinations and expression inputs like f(k − 5).
- [Open Exam Prep: Linear Functions, Slope and Rate of Change](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sat-math/algebra-linear/linear-functions-slope): f(a) = b as a point on the graph, checking the rate across every interval, and Desmos regression giving r² = 1 for exactly linear data.
- [Varsity Tutors: Linear Functions](https://www.varsitytutors.com/practice/subjects/sat-math/lessons/linear-functions): how to tell tables apart (equal differences mean linear, equal second differences mean quadratic, equal ratios mean exponential) and when each form of a line is most useful.
- [Albert.io: Creating linear functions](https://www.albert.io/blog/creating-linear-functions-connecting-representations-a-guide/): the four representations (words, table, rule, graph), "at this rate" as a slope signal, and checking where an axis starts.
- [Axiom Academy: Interpreting slope and intercept](https://theaxiomacademy.org/learn/sat-style-interpreting-slope-intercept): always attach units to a slope, and do a reasonableness check on the sign.
- [UWorld: SAT Algebra key concepts](https://collegeprep.uworld.com/blog/types-of-heart-of-algebra-sat-questions-and-strategies/): one useful trap, answering "change in output for 1 unit of input" when the question asks for the input change that gives a stated output change.
- [YouTube, Unique Prep: SAT Math 11, Interpreting Linear Functions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Asy7ZNar2MM) (about 27,000 views): the description says it applies to the Digital SAT, and the chapter plan shows a rearrange-and-match method, a units method and a note on fractional slopes.
- [YouTube, Penguin SAT Prep: The BEST Way to Solve SAT Linear Word Problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eHFr8frXTMA) (about 19,000 views): chapters on interpreting the parts of a linear model, interpreting functions, fractional slopes and rates, and standard-form problems.
- [YouTube, Langley Prep: Find the Slope from a Table in Seconds with Desmos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gl04WalEG6c): the description says tables often carry extra data, and that two points plus regression give the slope.
- [YouTube, Tutorllini Test Prep: Desmos Lesson #9 Regression](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yAYGDGOO5bA) (about 213,000 views) and [Settele Tutoring: Desmos Regression](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SJnY-hSTpFs): the most-watched regression lessons. Both start with linear regression from a table (per their chapters and descriptions).
- [YouTube, Point Avenue: SAT Math, Linear Functions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xbWNEY0lpuE) and [BY Prep: Digital SAT Linear Functions, Explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UO1U82LpEHY): short overviews. BY Prep's description claims only 12% of students in its system got linear functions right. That figure is unverified, and the presenter is AI-generated.

### What the best teachers agree on

- Slope is the change in output per one unit of input, always with units. The intercept is the output when the input is 0, the starting value.
- To build a rule from data, find the slope first, then b from one point, then check with a third point.
- f(a) is an output. f(x) = c means "solve for the input". The pair (a, f(a)) is a point on the graph.
- A table is linear only if the rate is the same across every interval. With uneven x-steps, divide each change in y by its own change in x.
- Translating a story: "per" and "each" give the slope, "initial" and "flat fee" give the intercept, and anything that drains or loses value has a negative slope.
- For data, Desmos regression (y_1 ~ mx_1 + b) is fast and exact.

### Disagreements and choices made

- **Regression versus hand work for tables.** Langley Prep, Erika and Settele promote regression even for easy tables. Khan and most written guides compute the slope from two rows. Choice: hand work first, because two rows take seconds. Regression is for messy numbers or as a check, and r² = 1 confirms exact linear data.
- **What the intercept means when 0 is outside the story.** Axiom gives the plain "value at 0" template. Test Ninjas and JustLockedIn warn that input 0 may not make sense. Choice: teach the template and add the structure trap with C(w) = 9 + 1.5(w − 2) and "years since 2015" (Example 5 and a Trap callout).
- **Where shifts belong.** Some courses teach f(x + k) only under nonlinear functions, but Test Ninjas and JustLockedIn list linear versions under this skill. Choice: a short rule section plus one hard SPR example (Example 6).

## Linear equations in two variables

### Best sources

- [College Board: Assessment Framework for the Digital SAT Suite](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/assessment-framework-for-digital-sat-suite.pdf): the testing points. A two-variable equation shows a constraint on two quantities where neither is the input. Others: interpret a solution, coefficient or term in context, interpret the graph of Ax + By = C, connect tables, graphs and equations, given one quantity find the other, and write a line from two points, a point and a slope, or a parallel or perpendicular line. One framework sample (perimeters of two regular polygons) shows the classic "interpret a coefficient" item. We studied its style but did not reuse it.
- [Khan Academy: Graphs of linear equations and functions (lesson)](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/v2-sat-math/x0fcc98a58ba3bea7:algebra-easier/x0fcc98a58ba3bea7:graphs-of-linear-equations-and-functions-easier/a/v2-sat-lesson-graphs-of-linear-equations-and-functions): the slope formula, writing a line from a point and slope or from two points, and parallel and perpendicular lines. It works only in slope-intercept form and never names point-slope form.
- [Test Ninjas: SAT Linear Equations in Two Variables](https://www.test-ninjas.com/sat-linear-equations-in-two-variables): a spot-extract-plug routine, the shortcuts slope −A/B and y-intercept C/B, and traps (reading the value at x = 1 as the intercept, missing a negative rate, multiplying a flat fee by the rate).
- [Varsity Tutors: Equations with Two Variables](https://www.varsitytutors.com/practice/subjects/sat-math/lessons/equations-with-two-variables): which form to use for which job, and checking the final line with both points.
- [JustLockedIn: two-variable patterns](https://justlockedin-main.pages.dev/patterns/math/algebra/linear-equations-in-two-variables/): [Interpret Models](https://justlockedin-main.pages.dev/patterns/math/algebra/linear-equations-in-two-variables/interpret-models.html) (a coefficient is a rate, a product term is a category total, the constant is the grand total), [Parallel and Perpendicular](https://justlockedin-main.pages.dev/patterns/math/algebra/linear-equations-in-two-variables/parallel-perpendicular.html) (the negative reciprocal of −1/3 is 3; slopes hidden in standard form) and [Properties From Equations](https://justlockedin-main.pages.dev/patterns/math/algebra/linear-equations-in-two-variables/properties-from-equations.html) (−A/B, not −B/A; moving a line up uses y − k).
- [1600.io: Finding the Slope of Any Equation in Any Form](https://1600.io/courses/1698988/lectures/51927809): Desmos has no built-in slope readout. The author suggests solving for y instead of memorizing −A/B.
- [Albert.io: Linear equations in two variables](https://www.albert.io/blog/linear-equations-in-two-variables-master-for-sat-math-success/): every point on the line is a solution, and plugging in one point can eliminate most answer choices.
- [Test Ninjas: Desmos calculator guide](https://test-ninjas.com/sat-desmos-calculator-guide): checked against College Board in October 2026. Bluebook's Desmos turns off images, folders and notes. Clicking a graph shows its intercepts. Comparing slopes is faster by hand.
- [YouTube, Southern Edge Math with Janet: Slope and Intercepts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gqBakZkvafU) and [Parallel and Perpendicular Lines](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNKpWyL7-k8) (2026, Digital SAT with Desmos): the chapter lists match the official sub-types closely: slope hidden in standard form, an x-intercept from a table, moving a line, and unknown constants from parallel and perpendicular conditions.
- [YouTube, OneForAllSat: SAT Linear Equations in Two Variables](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bln9UFMMR5Y) (November 2026): a long description notes that the reference sheet has nothing on lines, and covers −A/B, cover-up intercepts, horizontal and vertical lines, moving a line, and totals built as rate × number + rate × number.
- [YouTube, Scalar Learning: Critical Concepts for an 800, Heart of Algebra (Part 1 of 4)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b2I0GqUi2RM) (about 204,000 views, from 2020): the most-viewed overview. Its chapters cover slope-intercept, point-slope and standard form. It dates from the paper test, so we used it only for concepts.
- [YouTube, The Organic Chemistry Tutor: Slope and Linear Equations, SAT Math Part 37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oXgiUZERCwU) (about 46,000 views, from 2019): writing equations from a graph and from a table. Paper-test era, concepts only.

### What the best teachers agree on

- To read the slope and y-intercept, get to y = mx + b, or use −A/B and C/B for standard form. The x-coefficient in standard form is never the slope.
- Find intercepts by setting the other variable to 0 (the cover-up method for standard form).
- Parallel lines share a slope. Perpendicular slopes are negative reciprocals, and checking that the product is −1 catches both kinds of slip.
- A point is on a line exactly when it satisfies the equation, so substitute to check.
- In Ax + By = C stories, the coefficient is the price or amount per item, the term is the total for one category, and the constant is the overall total. An intercept means "all of one item, none of the other".
- Always verify the final equation with a known point.

### Disagreements and choices made

- **Memorize −A/B, or always solve for y?** Test Ninjas and JustLockedIn teach −A/B. 1600.io prefers solving for y. Choice: teach −A/B as the shortcut, with solving for y as the backup, and show both in Examples 1 and 3.
- **Point-slope form.** Khan avoids it. Varsity Tutors, Albert.io and Scalar teach it. Choice: teach it, because it is the fastest way to write a line through a given point. Example 3 shows that all four choices can pass through the point, so only the slope decides.
- **Desmos for lines.** Some videos graph every answer choice. 1600.io notes that Desmos shows no slope, and Test Ninjas says slope comparisons are faster by hand. Choice: use Desmos to see intercepts (click the line), to build a line from two points with regression, and to check. Use algebra for slopes, parallel lines and perpendicular lines, with a warning that pictures mislead when the axis scales differ.
