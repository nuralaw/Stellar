# The smallest safe step
Predict the conclusion the facts force.

## Predict "So…"

The blank ends an argument. **Pick the smallest step the facts force**: nothing new, nothing stronger.

```example
Gardeners blamed heavy rain for split tomatoes. But in the driest summer on record, tomatoes split just as often. This suggests that ______
1. Belief: rain splits tomatoes :: Give each sentence a job.
2. Twist: little rain, same splits :: The fact after "But".
3. So: rain isn't the cause :: Predict before reading the choices.
4. Answer: rain may not be the main cause :: One summer, so a careful "may".
```

## Predict, then match

```quiz
People thought homing pigeons follow roads home. Yet pigeons released over open sea still flew straight home. This suggests that ______
* pigeons can find their way without roads.
- pigeons released at sea flew home. :: That repeats a fact. The blank needs the next step.
- roads are useless to pigeons. :: Too strong. They just don't need roads.
> Belief: roads guide them. Twist: no roads, still home. So roads aren't required.
```

# The lead-in
The words before the blank set the job.

## Reason or conclusion?

Read the words before the blank. *Since* or *because* wants a reason, not a conclusion.

```example
A café's own customers rated its coffee 9 out of 10. A critic says the score proves little, because ______
1. Lead-in: "because" :: The blank is a reason.
2. Why does the score prove little? :: Turn the lead-in into a question.
3. Answer: its customers already chose that café :: They aren't typical coffee drinkers.
```

## Find the reason

```quiz
On a new park's opening day, 90% of visitors surveyed said they support the park. The council warns that this may overstate the town's support, since ______
- most of the town supports the park. :: That's the claim being doubted, not a reason.
* people who came to the opening probably already liked the park.
- the park cost more than planned. :: Cost is new and doesn't explain the survey.
> *Since* asks why the number may be too high: opening-day visitors aren't the whole town.
```

# Stay inside the text
Same place, same group, no new facts.

## Mark the limits

Same place and same group as the text. No new facts.

```example
At one Ohio school, students who ate breakfast scored higher on a morning quiz. This suggests that ______
1. Text covers one school, one quiz :: Mark the limits.
2. Out: "breakfast raises grades everywhere" :: Wider than one school.
3. Answer: breakfast may have helped these students on the quiz :: Same group, same quiz.
```

## Keep it local

```quiz
A factory opened upstream of Port Avel's bay. Its waste water warmed the bay by 3°C, and the bay's crabs breed poorly in water that warm. The crab catch has fallen. This suggests that ______
- factories harm sea life in bays worldwide. :: Too wide. The text covers one bay.
- the waste water also contains poisons. :: New fact. The text mentions only heat.
* the factory's warm water may be one reason for the smaller catch.
> Chain: factory → warmer bay → fewer young crabs → smaller catch. It stays in Port Avel.
```

# Firm facts, firm answer
"May" isn't always right.

## When a fact rules out

Careful words aren't always right. If a fact rules something out, the firm choice wins.

```example
Biologists assumed a cave fish finds food by sight. But the fish has no eyes. It can be concluded that ______
1. Fact: no eyes :: Certain, not a trend.
2. No eyes means no sight :: Impossible, so skip "may".
3. Answer: the fish does not find food by sight :: Firm fact, firm answer.
```

## Pick the firm one

```quiz
Historians dated a map to 1650. But it is drawn entirely in an ink first made in 1704. It can be concluded that ______
- the map was made in 1704. :: Too exact. It could be from any later year.
* the map was not made in 1650.
- the map may be a copy of a 1650 map. :: A guess. No sentence mentions a copy.
> Ink from 1704 can't be on a 1650 map. That's certain, so "was not" fits.
```

# Trap: it could be true
Likely isn't enough. Find the sentence that proves it.

## Which sentence proves it?

A sensible story the text never tells is wrong. Ask: which sentence proves this?

```example
A bakery moved next to a train station. Its morning sales tripled. Afternoon sales stayed flat. This suggests that ______
1. Tempting: "the bakery lowered its prices" :: Possible, but no sentence says so.
2. Stated: new spot, mornings tripled :: Use only what's written.
3. Answer: the station likely brought more morning customers :: Every piece is in the text.
```

## Spot the trap

```quiz
A city put bright lights in one park. Evening visits there doubled. At a similar unlit park nearby, they stayed the same. This suggests that ______
- people avoided the unlit park because of crime. :: Trap: sounds real, but crime is never mentioned.
- every city park should get lights. :: That's advice, and it stretches one park to all.
* the lights likely drew more evening visitors.
> The unlit park didn't change, so the lights explain the jump. No sentence mentions crime.
```
