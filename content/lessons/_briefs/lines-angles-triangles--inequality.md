# lines-angles-triangles--inequality

**Shows:** a triangle only closes if the third side is longer than the difference and shorter than the sum of the other two: \(\lvert a - b \rvert < c < a + b\).

**Storyboard:**
1. Two sticks lie on a baseline: a = 8 (blue) and b = 10 (orange), joined at a hinge point. A third stick c (green) with a handle is drawn from the free end of a to the free end of b. Labels \(a = 8\), \(b = 10\), \(c = ?\).
2. Drag c longer: it stretches until the two sticks lie flat in a line. At \(c = 18 = a + b\) the triangle collapses to a flat line. The text "c = a + b: flat, no triangle" turns red. The allowed range bar below the picture ends at 18 with an open circle.
3. Drag c shorter: sticks fold back on each other. At \(c = 2 = b - a\) the triangle collapses again, now folded. "c = b - a: flat, no triangle" in red, open circle at 2.
4. The range bar fills green between the two open circles, 2 and 18. A moving valid triangle (say c = 9) swings through shapes while the label shows \(2 < c < 18\).
5. A short rule line appears: "Any side is shorter than the other two put together."

**Labels & formulas:** \(a = 8\), \(b = 10\), \(c\), \(\lvert a - b \rvert < c < a + b\), \(2 < c < 18\), "open circles: ends are not allowed".

**Stress:** the collapse moments (flat line, red) draw the eye; the green allowed range is the take-away. Keep the number line with 2 and 18 large and readable on a phone; sticks at least 8 px thick.
