# equivalent-expressions--fractions

**Shows:** in a fraction you may cancel only factors, never terms, and a subtraction needs a common denominator and flips every sign of the second top.

**Storyboard:**
1. The fraction \(\dfrac{6x + 42}{12}\). The top is factored: \(\dfrac{6(x + 7)}{12}\). The factor 6 on top and the 12 below each glow; they reduce to \(\dfrac{x + 7}{2}\).
2. The trap: the same fraction with a "cancel 6" stroke through only the 6 of \(6x\) and the 12. A red cross, with the numbers check \(\dfrac{2 + 6}{2 + 4} \ne \dfrac{6}{4}\), 8/6 against 6/4.
3. Two fractions \(\dfrac{5}{x + 1} - \dfrac{2}{x + 3}\). Each bottom lights up; arrows carry the missing bottom to the other fraction's top: \(5(x + 3)\) and \(2(x + 1)\).
4. The second top sits in brackets with its minus: \(-(2x + 2)\); both signs flip in the accent: \(5x + 15 - 2x - 2\).
5. Result \(\dfrac{3x + 13}{(x + 1)(x + 3)}\).

**Labels & formulas:** "cancel factors", "not terms", \(\dfrac{6x + 42}{12}\), \(\dfrac{5}{x + 1} - \dfrac{2}{x + 3}\), \(\dfrac{3x + 13}{(x + 1)(x + 3)}\), "the minus flips both signs".

**Stress:** factors in the same colour pair up; the red cross on the trap frame is brief but clear. The flipped signs pulse once. Fractions stay at least 18 px tall so the numerators are readable on a phone.
