# percentages--part-whole

**Shows:** one bar for the whole, one shaded piece for the part: part = percent times whole, and the three unknowns (part, whole, percent) are the same equation.

**Storyboard:**
1. A long bar labelled "whole" with the number 80 above it, split by a 100% scale (ticks at 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%).
2. A piece of 20 fills from the left, labelled "part 20". A bracket under it reads "?% of the whole".
3. The equation \(\frac{p}{100} \times 80 = 20\) appears, then \(p = \frac{20}{80} \times 100 = 25\). The bracket now reads "25%" and lands on the 25% tick.
4. The bar swaps to a second case: the part 18 is shaded and labelled "30%", the whole is a question mark. The equation \(0.3w = 18\) and the result \(w = 60\) fill in; the bar stretches to 60.
5. A third case shows a part longer than the bar: a 140% bracket extends past the end of the bar labelled "whole". Caption "over 100%: the part is bigger".

**Labels & formulas:** "whole", "part", \(\text{part} = \frac{p}{100} \times \text{whole}\), \(p = 25\), \(w = 60\), "140%".

**Stress:** whole in a calm neutral tone, part in an accent tone, the unknown pulsing; the 100% line and the over-100% overhang must be unmistakable. Keep it to one case on screen at a time (phone readable).
