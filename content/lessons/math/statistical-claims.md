## Match claim to study

**Random sample** → the results fit the whole group it came from, no bigger. **Random assignment** → you may say *causes*. Otherwise, only a link.

```example
A researcher randomly selects 60 of a hospital's 400 nurses. In the sample, nurses who nap report less stress. What can she conclude?
1. Sample: random, from this hospital's 400 nurses :: Find who was sampled, and how.
2. Results fit all 400, not other hospitals :: A random sample fits its own group.
3. Naps assigned at random? No, nurses chose :: No assignment, so no cause.
4. For this hospital's nurses, napping is linked to less stress
```

## Find the largest group

```quiz
A researcher randomly selects 80 of the 500 ninth graders at one school and asks how long they sleep. What is the largest group the results describe?
- The 80 students surveyed :: Too small. A random sample speaks for its whole group.
* All 500 ninth graders at the school
- All students at the school :: Other grades had no chance of being picked.
- All ninth graders in the state :: Only this school was sampled.
> The sample was random from this school's ninth graders, so it describes exactly them.
```

## Biased sample, no generalizing

Volunteers or a hand-picked group may differ from everyone else. A bigger sample doesn't fix that.

```quiz
A fitness app asks its users to rate a new plan. Of the 6,000 who choose to answer, 85% love it. Why might this not represent all users?
- 6,000 users are too few. :: Size is fine. Who answered is the problem.
* Users chose whether to answer.
- 85% is too high to be true. :: A fair sample could give 85% too.
> Volunteers with strong opinions answer more often, so they may not match all users.
```

## Random assignment allows cause

When chance decides who gets the treatment, the groups start out alike. A difference then comes from the treatment.

```quiz
A researcher randomly selects 120 of a company's 2,000 employees and randomly assigns 60 of them to walk at lunch. The walkers report less tiredness, on average. Which conclusion is best?
* Lunch walks likely reduce tiredness for this company's employees.
- Walks are linked to less tiredness, but no cause is shown. :: Too weak. Random assignment allows a cause.
- Lunch walks likely reduce tiredness for all office workers. :: Too big. Only this company was sampled.
> Random assignment: cause. Random sample of this company: its employees, no further.
```

## Trap: big survey, no cause

A survey only watches. However big or random, it shows a link, never a cause.

```quiz
A random sample of 5,000 adults in a state is surveyed. Those who read before bed report falling asleep faster. Which conclusion is best?
- Reading before bed makes the state's adults fall asleep faster. :: Trap: nobody was assigned to read. Link only.
* For the state's adults, reading before bed is linked to falling asleep faster.
- Nothing can be concluded. :: Too weak. A random sample still shows a link.
> No assignment, so no cause. Random sample of the state, so the link fits its adults.
```

## Recap

- Random sample: the results fit the group it came from, no bigger.
