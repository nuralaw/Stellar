# Clue, predict, match
Find the clue. Predict a word. Match a choice.

## The text proves the word

Find the **clue**. Predict your own plain word. Then pick the choice that fits the clue best.

```example
Maya's grandfather is famously ______: he saves every receipt, labels every jar and never misplaces a key.
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
Choices: meticulous / flamboyant / secretive
1. Clue: "saves every receipt, labels every jar" :: The colon points to the clue.
2. Predict: very careful :: Your own word, before the choices.
3. flamboyant = showy; secretive = hides things :: Neither means careful. Cross out.
4. Answer: meticulous :: Careful about every detail.
```

```more
A **clue** is a word or group of words in the text that shows the blank's meaning. The test asks for the "most logical and precise" word: it must fit the clue's meaning and also sound natural with the words around it. Predict a simple word like *careful* first.
```

## Clue in the next sentence

```quiz
For the mill town of Brenton, the railway was ______. When the line closed, the mill could not ship its cloth, and half the town's families left.
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
* indispensable
- negligible :: That means unimportant. Losing the railway hurt the town.
- burdensome :: That means a heavy load. The railway helped the town.
> Without the railway the town fell apart, so it was necessary: *indispensable*.
```

# Contrast words
Contrast flips the blank only across the word.

## Opposite sides, opposite meaning

A contrast word (*but*, *although*, *however*) flips the meaning only if the clue and the blank are on **opposite sides** of it.

```visual
words-in-context--contrast-side
```

```example
Although the trail looks ______ on the map, hikers need a full day to finish it.
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
Choices: brief / rugged / scenic
1. Clue: "need a full day" :: The trail is long.
2. Blank and clue: opposite sides of *Although* :: So the blank is the opposite.
3. Answer: brief :: The opposite of long.
```

```more
*Although* starts the first idea, and the comma ends it. The blank is in the first idea. The clue ("a full day") is in the second. They are on opposite sides, so the blank means the opposite of the clue.
```

## Flip it yourself

```quiz
Critics first dismissed the album as ______. Ten years later, however, they ranked it among the decade's most original records.
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
- groundbreaking :: That is the later view. *However* flips it.
* derivative
- controversial :: Nobody argued about it. Critics just dismissed it.
> Later view: "most original". The blank is on the other side of *however*, so it means unoriginal: *derivative*.
```

## Same side, same meaning

```quiz
Even though the audience praised the talk, the speaker felt ______: her hands shook, and she lost her place twice.
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
- poised :: Poised means calm and steady. That flips the clue.
* apprehensive
- gratified :: Gratified means pleased. That fits the praise, not the shaking.
> The clue "hands shook" is on the same side as the blank. *Apprehensive* means nervous.
```

# Negatives
*Not*, *far from*, *by no means*: pick the opposite.

## Negatives flip it

*Not*, *far from*, *by no means*: decide what is true, then pick its opposite.

```example
The lake is far from ______: swimmers can see the bottom at twenty feet.
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
Choices: murky / pristine / frigid
1. Clue: "see the bottom at twenty feet" :: True: the water is clear.
2. *Far from* flips it :: The blank means not clear.
3. Answer: murky :: Clear is true. *Pristine* (clean, pure) is wrong.
```

```more
Check your answer by putting it back into the sentence. "Far from murky" means clear, and the clue says clear. A negative phrase (*not*, *far from*, *by no means*, *hardly*) turns a word into its opposite, so first decide what is true.
```

## Far from what?

```quiz
Critics called her films careless, but her notebooks show she planned every shot for weeks. Her method was far from ______.
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
- meticulous :: That is what is true. *Far from* needs the opposite.
* haphazard
- costly :: Nothing in the text is about money.
> True: she planned carefully. *Far from* flips it, so the blank means unplanned: *haphazard*.
```

# Match the strength
Mild clue, mild word. Strong clue, strong word.

## How strong is it?

Similar words differ in strength. Mild clues (*slightly*, *some*, *may*, *in part*) need a mild word.

```visual
words-in-context--strength
```

```example
The drug ______ headaches: in a trial, patients reported slightly less pain, though none were cured.
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
Choices: eliminates / eases / worsens
1. Clue: "slightly less pain" :: A small effect.
2. eliminates = removes fully :: Too strong: none were cured.
3. Answer: eases :: Mild, like the clue.
```

```more
Look at the whole word family. *Worsens*, *eases* and *eliminates* all describe pain, but each has a different size and direction. A mild word like *slightly* or *some* in the text points to a mild word in the blank.
```

## Pick the right strength

```quiz
One small study ______ the theory: its results point the same way, but larger tests are still needed.
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
- conclusively settles :: Too strong. More tests are still needed.
* lends some support to
- flatly contradicts :: Reversed. The results point the same way.
> The results agree with the theory but do not settle it. *Lends some support to* has the right strength.
```

# Unknown words
Cross out what you know. Use the + or − charge.

## Use the charge

The clue has a **charge**: positive (+), negative (−) or neutral. Pick the choice with the same charge.

```visual
words-in-context--charge
```

```example
The villagers praised the doctor as ______ for treating the poor without payment.
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
Choices: benevolent / malevolent / sedentary
1. Clue: "praised", "without payment" :: Positive (+).
2. *malevolent*: *mal-* means bad (−) :: Wrong charge. Cross out.
3. *sedentary* (sitting a lot): neutral :: Praise needs a positive word.
4. Answer: benevolent (*bene-* = good) :: Positive, and kind.
```

```more
**Charge** is a word's feeling: good (+), bad (−) or neutral. Word parts can hint at it: *bene-* means good, *mal-* means bad, *un-* and *in-* often mean not. Even if you do not know a whole word, its charge can still help you cross it out.
```

## Cross out what you know

Know that some choices are wrong? Cross them out. The word left over is probably right, even if you do not know it.

```quiz
The study's results were ______, leaving researchers unable to decide: some data pointed one way, and some pointed the other.
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
- conclusive :: Known word: it means settled. The text says the opposite.
- lucrative :: Known word: it means profitable. Money is not the topic.
* equivocal
- tangible :: Known word: it means touchable. It does not fit.
> Cross out the three words you know. *Equivocal* (open to more than one reading) is what is left.
```

# Trap: the meaning you know
The meaning you know is often the trap.

## Treat it as a blank

"Most nearly means": cover the word and predict, as with a blank.

```example
The new law will check the spread of the weed: stores may no longer sell its seeds.
As used in the text, what does the word "check" most nearly mean?
Choices: inspect / limit / mark
1. Blank it: "will ______ the spread" :: Forget the meaning you know.
2. Clue: "no longer sell its seeds" :: The law slows the spread.
3. Answer: limit :: *Inspect* is the everyday meaning: the trap.
```

```more
Many words have several meanings. Cover the word, read the sentence as if it had a blank, and predict your own word from the clue. Then find the choice that means the same. In stories and poems, a word may also be used in a figurative way, not its literal way.
```

## Spot the trap

```quiz
The board refused even to entertain the proposal: members voted it down without a minute of discussion.
As used in the text, what does the word "entertain" most nearly mean?
- amuse :: Trap: the everyday meaning. A board cannot amuse a proposal.
* consider
- host :: You host guests, not proposals.
> No discussion at all, so they refused to think about it: *consider*.
```

## Figurative words

```quiz
At the town's oldest college, Professor Wren spoke without gestures or jokes, and he never raised his voice. His lectures were thought dry, yet students returned eagerly, for each hour gave them some insight worth keeping.
As used in the text, what does the word "dry" most nearly mean?
- lacking water :: Trap: the literal meaning. Lectures are not wet or dry.
* dull
- thirsty :: Nothing in the text is about drinking.
> No gestures, no jokes: the lectures seemed dull. *Dry* is used figuratively, not literally.
```
