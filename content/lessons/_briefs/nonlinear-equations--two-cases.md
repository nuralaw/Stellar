# nonlinear-equations--two-cases

**Shows:** undoing a square or an absolute value gives two solutions that sit the same distance either side of the middle number; if the right side is negative there is none.

**Storyboard:**
1. A number line with the centre 7 marked. The equation \(\vert x - 7 \vert = 6\) appears: "distance from 7 is 6".
2. Two arrows of length 6 leave 7, one to the left and one to the right. Dots land at 1 and 13 with labels \(x = 1\), \(x = 13\).
3. The equation splits into two lines: \(x - 7 = 6\) and \(x - 7 = -6\), each with its dot lighting up.
4. The same picture for a square: \((x - 7)^2 = 36\), the root step \(x - 7 = \pm 6\), same two dots.
5. Last frame: right side \(-6\): \(\vert x - 7 \vert = -6\). The arrows can't be drawn: a red "no solution: a distance is never negative".
6. A small note: the sum of the two solutions is \(2 \cdot 7 = 14\).

**Labels & formulas:** \(\vert x - 7 \vert = 6\), \((x - 7)^2 = 36\), \(x - 7 = \pm 6\), \(x = 1\), \(x = 13\), "centre 7", "sum = 14".

**Stress:** the centre point is accent coloured; the two equal arrows are the same colour, so the symmetry reads at once. Keep the number line wide, labels at least 12 px.
