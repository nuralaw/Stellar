## Evidence must match the claim

Find the **claim**. Ask: **if it's true, what should we see?** Pick the choice that shows exactly that.

```example
Hypothesis: traffic noise makes city birds sing higher. Which finding supports it?
(A) City birds lay more eggs.
(B) When noise was played in a quiet forest, birds there sang higher.
1. Claim: noise → higher pitch :: Shrink the claim to its link.
2. Prediction: add noise, pitch goes up :: If true, what should we see?
3. (A) is about eggs, not pitch :: On topic, wrong link. Cut.
4. Answer: (B) :: Noise added, pitch rose. It matches.
```

## Predict, then match

```quiz
Biologist Rafa Lim claims that gobies, a small fish, recognize themselves in a mirror. He painted a dot on each goby's throat, a spot a goby can see only in a mirror.
Which finding would most directly support Lim's claim?
- Lim's mirrors were about the size of a goby. :: That's the method, not a result.
* Gobies ignored the dot until they got a mirror, then rubbed their throats on rocks.
- Many fish species have natural throat spots. :: Other species. Nothing about gobies and mirrors.
> If a goby knows the reflection is itself, the mirror should make it notice its own dot.
```

## Weaken: flip the prediction

Ask what the claim says you should **not** see. That finding weakens it.

```quiz
Millbrook tripled in size from 1850 to 1870. Historian Clara Osei argues that the main cause was a bridge built in 1851, which let farmers reach the town's market.
Which finding would most directly weaken Osei's argument?
- Millbrook's market sold mostly grain and wool. :: What was sold doesn't say why the town grew.
- The bridge's stone came from a nearby quarry. :: The stone's source says nothing about growth.
* Millbrook grew just as fast in the twenty years before the bridge.
> Same growth with no bridge, so the bridge isn't needed to explain it.
```

## Quotes: match every part

Put each quotation in plain words. It must show **every part** of the claim.

```quiz
"Night Train to the City" is a poem by Isabel Varga. The speaker feels both wonder at the city and loneliness there: ______
Which quotation best illustrates the claim?
- "A thousand windows burn like captured stars, / And every street is humming with a song." :: Wonder only. That's half the claim.
* "How bright the towers rise, how proud, how tall! / And yet among their lights I know no one."
- "I walk among the crowds as if through rain, / And not one face turns toward me as I pass." :: Loneliness only. That's half the claim.
> Bright, proud towers: wonder. "I know no one": loneliness. Both parts in one quote.
```

## Trap: the opposite job

Reread the question's verb: **support** or **weaken**? A strong supporter is wrong when the job is weaken.

```quiz
Lena Brandt concluded that a reading app makes children read faster: children who used it for a month read faster afterward.
Which finding would most directly weaken her conclusion?
- Children who used the app for two months improved even more. :: Trap: that supports her. The job is weaken.
- Most children enjoyed the app. :: Enjoying it isn't reading faster.
* Similar children without the app got just as much faster that month.
> If children improve equally without the app, something else explains the gain.
```

## Recap

- Find the claim. Predict what you'd see.
- On topic isn't enough: match the claim's link, every part.
- Reread the job: support or weaken.
