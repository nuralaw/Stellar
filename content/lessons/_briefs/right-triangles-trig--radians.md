# right-triangles-trig--radians

**Shows:** a radian is an angle that cuts an arc as long as the radius; a half turn is \(\pi\) radians, so \(\pi\) radians is \(180^\circ\), and the benchmark angles line up as fractions of \(\pi\).

**Storyboard:**
1. A circle with radius \(r\) drawn from the center. A piece of "string" the length of the radius lies straight, then wraps onto the edge of the circle, starting at the right-hand point. The angle at the center opens to 1 radian (about \(57^\circ\)) and is shaded.
2. The same string wraps again and again: 2, 3 radians. After 3 strings, a short leftover (0.14 of a string) finishes the half circle. The text "a half turn is \(\pi \approx 3.14\) radians" appears; a full circle is \(2\pi\).
3. The circle is cut into marked angles: \(30^\circ\), \(45^\circ\), \(60^\circ\), \(90^\circ\), \(180^\circ\), each with its radian label \(\frac{\pi}{6}\), \(\frac{\pi}{4}\), \(\frac{\pi}{3}\), \(\frac{\pi}{2}\), \(\pi\). The labels appear one at a time as a pointer sweeps counterclockwise.
4. Two conversion arrows between a "degrees" box and a "radians" box: degrees to radians labelled \(\times \frac{\pi}{180}\), radians to degrees labelled \(\times \frac{180}{\pi}\). A worked chip: \(150^\circ \to \frac{5\pi}{6}\).
5. A warning pair: "\(\frac{33}{5}\) radians" and "\(\frac{33\pi}{5}\) radians" are drawn as two different pointer positions on the circle, with the text "a \(\pi\) in the number means radians; read the unit".

**Labels & formulas:** \(\pi \text{ radians} = 180^\circ\), \(\times \frac{\pi}{180}\), \(\times \frac{180}{\pi}\), \(\frac{\pi}{6}\), \(\frac{\pi}{4}\), \(\frac{\pi}{3}\), \(\frac{\pi}{2}\), \(\pi\), \(2\pi\).

**Stress:** the string wrapping onto the circle is the first thing to see; the conversion factors and benchmark labels use the accent colour. Labels at least 14 px on a phone; if space is short, show only the right half circle for the benchmark frame.
