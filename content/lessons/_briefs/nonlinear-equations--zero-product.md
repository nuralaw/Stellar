# nonlinear-equations--zero-product

**Shows:** a product can only be 0 if one of its factors is 0, so a quadratic that equals 0 splits into two easy equations; and the other side must be 0 first.

**Storyboard:**
1. Equation \(2x^2 + x = 15\). The 15 slides across the equals sign and becomes \(-15\): \(2x^2 + x - 15 = 0\). The "0" on the right glows.
2. The left side breaks into two brackets: \((2x - 5)(x + 3) = 0\). Two boxes, one per bracket.
3. A small "A times B = 0" card: a 0 appears in either box; each bracket in turn turns into "= 0". The solutions fall out: \(x = \dfrac52\) and \(x = -3\).
4. Both values appear as two dots on a number line (or on the parabola's \(x\)-axis crossings).
5. The trap flashes in red: \((x - 1)(x - 2) = 6\) with "\(x - 1 = 6\)?" crossed out, and the note "the other side must be 0".

**Labels & formulas:** \(2x^2 + x = 15\), \((2x - 5)(x + 3) = 0\), \(x = \dfrac52\), \(x = -3\), "A times B = 0, so A = 0 or B = 0", "other side must be 0".

**Stress:** the zero on the right side is the hero (accent, a soft pulse). Each bracket gets its own colour and its solution appears in the same colour. The red trap frame is brief. Formulas stay at least 14 px for phones.
