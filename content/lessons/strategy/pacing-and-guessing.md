## Stuck? Guess, flag, move

A wrong answer costs nothing. A stuck question costs time. **Budget spent and no plan? Guess, flag, move on.**

```example
Math module: 22 questions, 35 minutes. You've spent 95 seconds on question 9 and still have no plan.
1. 35 min ÷ 22 ≈ 95 s per question :: Your average budget.
2. Budget spent, no plan → stop :: More time here steals from other questions.
3. Cross out impossible choices, pick one :: A wrong answer costs nothing.
4. Flag it, go to question 10 :: Come back if time is left.
```

## Reading and Writing: 71 seconds

R&W gives 27 questions in 32 minutes: about 71 s each.

```quiz
R&W, question 5. After 2 minutes you're down to two choices and still unsure. Best move?
* Pick one of the two, flag it, move on
- Reread the passage until you're sure :: You're already far past the 71 s budget.
- Flag it and leave it unanswered for now :: A blank scores zero if you never get back.
> A 1-in-2 guess now. The flag lets you return if time is left.
```

## Cross out before guessing

Each choice you cross out raises your odds.

```quiz
No time to solve a probability question. The choices are −0.2, 0.35, 0.6 and 1.4. You cross out the impossible ones and guess. Your chance of being right?
* 1 in 2
- 1 in 4 :: That's a blind guess. Two choices are impossible.
- 1 in 3 :: Both −0.2 and 1.4 are impossible, not just one.
> A probability is between 0 and 1. Two choices are left: 1 in 2.
```

## Math: save time early

Math questions get harder through the module. Go under budget early so the last ones get more.

```quiz
Math module, 22 questions. 17 minutes are left and you're starting question 9. Where do you stand?
* Behind: 14 harder questions, about 73 s each
- On pace: about half the time is left :: Half the time, but 14 of 22 questions remain.
- Ahead: the hardest questions are already done :: Math runs from easier to harder.
> 17 min ÷ 14 ≈ 73 s, under the 95 s average, with the hardest still ahead.
```

## Out of time? Fill everything

Any answer beats none.

```quiz
30 seconds left. Three multiple-choice questions have no answer. Best move?
* Click an answer on all three
- Solve the first one carefully :: That leaves two blanks: two sure zeros.
- Leave them blank; wrong answers lower your score :: There is no penalty for a wrong answer.
> Three guesses can still score. Three blanks can't.
```

## Trap: flagging a blank

A flag is a reminder, not an answer. You may never get back.

```quiz
Question 14 looks long. You want to skip it and return later. What do you do before clicking Next?
* Enter a guess, then flag it
- Flag it and leave it unanswered :: Trap: if time runs out, the blank scores zero.
- Write "14" on your scratch paper :: Still a blank. Enter a guess first.
> A guess costs seconds and can score. Change it if you get back.
```

## Recap

- Budget: about 71 s per question in R&W, 95 s in Math.
- Stuck? Cross out, guess, flag, move on.
- Never leave a blank.
