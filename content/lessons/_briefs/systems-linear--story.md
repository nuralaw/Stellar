# systems-linear--story

**Shows:** a word problem turns into two equations: one for "how many", one for "how much money".

**Storyboard:**
1. A short story card: "90 tickets, $715 total. Student $6, adult $11." Two numbers are tagged: "90 tickets" (blue chip), "$715" (amber chip).
2. Two labelled boxes appear: `\(s\)` = students and `\(a\)` = adults.
3. The blue chip drops down and builds the count equation `\(s + a = 90\)`.
4. The amber chip drops down and builds the money equation `\(6s + 11a = 715\)`; the prices 6 and 11 fly in from the story and attach to `s` and `a`.
5. Both equations are stacked with a bracket "system"; a tick shows each fact used once.

**Labels & formulas:** `\(s + a = 90\)`, `\(6s + 11a = 715\)`, tags "how many", "how much".

**Stress:** colour-code the two facts (count vs money) and keep the same colours in the equations. The price numbers 6 and 11 are the moving parts. Short words only; the story card is the only sentence.
