# command-of-evidence-textual--old-words

**Shows:** translate an old or figurative line into plain words first, then match the feeling the claim names.

**Storyboard:**
1. A quotation in an old-style font appears: "Fain would I flee this din". Words *fain* and *din* are underlined.
2. Each underlined word lifts out and swaps to its plain meaning on a small chip: fain = gladly, din = loud noise.
3. The whole line re-forms in plain words below: "I would gladly flee this noise".
4. The claim card "the speaker longs to leave the noisy city" slides next to it; a green link line joins them.
5. A second quotation ("The city's gilded halls do shine so bright") is translated the same way and gets no link line (grey, "no wish to leave").

**Labels & formulas:** "old words", "plain words", "claim", the translations above. No formulas.

**Stress:** the swap from old word to plain word (flip/morph) and the green link line. Keep the old-style font readable (no thin script); on a phone stack the original line above the plain line.
