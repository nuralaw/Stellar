# systems-linear--three-cases

**Shows:** two lines can cross once, never, or be the same line, and the slopes (and intercepts) decide which.

**Storyboard:**
1. Three small panels side by side, each a grid with two lines drawn one after another.
2. Panel 1: two lines with different slopes cross at one dot. Label: "different slopes", result "1 solution" (green).
3. Panel 2: two parallel lines (same slope, different intercepts). Label: "same slope, different intercept", result "0 solutions" (red).
4. Panel 3: the second line is drawn exactly over the first. Label: "same line", result "infinitely many" with a small infinity marker.
5. Under each panel a tiny slope readout: `\(m = 2\)` and `\(m = -1\)`; `\(m = 2\)` and `\(m = 2\)`; `\(m = 2\)` and `\(m = 2\)` with equal intercepts.

**Labels & formulas:** `\(y = 2x + 1\)` with `\(y = -x + 4\)`; `\(y = 2x + 1\)` with `\(y = 2x - 3\)`; `\(y = 2x + 1\)` with `\(2y = 4x + 2\)`. Short result tags "1", "0", "infinitely many".

**Stress:** the slope numbers are what the eye should compare; highlight them in primary. Never show "2 solutions".
