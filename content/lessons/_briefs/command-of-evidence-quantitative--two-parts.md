# command-of-evidence-quantitative--two-parts

**Shows:** a claim with two parts needs a choice that proves both parts, for both groups; a choice that gives only one part or only one group proves nothing.

**Storyboard:**
1. The claim "Aero goes farther and weighs less than the Trek" splits into two lock pieces: [farther: range up] and [lighter: weight down]. Two keys (empty) hover beside them.
2. Three choice cards. Card A ("Aero ran 60 km") fits one key only; the second lock stays red: tag "half the claim".
3. Card B ("Aero ran 60 km and weighs 11 kg") fits both keys but has no Trek data: both locks flash amber, tag "nothing to compare".
4. Card C (both bikes, both numbers) fits both locks; they turn green and open.
5. The mini table (Aero 60/11, Trek 45/14) appears with the arrows "range up" and "weight down" drawn on it.

**Labels & formulas:** "part 1", "part 2", "half the claim", "nothing to compare", "range up, weight down". No formulas.

**Stress:** two locks opening at the same time is the key moment; arrows for direction (up for range, down for weight) must be clearly different colours. Stack the cards on a phone.
