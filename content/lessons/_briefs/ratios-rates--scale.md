# ratios-rates--scale

**Shows:** a map scale is a ratio of lengths (2 cm : 5 km), so a longer map length multiplies the real length by the same factor.

**Storyboard:**
1. A map strip with two towns, A and B, 7 cm apart (bracket "7 cm"). At the left, a small scale bar "2 cm = 5 km".
2. The scale bar is copied along the strip: three full copies of 2 cm plus a half copy (3.5 copies). Each copy shows "5 km" above it.
3. The copies line up under a real-life bar: 5 + 5 + 5 + 2.5 km. The equal-ratio equation appears: \(\frac{2\ \text{cm}}{5\ \text{km}} = \frac{7\ \text{cm}}{x\ \text{km}}\).
4. The cross-multiplying arrows light up: \(2x = 35\), then \(x = 17.5\). The real bar fills with "17.5 km".
5. A small side panel: a 3 by 2 cm rectangle becomes 7.5 by 5 km. The caption shows "area: use the real lengths" with 37.5 km squared (written \(37.5\ \text{km}^2\)), not 6 times 5.

**Labels & formulas:** "2 cm = 5 km", \(\frac{2}{5} = \frac{7}{x}\), \(2x = 35\), \(x = 17.5\ \text{km}\).

**Stress:** the same-unit-in-the-same-place alignment of the two fractions (cm over cm, km over km) in two colours; the repeated scale copies are the hero. The side panel is quiet and optional on a phone.
