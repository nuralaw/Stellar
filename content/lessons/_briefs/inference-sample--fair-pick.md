# inference-sample--fair-pick

**Shows:** a random sample gives every member the same chance and mirrors the population; a biased pick over-represents one kind of member.

**Storyboard:**
1. A population of 40 dots in two colours (24 teal, 16 orange), labelled "population".
2. Names drop into a hat and 8 dots are drawn. The sample row shows 5 teal and 3 orange, close to the population mix. Caption: "same chance for everyone".
3. A scale reads "teal 60% vs 62%" (population vs sample). Both bars nearly match.
4. New scene: the same population, but a funnel only catches the orange dots near the door ("easy to reach"). The sample row is 7 orange and 1 teal.
5. The bars no longer match. A red tag: "biased". A small badge shows a bigger sample (20 dots) with the same skew: "bigger does not fix it".

**Labels & formulas:** "population", "random sample", "biased sample", "same chance for every member".

**Stress:** the match of the two bars in the random case, and the mismatch in a warm colour in the biased case. Dots at least 14 px on a phone.
