# Claim first
True in the data, and it proves the claim.

## True and on target

The right choice is **true in the data** and **proves the claim**.

```example
Cups a café sold in one day: coffee 65, tea 40, juice 20. The owner says coffee was by far her most popular drink: ______ Which choice uses the data to complete the statement?
1. Claim: coffee far ahead of the others :: Read the claim before the numbers.
2. "Tea sold 40, twice as many as juice" :: True, but not about coffee. Cut.
3. "Coffee sold 65, far more than tea (40) or juice (20)" :: True and on target.
```

## Cut what misses

```quiz
How Lake County students got to school. 2010: walked 22%, biked 30%. 2020: walked 35%, biked 25%, bus 40%.
Claim: in 2020, walking was more common than biking. Which choice supports it?
* In 2020, 35% walked and 25% biked.
- In 2010, 22% walked and 30% biked. :: Wrong year. The claim is about 2020.
- In 2020, 40% rode the bus. :: True, but the bus isn't in the claim.
- From 2010 to 2020, walking rose from 22% to 35%. :: Walking alone can't show it beat biking.
> The claim compares walking with biking in 2020. Only one choice gives both, for that year.
```

# Right row, right unit
Real numbers from the wrong row are still wrong.

## Find the row and unit

Wrong choices use real numbers from the wrong row, or drop the unit.

```example
Books borrowed (hundreds). March: fiction 12, comics 7. April: fiction 15, comics 9. In April, more fiction than comics was borrowed: ______
1. Claim: April, fiction against comics :: Note the month.
2. April row: 15 and 9 :: Not March's 12 and 7.
3. "1,500 fiction books and 900 comics" :: Hundreds: 15 means 1,500.
```

## Read it yourself

```quiz
Park visitors (thousands). 2021: Elm 61, Oak 48. 2023: Elm 70, Oak 52.
In 2021, Elm Park drew more visitors than Oak Park: ______
- Elm had 70,000 visitors and Oak had 52,000. :: Those are the 2023 numbers. Wrong row.
- Elm had 61 visitors and Oak had 48. :: The data are in thousands: 61 means 61,000.
* Elm had 61,000 visitors and Oak had 48,000.
> Find the 2021 row, then read the unit: 61 and 48 thousand.
```

# Two-part claims
A claim with "and" needs both parts proved.

## Prove both parts

A claim with *and* has two parts. The choice must prove both.

```example
Phones tested. Model A: 14 hours of battery, 180 g. Model B: 9 hours, 210 g. A reviewer says Model A lasts longer and weighs less than Model B: ______
1. "Model A ran 14 hours; Model B ran 9" :: Battery only. Half the claim.
2. "Model A ran 14 hours and weighs 180 g" :: Model B is missing.
3. "A: 14 hours, 180 g. B: 9 hours, 210 g" :: Longer and lighter. Both proved.
```

## Both or nothing

```quiz
Snails on white rocks: 31°C, 4% of body water lost. Snails on black rocks: 41°C, 11% lost.
Hypothesis: snails on lighter rocks stay cooler and lose less water than snails on darker rocks. Which choice supports it?
* White-rock snails were cooler and lost less water than black-rock snails.
- Black-rock snails were hotter than white-rock snails, 41°C versus 31°C. :: True, but temperature only. Half the hypothesis.
- White-rock snails were 31°C, and black-rock snails lost 11% of their water. :: Two different measures. Nothing is compared.
> Both parts hold: cooler (31°C < 41°C) and less water lost (4% < 11%).
```

# Weaken a claim
Flip the job: true data against the claim.

## Flip the job

To weaken a claim, pick true data that goes against it.

```example
Average test scores. Class with background music: 78. Class without: 81. A teacher claims music raises scores. Which choice weakens the claim?
1. Claim predicts: music class scores higher :: Say what the claim expects.
2. Data: 78 with music, 81 without :: The opposite happened.
3. "The music class averaged 78, below the other's 81" :: True and against the claim.
```

## Weaken it yourself

```quiz
Share of workers who bike to work. Alton: 9%. Brisk: 15%. Dunmore: 12%.
An official claims Dunmore has the highest rate of the three cities. Which choice weakens the claim?
* Brisk's rate (15%) is higher than Dunmore's (12%).
- Dunmore's rate (12%) is higher than Alton's (9%). :: True, but that supports the claim. Opposite job.
- Brisk's rate (15%) is higher than Alton's (9%). :: True, but Dunmore isn't in it.
> "Highest" fails if any city beats Dunmore. Brisk does, 15% to 12%.
```

# Trap: true but useless
Every choice is true. Only one proves the claim.

## True isn't enough

On hard questions every choice is true. Ask: does it prove this exact claim?

```example
Plant height after a month. Fertilizer X: 30 cm. Fertilizer Y: 22 cm. No fertilizer: 20 cm. Claim: X works better than Y: ______
1. "Y plants reached 22 cm; unfertilized plants, 20 cm" :: True, but X is missing.
2. "X plants reached 30 cm" :: True, but nothing to compare.
3. "X plants reached 30 cm; Y plants, 22 cm" :: True, and compares X with Y.
```

## Spot the useful one

```quiz
Tomato plots. A: 8 hours of sun, watered daily, 95 cm. B: 8 hours, weekly, 92 cm. C: 4 hours, daily, 60 cm. D: 4 hours, weekly, 58 cm.
A gardener concludes that sunlight, not watering, explains the height differences. Which choice supports this?
- Plot A, with 8 hours of sun, reached 95 cm. :: True, but one plot shows no comparison.
* With 8 hours of sun, plants reached 92–95 cm on either watering; with 4 hours, 58–60 cm.
- Plot A grew taller than Plot B, 95 cm versus 92 cm. :: Trap: true, but A and B differ only in watering.
- Plot D was the shortest, at 58 cm. :: True, but one plot can't explain the differences.
> Sun changes height by about 35 cm; watering changes it by 2–3 cm. Only that comparison proves the claim.
```
