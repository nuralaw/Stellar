# statistical-claims--confounder

**Shows:** a third factor linked to both the group and the outcome can produce a difference that looks like a cause, and random assignment balances it.

**Storyboard:**
1. Two boxes: "takes vitamins" and "fewer colds", joined by an arrow with a question mark.
2. A third box appears above: "exercises more". Arrows go from it to both boxes. Caption: "linked to both".
3. The first arrow dims: "the difference may come from exercise, not vitamins".
4. New scene: people dots with a coin flip splitting them into two groups. Each group gets the same mix of "exercises" (filled) and "does not" (empty) dots. Caption: "random assignment: groups start alike".
5. The confounder box fades out, and the vitamin to colds arrow turns solid.

**Labels & formulas:** "vitamins", "fewer colds", "exercise (confounder)", "random assignment".

**Stress:** the two arrows from the confounder in the warm colour; the balanced mix of dots in the calm colour. Readable at phone width.
