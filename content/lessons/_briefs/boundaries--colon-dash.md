# boundaries--colon-dash

**Shows:** a colon or one dash needs a full sentence on its left and a list, phrase or explaining sentence on its right; a fragment on the left breaks it.

**Storyboard:**
1. A solid green block "full sentence" (text: "She packed two things") sits on the left; an empty slot follows.
2. A big colon ":" drops into the slot and a signpost arrow points right, labelled "here comes the detail".
3. Three cards fly in on the right, one at a time, each with a tick: list ("a map and a compass"), phrase ("one answer: yes"), explaining sentence ("it cost too much").
4. The colon morphs into a single dash and the three cards stay, with the note "one dash = colon".
5. Wrong case: the left block turns into a broken grey fragment ("She packed such as") and the colon bounces back with a red cross: "no colon after an unfinished phrase".

**Labels & formulas:** "full sentence", ":", a long dash, "list", "phrase", "explains it", "fragment before a colon = wrong". No math formulas.

**Stress:** green full-sentence block versus the grey broken block; the colon/dash glyph is large and animated. On a phone, stack the three right-hand cards vertically and keep each under six words.
